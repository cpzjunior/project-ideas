# Electoral Fraud Prevention Act

## 1. Concept

Create a law to establish accountability mechanisms for elected candidates who fail to fulfill the key commitments formally made during their election campaigns.

Every candidate must define and officially register the main commitments they intend to fulfill if elected. These commitments must be specific, objective, and auditable, allowing their fulfillment to be assessed at a later date.

The proposal establishes the following mechanisms:

- Official commitments: each candidate will be responsible for selecting and registering their own priority campaign commitments.
- Audit criteria: commitments must have objective and verifiable criteria that allow their fulfillment to be assessed.
- Oversight: the Superior Electoral Court (TSE) will be responsible for assessing whether registered commitments have been fulfilled.
- Justifications: the officeholder may provide justifications for unfulfilled commitments, including external circumstances, legal impediments, budgetary constraints, or unforeseen events.
- Penalties: unjustified failure to fulfill registered commitments may result in a financial fine and disqualification from running for office in the subsequent election.
- Right to defense: all proceedings must respect the constitutional guarantees of the right to be heard and the right to a full and effective defense.

## 2. Rationale

Election campaigns frequently feature promises that influence voters' decisions but may never be fulfilled. Currently, there is no general mechanism specifically designed to hold officeholders accountable for failing to fulfill their key electoral commitments.

The proposal seeks to establish a clearer relationship between campaign promises and the responsibilities assumed by elected candidates, encouraging greater transparency, accountability, and seriousness in election campaigns.

Another important objective is to provide greater predictability for voters and the market. The formal registration of key commitments allows the public, companies, and investors to know in advance the priorities and guidelines that a candidate intends to pursue if elected. This contributes to better-informed decisions and reduces uncertainty about the direction of public administration.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Electoral accountability: establish consequences for unjustified failure to fulfill formally registered commitments.
- Transparency: make candidates' key commitments public and verifiable.
- More responsible campaigns: encourage realistic, objective, and measurable proposals.
- Predictability: provide greater clarity regarding the priorities of the future administration, benefiting voters, companies, and investors.
- Voter awareness: provide concrete information so that the public can assess the performance of elected officials.
- Public trust: strengthen the accountability relationship between elected representatives and the public.

## 4. Potential Impacts

- Greater accountability in the development of electoral platforms and proposals.
- Increased transparency regarding the fulfillment of campaign commitments.
- Greater political and economic predictability, allowing citizens, companies, and investors to consider registered commitments in their decision-making processes.
- Strengthening of the accountability culture in public administration.
- Potential reduction in unrealistic or misleading electoral promises.
- Expansion of the Electoral Justice System's oversight responsibilities.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

- Legal basis: define the legal grounds for imposing penalties on elected candidates for failing to fulfill registered commitments.
- Commitment assessment: establish objective criteria for evaluating different types of promises, including those related to public policies and long-term initiatives.
- External circumstances: distinguish unjustified non-fulfillment from situations beyond the officeholder's control.
- Institutional oversight: define the responsibilities, procedures, and resources required for the TSE to conduct assessments.
- Constitutional compatibility: ensure that penalties and ineligibility rules comply with the Constitution and electoral legislation.
- Due process of law: ensure the right to defense and the possibility of challenging decisions.

## 6. Open Questions

- How many commitments must each candidate register?
- Should commitments be assessed only at the end of the term or periodically?
- How should the partial fulfillment of a commitment be assessed?
- How should shared responsibilities among members of the Executive and Legislative branches be evaluated?
- How should the amount of fines be calculated?
- What procedures should be adopted for assessing non-compliance and imposing penalties?
