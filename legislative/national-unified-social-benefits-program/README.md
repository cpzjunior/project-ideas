# National Unified Social Benefits Program

## 1. Idea

Create a national program that unifies existing social benefits, replacing the multiplicity of programs and registrations with a single structure for granting, paying, monitoring, and overseeing benefits.

The proposal aims to simplify the relationship between citizens and the State, improve the territorial distribution of social resources, and enable the government to monitor social assistance policies in an integrated manner.

Although benefits will be presented to citizens in a unified manner, each component will remain separately identified for purposes of transparency, budgetary control, auditing, and performance evaluation.

The program will be based on seven main rules:

### 1. Unification of social benefits

All social benefits covered by the program will be consolidated into a single administrative structure.

For citizens, there will be a single social benefit, with centralized registration, application, monitoring, and payment.

However, the financial components will remain individually identified, making it possible to determine the origin and purpose of each portion.

The structure must allow:

- Consultation of the total amount received.
- Breakdown of each benefit component.
- Identification of the purpose of each benefit category.
- Consultation of eligibility criteria.
- Payment history.
- Identification of the agencies responsible for funding.
- Consultation of changes made to the benefit composition.

In this way, unification will simplify the citizen experience without eliminating transparency regarding the use of public resources.

### 2. Territorial eligibility

The granting of benefits will also be conditional on the citizen's place of residence.

The territory will be used as an eligibility criterion, allowing resources to be directed toward regions whose socioeconomic needs are consistent with each type of assistance.

The reference unit may be the IBGE census tract, allowing the use of smaller and more specific geographic divisions than traditional neighborhoods.

Territorial divisions used by municipal administrations may be considered when defining eligibility. Changes to these divisions may modify which localities meet the program's territorial criteria.

The socioeconomic classification must consider indicators such as:

- Average household income per capita.
- Municipal Human Development Index (IDHM), when available at an appropriate geographic scale.
- Social Vulnerability Index (IVS).
- Housing conditions.
- Access to basic sanitation.
- Educational attainment.
- Infrastructure conditions.
- Other indicators relevant to each type of assistance.

Each benefit component may have its own territorial eligibility criteria.

For example, the Gas Assistance benefit may be made available only in regions where a need for this type of assistance has been identified, while other benefit categories may serve a different set of territories.

Territorial classification will be complemented by an assessment of each family's socioeconomic situation, preventing high-income families from receiving benefits solely because they reside in eligible regions.

Territorial eligibility will not operate through a fixed number of available slots. All families that meet the applicable criteria will be able to apply for the corresponding components.

### 3. Household income limit

Economic eligibility will be conditional on household income.

The proposed general limit will be two minimum wages in total household income, considering all members of the household.

Families exceeding this limit will lose eligibility for the components subject to this rule.

The objective is to establish a simple, objective, and easy-to-understand criterion, avoiding excessively complex rules for determining continued eligibility.

Income assessment must follow uniform criteria, with verification mechanisms and integration with government databases.

Financial incentives granted by the program itself in recognition of development activities must not be counted as household income for eligibility purposes.

### 4. Voluntary participation and monthly flexibility

Families will be able to choose which components they wish to receive, even when they meet the eligibility criteria.

This decision may be changed monthly, allowing families to accept or decline any component without having to complete an entirely new application.

The system must allow families to:

- Individually accept each available component.
- Decline components they do not wish to receive.
- Change their decision for each monthly benefit period.
- Consult components previously declined.
- Request components again after having declined them in previous periods.

Declining one component must not affect the receipt of other components.

It must also not result in penalties or future restrictions.

The objective is to guarantee citizens' autonomy and prevent a previous decision from blocking future access to assistance.

### 5. Continuous eligibility and the possibility of returning

Families may stop receiving benefits when their income exceeds the established limit, but they will not permanently lose their connection to the program.

The proposal establishes that social assistance must allow both exit and return, depending on the family's economic situation.

The system will operate according to the following rules:

- Eligible families may receive the corresponding components.
- When household income exceeds two minimum wages, eligibility will be suspended.
- Registration and benefit receipt histories will be preserved.
- If income returns to the permitted limit, the family may request reactivation.
- Reactivation will take place through a simplified verification of eligibility criteria.
- Families will not need to complete the entire registration process again.

Reactivation will not depend on the existence of available slots or discretionary resource availability for families that meet the program's legal criteria.

An improvement in economic circumstances must not represent a permanent loss of access to social protection.

### 6. Financial incentives for development

The program will also include an additional benefit category called Development Incentives.

Its purpose will be to encourage activities that contribute to education, professional training, employment, and the economic independence of families.

The incentives will be paid as additional amounts, without replacing or reducing regular assistance components.

Activities that may qualify for bonuses include:

- Completion of vocational training courses.
- Completion of primary or secondary education.
- Obtaining professional certifications.
- Entering the formal labor market.
- Remaining employed for specified periods.
- Formalizing entrepreneurial activities.
- Completing training and professional development programs.

Each activity may have a specific financial reward, with eligibility and verification criteria established by the program.

Citizens will have access to a dedicated section to monitor:

- Available goals.
- Activities in progress.
- Completed activities.
- Financial rewards.
- History of incentives received.
- Upcoming development opportunities.

Participation will be voluntary.

Failure to participate or complete activities must not result in the loss of regular social protection benefits.

Incentives must also not cause a loss of eligibility by exceeding the income limit.

### 7. Management, transparency, and oversight

The State will have access to a centralized benefits monitoring structure, allowing each component to be individually monitored and information to be consolidated by territory.

The platform must provide information such as:

- Number of beneficiary families.
- Number of eligible families.
- Amounts granted by benefit category.
- Expenditures by municipality and census tract.
- Territorial distribution of resources.
- History of benefit grants and suspensions.
- Number of families that have returned to the program.
- Amounts paid in development incentives.
- Evolution of socioeconomic indicators.

Individual data must remain protected, while aggregated indicators may be made publicly available.

The structure must also allow the auditing of benefit grants, payments, registration changes, and eligibility decisions.

## 2. Motivation

Brazilian social assistance currently consists of different programs, each with its own rules, registrations, procedures, and monitoring mechanisms.

This fragmentation can make it difficult for citizens to understand their entitlements and increase the complexity of managing public resources.

The proposal seeks to replace this multiplicity of structures with a unified program in which citizens have a single relationship with the social assistance system.

Unification does not mean eliminating the individual identity of benefits. Each category will remain separately identified so that the State can determine exactly how much it is spending, for what purpose, and in which locations.

Another problem the proposal seeks to address is the insufficiently targeted distribution of social resources.

The use of smaller territorial units will make it possible to identify regions with different socioeconomic needs and direct each type of assistance according to local conditions.

For example, one region may demonstrate a need for assistance with the purchase of cooking gas, while another may require greater food or housing assistance.

The use of specific territorial criteria for each component will allow resources to be directed more precisely.

Individual household income assessments will complement territorial classification, preventing families with high economic capacity from receiving benefits intended for vulnerable populations.

Another important aspect is the relationship between social assistance and economic mobility.

Currently, the possibility of losing benefits when income increases may create disincentives to entering the workforce, especially when losing assistance represents a significant reduction in disposable income.

The proposal seeks to address this problem by establishing a general income limit of two minimum wages and allowing families that exceed this limit to return to the program if they experience economic difficulties again.

In addition, the creation of financial incentives for education, professional training, and employment seeks to transform the program into an instrument of socioeconomic development, rather than merely a means of income transfer.

The ultimate objective is to build a social assistance system that is simpler for citizens, more territorially targeted, and more efficient for the State.

## 3. Expected benefits

- Administrative simplification: replacement of multiple programs and registrations with a unified structure.
- Ease of access: ability to consult and apply for benefits through a single system.
- Transparency: preservation of the individual breakdown of financial components.
- Better territorial targeting: allocation of resources according to the socioeconomic needs of each territorial unit.
- Reduction of distortions: joint use of territorial and household criteria to determine eligibility.
- Fiscal efficiency: improved identification of resources allocated to each type of assistance.
- Government oversight: centralized monitoring of grants, payments, and suspensions.
- Citizen autonomy: ability to individually accept or decline components each month.
- Flexibility: ability to return to the program when economic circumstances once again meet the eligibility criteria.
- Educational incentives: financial bonuses for completing educational stages.
- Training incentives: encouragement to pursue vocational courses and obtain certifications.
- Employment incentives: financial rewards for entering and remaining in the labor market.
- Socioeconomic mobility: encouragement of improvements in families' economic circumstances.
- Territorial intelligence: production of detailed information about the social needs of each region.
- Budgetary transparency: ability to identify the share of public spending represented by each benefit category.
- Public policy evaluation: greater ease in comparing results and identifying which benefit categories produce better outcomes.

## 4. Potential impacts

Positive impacts:

- Reduction of administrative fragmentation across social programs.
- Simplification of application and monitoring procedures.
- Better targeting of public resources.
- More precise identification of regions requiring assistance.
- Reduction of improper payments through cross-referencing of information.
- Greater ease of monitoring benefit grants.
- Improved transparency regarding the amounts received by families.
- Reduction of bureaucratic barriers to returning to social assistance.
- Reduction of potential disincentives to entering the workforce.
- Encouragement to complete courses and pursue professional training.
- Incentives for formal employment and entrepreneurship.
- Improved budget planning capacity.
- Production of more detailed socioeconomic indicators.
- Greater ability to evaluate the effectiveness of social programs.
- Possibility of identifying the specific needs of each territory.

Negative impacts or risks:

- Families experiencing vulnerability who reside in territories classified as ineligible may be left without access to benefits.
- Inadequate territorial classifications may result in wrongful exclusions.
- Outdated census data may compromise the accuracy of eligibility determinations.
- Changes of address may cause significant changes in the assistance received.
- The fixed limit of two minimum wages may create discontinuities in families' disposable income.
- Unification may require complex migration of existing databases and systems.
- Integration of government databases may present technical and legal difficulties.
- Bonuses may encourage attempts to commit fraud when verifying course completion and employment.
- Financial incentives may generate additional costs for the public budget.
- The concentration of socioeconomic information in a single platform will increase the importance of information security and data protection mechanisms.
- Replacing existing programs may face legal, institutional, and administrative difficulties.

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

The proposal may be implemented through a reformulation of the national social assistance structure, with the gradual replacement of the covered programs and integration of existing systems.

Implementation must comply with the social rights established in the Federal Constitution, social assistance legislation, and the responsibilities of the different levels of government.

The main challenges are:

- Program unification: identifying existing benefits, their purposes, rules, funding sources, and beneficiary populations, and establishing how they will be incorporated into the new program.

- Legislative transition: establishing the legal instruments necessary to replace existing programs while preserving rights and preventing unjustified interruptions in assistance.

- Territorial classification: developing a methodology to classify census units according to socioeconomic indicators and the specific needs of each type of assistance.

- Municipal territorial divisions: establishing how territorial divisions used by municipalities will be incorporated and how changes to them will be reflected in eligibility.

- Socioeconomic indicators: defining which indicators will be used for each benefit category, considering income, infrastructure, housing conditions, educational attainment, and social vulnerability.

- Data updates: establishing how frequently territorial classifications will be reviewed, considering that socioeconomic conditions may change over time.

- Household eligibility: establishing a uniform methodology for calculating household income and identifying members of each household.

- Income limit: regulating the application of the two-minimum-wage ceiling, including the definition of which income sources are considered and how temporary income fluctuations are treated.

- Financial incentives: establishing amounts, granting conditions, and verification mechanisms for activities that qualify for bonuses.

- Bonus neutrality: ensuring that financial incentives granted by the program are not counted when determining a family's economic eligibility.

- Possibility of returning: developing mechanisms that allow simplified reactivation of benefits, preserving registration history and avoiding new bureaucratic procedures.

- Monthly participation: adapting payment systems to allow families to individually accept or decline components for each benefit period.

- Unified payment: developing a financial structure capable of consolidating amounts into a single payment while maintaining the accounting identification of each component.

- Interoperability: integrating the platform with government databases, including the Unified Registry (Cadastro Único), income records, employment information, educational records, and other necessary sources.

- Information security: establishing access controls, traceability, auditing, and personal data protection in compliance with the LGPD.

- Oversight: developing mechanisms to identify registration inconsistencies, residency fraud, and economic information discrepancies.

- Governance: defining institutional responsibilities for program management, territorial classification, granting, payment, and oversight.

- Fiscal sustainability: estimating the total cost of the program, considering the eligible population, financial components, incentives, and administrative costs.

- Technology migration: establishing a gradual transition from existing systems, avoiding payment interruptions during implementation.

The platform must be developed using a modular architecture, allowing components to be added, modified, or discontinued without requiring the entire program structure to be rebuilt.

Each type of assistance must have its own eligibility, calculation, and funding rules while sharing the central infrastructure for registration, granting, payment, and auditing.

## 6. Open points

- Define which social programs will be incorporated into the new system.
- Establish which benefits will be fully replaced and which may retain specific rules.
- Define the methodology for socioeconomic classification of census units.
- Evaluate the use of the IDHM, IVS, and other territorial indicators.
- Define how frequently territorial classifications will be updated.
- Establish how changes to municipal territorial divisions will be handled.
- Define whether all components will have their own territorial criteria or whether some categories will have nationwide coverage.
- Define the methodology for calculating household income.
- Establish how variable, temporary, and informal income will be treated.
- Define how the two-minimum-wage rule will be applied to families with different numbers of members.
- Establish the procedure for suspending and reactivating benefits.
- Define whether reactivation will be automatic through data cross-referencing or will depend on a citizen's request.
- Establish deadlines for changing the monthly selection of components.
- Define how the amounts of each financial component will be calculated.
- Establish which activities may qualify for development incentives.
- Define the amounts and limits for each bonus category.
- Establish mechanisms for verifying courses, certifications, employment, and entrepreneurial activities.
- Define whether bonuses will have annual limits or may be accumulated.
- Establish rules to prevent the improper granting of bonuses.
- Define the mechanisms for unified payment and individual accounting identification.
- Establish the governance structure and responsibilities of the agencies involved.
- Define the migration strategy for existing programs.
- Develop fiscal simulations to assess the cost and sustainability of the new model.
- Assess the legal implications of using territory as an eligibility requirement, particularly in relation to the universality and protection of social rights.

## 7. References

- Federal Constitution of 1988: particularly Articles 6, 203, and 204, which address social rights and social assistance.
- Law No. 8,742/1993 (LOAS): Organic Law on Social Assistance, which establishes the organization of social assistance in Brazil.
- Law No. 14,601/2023: establishes the Bolsa Família Program.
- Federal Government Unified Registry for Social Programs (Cadastro Único): reference for identifying and profiling the socioeconomic conditions of low-income families.
- Unified Social Assistance System (SUAS): organizational and management framework for Brazilian social assistance.
- Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE): reference for demographic, territorial, and socioeconomic data, including the division of territory into census tracts.
- Municipal Human Development Index (IDHM): human development indicator based on income, education, and life expectancy.
- Social Vulnerability Index (IVS/Ipea): reference for assessing social vulnerability across different dimensions.
- Unified Registry Family Vulnerability Index (IVCAD): reference for assessing family vulnerability.
- Law No. 13,709/2018 (LGPD): establishes rules for the processing and protection of personal data.
