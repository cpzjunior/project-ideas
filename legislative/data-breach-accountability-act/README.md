# Data Breach Accountability Act

## 1. Concept

Establish legislation to strengthen the prevention, investigation, and accountability for leaks of personal data and confidential information, in both the public and private sectors, with particular attention to public administration bodies and entities.

The proposal is based on the principle that data breaches cannot be treated merely as technological incidents or administrative failures. When there is human involvement, significant negligence, deliberate facilitation, or intentional exploitation of vulnerabilities, those responsible must be held individually accountable for their conduct.

The law must establish the following mechanisms:

**1. Mandatory investigation of everyone involved**

Every significant data breach must trigger a technical and administrative investigation to identify:

- Who had access to the compromised information.
    
- Who accessed, copied, transferred, or exported the data.
    
- Who authorized or facilitated access.
    
- Who failed to fulfill security obligations under their responsibility.
    
- Who had prior knowledge of vulnerabilities or risks and failed to take required action.
    
- Whether third parties, suppliers, contracted companies, or criminal organizations were involved.
    

The investigation may not be limited to the institution's managers. It must cover all individuals whose participation, facilitation, or culpable omission is related to the incident.

**2. Accountability of the individuals involved**

Public servants, employees of state-owned enterprises, managers, contractors, service providers, and technology suppliers must be held accountable according to their participation and culpability.

Consequences may include:

- Removal from appointed positions when responsibility is proven.
    
- Dismissal of tenured public servants, following disciplinary administrative proceedings and observing due process of law.
    
- Termination of contracts with involved service providers and suppliers, in accordance with applicable legislation.
    
- Civil liability for damages caused.
    
- Referral to the competent authorities for criminal investigation when there is evidence of a crime.
    

The law must provide for precautionary removal from duties or suspension of access to systems and databases when necessary to preserve evidence, prevent further breaches, or avoid interference with the investigation.

Precautionary removal will not constitute a presumption of guilt.

**3. Criminal and corruption investigations**

Every data breach involving indications of criminal conduct must be referred to law enforcement authorities and the Public Prosecutor's Office for investigation.

When there is evidence of data sales, receipt of improper benefits, bribery, favoritism toward third parties, or use of confidential information for private gain, a specific investigation must be opened to examine possible corruption offenses and other related crimes.

The investigation must cover all identified participants, regardless of their position, hierarchical level, or contractual relationship.

**4. Institutional accountability**

Public bodies and private companies responsible for processing data must demonstrate that they have adopted appropriate technical and administrative measures to protect the information.

Institutional accountability must take into consideration:

- Absence of minimum security controls.
    
- Lack of access monitoring.
    
- Improper sharing of credentials.
    
- Absence of audit logs.
    
- Failure to remediate known vulnerabilities.
    
- Failure to act on previous alerts.
    
- Concealment or unjustifiably delayed reporting of the incident.
    
- Recurrence of security failures.
    

The institution's accountability will not exclude the individual liability of the agents involved when their responsibility is proven.

**5. Independent reporting channel**

Create a permanent channel for reporting data breaches, data sales, unauthorized access, concealment of incidents, and deliberate security failures.

The channel must:

- Allow anonymous or identified reports.
    
- Protect the identity of the whistleblower.
    
- Provide mechanisms against professional or employment-related retaliation.
    
- Generate a tracking reference number.
    
- Allow the secure submission of documents and evidence.
    
- Refer reports indicating criminal conduct to the competent authorities.
    
- Enable independent investigations when the reported institution itself is involved.
    

Protection must extend to public servants, employees, contractors, and anyone who provides information in good faith about misconduct.

**6. Mandatory notification and transparency**

Every significant incident must be reported to the competent authorities and affected data subjects, in accordance with applicable legal criteria.

The institution must provide information on:

- The nature and volume of the compromised data.
    
- The estimated number of affected data subjects.
    
- The likely period of exposure.
    
- The origin and circumstances of the incident.
    
- Measures taken to stop the breach.
    
- Risks of improper use of the information.
    
- Actions taken to protect affected data subjects.
    

Notification may not be deliberately delayed to preserve the institution's reputation or avoid accountability.

## 2. Rationale

The Brazilian General Data Protection Law (LGPD) already establishes security obligations, incident notification requirements, and administrative penalties for data processing agents.

However, this proposal seeks to strengthen individual and institutional accountability, particularly in the public sector, where the exposure of information can compromise the data of millions of citizens.

Personal, financial, registration, asset-related, and confidential information can be used for fraud, extortion, scams, identity theft, and other criminal activities.

When information held by the State is exposed, the problem goes beyond the relationship between an institution and its users: it affects public confidence in the government's ability to protect information under its responsibility.

The proposal aims to prevent significant data breaches from being treated exclusively as operational failures, without an in-depth investigation into the individuals who had access to the data and any possible criminal conduct.

It also seeks to prevent accountability from being limited to senior management, while ignoring employees, operators, contractors, and suppliers who directly participated in the incident.

## 3. Expected benefits

- **Greater individual accountability:** investigation of all agents involved, regardless of their hierarchical position.
    
- **Data breach prevention:** incentives to adopt stricter technical and administrative controls.
    
- **Combating corruption:** identification of potential schemes involving the sale, commercialization, or improper use of confidential information.
    
- **Citizen protection:** reduction of fraud and scam risks resulting from data exposure.
    
- **Transparency:** greater clarity regarding the scale of incidents and the measures taken.
    
- **Whistleblower protection:** encouragement to report misconduct without the risk of retaliation.
    
- **Institutional accountability:** incentives to maintain appropriate information security structures.
    
- **Strengthening public trust:** stricter protection of information held by the State.
    

## 4. Potential impacts

**Positive impacts:**

- Reduced negligence in the management of sensitive information.
    
- Greater investment in information security.
    
- Identification of employees and third parties who misuse their access privileges.
    
- Greater investigative capacity to uncover data commercialization schemes.
    
- Fewer opportunities for fraud resulting from data breaches.
    
- Greater transparency in public administration.
    
- Strengthening of internal and external oversight mechanisms.
    

**Operational and legal impacts:**

- Need for specialized digital investigation and forensic analysis teams.
    
- Greater requirements for access logs and operational traceability.
    
- Need for integration among oversight bodies, law enforcement authorities, the Public Prosecutor's Office, and the ANPD (National Data Protection Authority).
    
- Possibility of precautionary removal from duties during investigations.
    
- Need for administrative proceedings that ensure the right to be heard and the right to a full defense.
    
- Costs of adapting systems and security procedures.
    
- Need to protect whistleblowers and prevent unfounded accusations.
    

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

The proposal must complement Law No. 13,709/2018 (LGPD), Law No. 12,527/2011 (Access to Information Law), criminal legislation, and the statutes applicable to public servants.

The main challenges are:

- **Identification of those responsible:** establishing mandatory logging and auditing mechanisms to identify data access, queries, transfers, and modifications.
    
- **Independent investigation:** ensuring that incidents involving public bodies can be investigated by entities independent of the directly involved organizational structure.
    
- **Individual accountability:** distinguishing intentional misconduct, negligence, unavoidable technical failures, and incidents resulting from external attacks without culpable contribution by the agents involved.
    
- **Precautionary removal:** establishing objective criteria for temporarily suspending access or removing individuals from their duties during an investigation.
    
- **Removal and dismissal:** defining procedures according to the employment or public service relationship, while respecting due process of law and applicable guarantees.
    
- **Criminal accountability:** establishing mandatory referral to the competent authorities when there is evidence of a crime, including possible corruption, sale of information, and breach of confidentiality.
    
- **Incident notification:** establishing deadlines and procedures consistent with the LGPD and ANPD regulations, preventing omissions and unjustified delays.
    
- **Whistleblower protection:** ensuring confidentiality, protection against retaliation, and secure mechanisms for submitting evidence.
    
- **Contractors and suppliers:** requiring contractual clauses covering security, traceability, auditing, and accountability.
    
- **Preservation of evidence:** establishing procedures for retaining records, images, logs, and other digital evidence.
    
- **Data protection:** ensuring that investigations have a defined scope and comply with legal limits on access to personal and confidential information.
    

Investigations must cover all agents for whom there is evidence of involvement, but the imposition of sanctions will depend on proof of each individual's conduct and responsibility.

## 6. Open issues

- Define which categories of data breaches will be considered highly severe.
    
- Establish severity criteria based on the number of data subjects, data sensitivity, duration of exposure, and potential for fraud.
    
- Define the maximum deadline for opening an administrative investigation.
    
- Establish deadlines for completing investigations, allowing extensions when justified.
    
- Define which authorities may conduct independent investigations involving public bodies.
    
- Establish criteria for precautionary removal and suspension of access privileges.
    
- Define additional financial penalties for private companies and suppliers involved.
    
- Assess the creation of specific criminal aggravating circumstances for the deliberate commercialization of large databases.
    
- Define protection mechanisms and possible rewards for whistleblowers who effectively contribute to identifying those responsible.
    
- Establish criteria for public disclosure of investigation results while protecting confidential information and victims' data.
