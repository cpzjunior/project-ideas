# Anti-Excessive Public Salaries Act

## 1. Proposal

Create national legislation establishing objective limits on the compensation and financial benefits granted to public officials, preventing payments above the constitutional salary cap through allowances, bonuses, artificial reimbursements, accumulated benefits, or retroactive payments.

The proposal seeks to strengthen the constitutional principle of checks and balances, preventing public institutions from creating financial benefits for their own members and establishing greater participation by the Legislative Branch in defining compensation rules for public servants.

The legislation will be based on seven main rules:

### 1. Compensation limits and the elimination of excessive benefits

All payments made to public officials must comply with the constitutional salary cap, regardless of their designation, accounting classification, or paying institution.

All payments that represent regular financial benefits or salary supplements must be considered compensation, including:

- Bonuses.
- Additional payments.
- Cash allowances.
- Allowances without an actual reimbursement purpose.
- Personal benefits.
- Payments for artificially accumulated duties.
- Conversion of leave into cash.
- Other payments used to increase regular compensation.

No public institution may create new compensation benefits through resolutions, administrative orders, administrative decisions, or internal regulations without express legislative authorization.

Classifying a payment as an indemnity will not be sufficient to exclude it from the constitutional salary cap. There must be a legal basis and a genuine indemnification purpose.

Exceptions to the salary cap must be expressly established by national ordinary legislation, approved by the National Congress and applicable to all Branches of Government and constitutionally autonomous bodies, pursuant to Article 37, Paragraph 11, of the Federal Constitution.

### 2. Limits on allowances

All temporary financial allowances granted to public officials must be exceptional, occasional, and related to a specific need arising from the performance of their duties.

The proposal establishes two simultaneous limits:

Time limit:

- Temporary allowances may be granted for a maximum of 90 days per fiscal year.
- Successive renewals or artificial replacement of benefits to circumvent the limit are prohibited.
- Benefits may not be converted into permanent or regular payments.

Financial limit:

- The combined amount of temporary allowances may not exceed 30% of the official's base compensation per month.
- The limit must account for all allowances received, regardless of their designation or paying institution.
- Dividing a benefit into different installments or classifications to circumvent the limit is prohibited.

Reimbursement of expenses actually incurred in the performance of official duties must be subject to separate rules, requiring documentation, a legitimate purpose, and objective criteria. It may not be used as a mechanism for supplementing compensation.

Examples of situations that may justify occasional allowances:

- Relocation due to service requirements.
- Official travel.
- Extraordinary expenses arising from official duties.
- Other exceptional situations expressly provided for by law.

### 3. Prohibition of retroactive benefits

The creation of financial benefits with retroactive effects is prohibited, particularly when intended for members of the institution responsible for granting them.

The rule seeks to prevent an institution from creating a particular benefit and subsequently recognizing accumulated credits corresponding to periods before the benefit existed.

The following practices shall be prohibited:

- Creation of bonuses with financial effects preceding the publication of the authorizing law.
- Administrative granting of retroactive benefits without a legal basis in force during the corresponding period.
- Artificial accumulation of benefits for consolidated payments at a later date.
- Administrative recognition of extraordinary credits benefiting the institution's own members without a pre-existing legal basis.
- Subsequent conversion of nonexistent benefits into accumulated financial credits.

The prohibition must not extinguish legitimate property rights, such as compensation differences actually owed due to administrative errors or rights recognized by judicial decision.

Such payments must be subject to auditing, transparency, specific settlement rules, and the constitutional salary cap whenever legally applicable.

### 4. Checks and balances in determining compensation

The proposal seeks to prevent one Branch of Government from unilaterally concentrating the power to propose, create, authorize, and oversee financial benefits intended for its own members.

Compensation rules must ensure effective participation by the Legislative Branch through a public and transparent legislative process.

The following principles shall be established:

- No institution may unilaterally create financial benefits for its own members.
- The creation of compensation benefits shall require legislative authorization.
- Beneficiary institutions may not establish administrative exceptions to the constitutional salary cap.
- Oversight must be exercised by institutions with supervisory authority independent of the paying institution.
- Mechanisms must be established to prevent conflicts of interest in administrative decisions concerning benefits intended for the institution's own members.

Additionally, a constitutional amendment is proposed to revise the rules reserving legislative initiative to courts for bills establishing judicial compensation.

The proposal must allow greater participation by the Legislative Branch in introducing and defining compensation rules for judges, while preserving judicial independence, institutional autonomy, and the constitutional guarantees of the judiciary.

The establishment and revision of judicial salaries must continue to require legislative approval, in accordance with applicable budgetary and constitutional rules.

### 5. Transparency and oversight

All payments made to public officials must be individually identified and supported by a legal basis accessible to the public.

Disclosure must include:

- Identification of the beneficiary.
- Position and institution.
- Base compensation.
- Bonuses and additional payments.
- Allowances and indemnities.
- Retroactive payments.
- Total gross amount.
- Legal basis for each payment.

Oversight bodies must have mechanisms for cross-referencing data to identify payments inconsistent with the constitutional salary cap and the limits established by legislation.

Oversight must involve, according to their respective legal powers:

- Federal Court of Accounts (TCU).
- State and municipal Courts of Accounts.
- National Council of Justice (CNJ).
- National Council of the Public Prosecutor's Office (CNMP).
- Office of the Comptroller General of the Union (CGU).
- Other internal and external oversight bodies.

Payments lacking a legal basis must be subject to investigation, with the possibility of suspension and recovery of undue amounts, respecting due process and institutional jurisdiction.

### 6. Accountability for irregular grants

Public officials and authorities who create, authorize, or execute payments in violation of the law must be subject to applicable administrative, civil, and criminal consequences.

Accountability must consider:

- Actual involvement in granting the irregular benefit.
- The existence of intent or negligence, as applicable under the relevant legal framework.
- Any financial benefit obtained.
- Damage caused to public funds.
- Attempts to conceal or manipulate the nature of payments.

Unduly paid amounts must be subject to recovery procedures, respecting the right to due process, the right to a full defense, and the legal rules governing reimbursement.

The legislation must prevent internal administrative decisions from being used to justify payments without a legal basis.

### 7. Complementary constitutional amendment

In addition to national ordinary legislation, the proposal includes a constitutional amendment to improve the constitutional mechanisms governing compensation oversight.

The amendment must address:

- Revision of the legislative initiative rules for establishing and revising judicial salaries.
- Strengthening the Legislative Branch's participation in determining compensation.
- Improvement of external oversight mechanisms for payments made by public institutions.
- Strengthening of conflict-of-interest prevention rules.
- Preservation of independence among the Branches of Government and the constitutional guarantees of public officials.

The constitutional amendment must complement the Anti-Excessive Public Salaries Act without replacing the national regulation of indemnity payments provided for in Article 37, Paragraph 11, of the Federal Constitution.

## 2. Rationale

The Federal Constitution already establishes limits on public officials' compensation, particularly through Article 37, Section XI.

However, the existence of different bonuses, allowances, indemnities, and financial benefits may allow certain public officials to receive amounts significantly above the constitutional salary cap.

The proposal seeks to eliminate the use of administrative and financial classifications to transform regular compensation into supposed indemnity payments.

Another problem is the possibility of public institutions creating financial benefits intended for their own members through administrative decisions and internal regulations.

This situation creates an institutional governance problem: beneficiaries may participate in administrative processes that determine the creation, interpretation, or granting of benefits they themselves will receive.

Although the establishment of salaries requires legislative approval, the Constitution reserves the initiative for the corresponding bills to the courts, pursuant to Article 96, Section II, item "b".

The proposal seeks to improve this mechanism, strengthening checks and balances and expanding the Legislative Branch's participation in defining compensation rules.

The creation of retroactive benefits also represents a significant problem.

When a benefit is created at a later date and its financial effects are extended to previous periods, accumulated credits and extraordinary payments may arise, allowing certain officials to receive amounts far exceeding their regular monthly compensation.

The proposal intends to prevent this practice by establishing that new benefits may only produce financial effects from their effective date, without affecting legitimate property rights previously established.

Furthermore, the granting of permanent allowances may transform benefits originally intended for exceptional circumstances into mechanisms for supplementing salaries.

The proposed 90-day-per-fiscal-year time limit and the 30% base compensation financial limit seek to prevent this use.

Finally, the lack of individualized transparency and effectively independent oversight makes it difficult to identify payments inconsistent with the law.

The proposal intends to transform the constitutional salary cap into an effectively enforceable limit, reducing opportunities for arbitrary grants and extraordinary payments.

## 3. Expected benefits

- Reduction of excessive public salaries: reduction of payments above the constitutional salary cap through artificial benefits.
- Elimination of excessive benefits: prevention of compensation benefits being created without legislative authorization.
- Allowance control: time and financial limits on exceptional benefits.
- End of artificial retroactive payments: prevention of newly created benefits having retroactive financial effects.
- Strengthening of checks and balances: greater separation between beneficiaries, legislators, and oversight bodies.
- Reduction of conflicts of interest: decreased possibility of benefits being granted by their own beneficiaries.
- Compensation transparency: individualized disclosure of all payments made.
- Independent oversight: greater ability to identify and challenge irregular payments.
- Accountability: strengthening of mechanisms for recovering amounts unduly paid.
- Budget predictability: greater control over personnel expenses and extraordinary benefits.
- Equity in public service: reduction of compensation disparities caused by unequally granted benefits.
- Institutional strengthening: greater confidence in public administration oversight mechanisms.

## 4. Potential impacts

Positive impacts:

- Reduction of public spending on extraordinary compensation benefits.
- Greater effectiveness of the constitutional salary cap.
- Reduced possibility of public institutions creating benefits for their own members.
- Greater participation by the Legislative Branch in defining compensation rules.
- Reduction of retroactive payments resulting from benefits created at a later date.
- Greater transparency regarding the actual compensation received by public officials.
- Reduced use of indemnities as mechanisms for supplementing salaries.
- Greater predictability of public expenditure.
- Strengthening of oversight and external control mechanisms.
- Greater standardization of benefits granted to public officials.

Negative impacts or risks:

- Possibility of legal challenges concerning the application of the limits to existing benefits.
- Need to review administrative rules and regulations currently in force.
- Possibility of institutional conflicts involving the administrative and financial autonomy of the Branches of Government.
- Risk of inappropriate restrictions on legitimate reimbursement of official expenses.
- Need to adapt payroll systems.
- Possibility of increased litigation during the transition to the new framework.
- Need to establish specific rules for acquired rights and legally recognized obligations.
- Risk of alternative classifications being used to circumvent the new limits.

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

The proposal may be implemented through national ordinary legislation, supplemented by a constitutional amendment on matters requiring changes to the structure of constitutional powers and legislative initiatives.

National regulation of indemnity payments is directly supported by Article 37, Paragraph 11, of the Federal Constitution, as amended by Constitutional Amendment No. 135/2024.

The main challenges are:

- Definition of base compensation: establishing a uniform methodology for calculating the 30%, considering the official's permanent salary or fixed compensation, without including allowances and temporary benefits.
- Allowance limits: defining which benefits are subject to the 90-day-per-fiscal-year and 30%-of-base-compensation limits.
- Legitimate reimbursements: distinguishing indemnities for expenses actually incurred from benefits used to supplement compensation.
- Application of the salary cap: establishing criteria for accounting for all compensation components, regardless of their designation or paying institution.
- Retroactivity: defining rules to prevent the creation of benefits with financial effects preceding their legal authorization, while preserving legitimate pre-existing obligations.
- Checks and balances: assessing the constitutional amendments necessary to expand the Legislative Branch's participation in determining compensation for members of the different Branches of Government.
- Institutional autonomy: reconciling compensation oversight rules with the administrative, financial, and functional independence of the Branches of Government and constitutionally autonomous bodies.
- Oversight: establishing auditing and information cross-referencing mechanisms among oversight bodies.
- Accountability: defining procedures for suspending irregular payments and recovering undue amounts.
- Transparency: standardizing the publication of individual payments and their respective legal grounds.
- Legislative transition: establishing deadlines for reviewing existing benefits and bringing administrative rules into compliance.
- Payment systems: adapting payroll platforms to automatically identify the salary cap and the financial and time limits applicable to allowances.

The proposal must ensure national application of the rules, covering the Federal Government, states, the Federal District, and municipalities, while respecting constitutional powers.

The 90-day and 30% limits must operate as general limits for temporary allowances covered by the legislation and may not be circumvented by creating different designations for benefits serving the same purpose.

The constitutional amendment must be treated separately from ordinary legislation, since changing the constitutional rules governing courts' legislative initiative requires a constitutional amendment.

## 6. Open issues

- Precisely define which components make up the base compensation used to calculate the 30% limit.
- Determine whether the 30% limit will apply individually to each month or will also have an accumulated annual limit.
- Establish the relationship between the 90-day limit and the different types of temporary allowances.
- Define the treatment of per diems, relocation assistance, moving expenses, and other official expense reimbursements.
- Establish a closed national list of indemnity payments that may be excluded from the constitutional salary cap.
- Define criteria to prevent bonuses from being artificially classified as indemnities.
- Establish how retroactive rights recognized by judicial decision before the law takes effect will be treated.
- Define rules for installment payments of legitimate outstanding obligations without allowing extraordinary payments to be used to circumvent the salary cap.
- Assess the possibility of amending Article 96, Section II, item "b", of the Federal Constitution to change the legislative initiative reserved to the courts.
- Assess constitutional mechanisms to expand legislative oversight over the creation and revision of compensation benefits.
- Define which bodies will have the authority to audit, suspend, and order the recovery of irregular payments.
- Establish deadlines for reviewing and adapting currently existing benefits.
- Assess the need for specific rules for careers involving extraordinary official expenses.
- Define accountability criteria for authorities who authorize payments without a legal basis.
