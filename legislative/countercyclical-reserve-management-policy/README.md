# Countercyclical Reserve Management Policy

## 1. Idea

Create legislation establishing a permanent policy for the coordinated management of international reserves and federal public debt, introducing mechanisms to use the public sector's financial capacity in a countercyclical manner.

The proposal seeks to integrate foreign exchange management and public debt management, allowing the country to use periods of greater external resource availability to strengthen its reserves and, when there are legally available resources exceeding a prudential threshold, contribute to reducing public debt.

During periods of foreign exchange stress, the policy will enable coordinated action between the Central Bank and the National Treasury, combining foreign exchange market intervention instruments with public debt management measures aimed at strengthening the confidence of economic agents.

The legislation will be based on five main rules:

1. Minimum safety reserve

Establish a prudential minimum level of international reserves, below which resources may not be used for public debt amortization purposes.

The minimum level shall consider external vulnerability indicators, including:

- Short-term external debt.
- Import coverage.
- External financing requirements.
- Potential capital flight.
- Volatility of international capital flows.
- Foreign exchange and financial stress scenarios.

The parameter shall be reviewed periodically, based on technical criteria and reserve adequacy studies.

2. Use of excess resources to reduce debt

When international reserves exceed the established prudential level, an assessment shall be conducted regarding the possibility of using legally available resources to reduce federal public debt.

The use of these resources shall observe the following:

- Full preservation of the prudential minimum reserve level.
- Existence of financial and legal availability for the transaction.
- Comparison between the cost of public debt and the return on international reserves.
- Assessment of the foreign exchange and financial risks involved.
- Analysis of the impact on the consolidated public sector balance sheet.

Amortization shall prioritize transactions that offer a net economic benefit, taking into account interest savings, the costs of using reserves, and the strategic value of international liquidity.

The transfer of resources shall comply with the legal and accounting framework governing the relationship between the Central Bank and the National Treasury. The mere fact that reserves exceed a given threshold shall not be sufficient to authorize their transfer.

3. Mechanism for action during periods of foreign exchange stress

Establish a coordinated response mechanism for situations involving significant currency depreciation or deteriorating conditions in the foreign exchange market.

When stress conditions are identified, the Central Bank may use its foreign exchange intervention instruments, including:

- Foreign exchange swap operations.
- Dollar sales in the spot market.
- Other legally available instruments to provide liquidity and reduce market dysfunction.

In parallel, the Central Bank and the National Treasury shall assess the country's fiscal and financial conditions, including the possibility of accelerating or expanding public debt management operations, provided that legally available resources exist and a demonstrable economic benefit can be established.

The assessment shall consider whether currency depreciation is associated with external factors, changes in economic fundamentals, fiscal deterioration, or loss of investor confidence.

Debt amortization shall not be used as a substitute for the foreign exchange interventions required to stabilize the market, but it may form part of a coordinated economic response when there is a relationship between foreign exchange stress and the government's financing conditions.

4. Reserve replenishment mechanism

During periods of currency appreciation, increased capital inflows, or favorable external conditions, the Central Bank may consider purchasing foreign currency to replenish international reserves.

Replenishment shall take into account:

- The current level of reserves.
- The gap relative to the prudential level.
- Foreign exchange market liquidity conditions.
- The monetary impact of the operations.
- The costs and benefits of maintaining additional reserves.

The objective shall be to preserve the country's external protection capacity and, when economically justified, allow future excess resources to be assessed for public debt reduction.

5. Joint governance and transparency

The policy shall be implemented through institutional coordination between the Central Bank and the National Treasury, while respecting the legal powers of each institution.

Responsibilities shall be distributed as follows:

- Central Bank: assessment of foreign exchange conditions, management of international reserves, and execution of interventions in the foreign exchange market.
- National Treasury: management of federal public debt, assessment of amortization opportunities, and execution of the corresponding transactions.
- National Monetary Council: establishment of guidelines and parameters within its legal powers.
- National Congress: oversight of the policy's implementation, with access to periodic reports and information on the results achieved.

The Copom shall remain responsible for defining monetary policy and shall not assume fiscal management or public debt amortization responsibilities.

Decisions shall be based on technical criteria, with the publication of reports enabling the monitoring of reserve levels, transactions carried out, and their effects on public debt.

## 2. Motivation

Brazil maintains a substantial volume of international reserves as a means of protection against external crises, exchange rate fluctuations, and difficulties in international financing.

At the same time, the country has significant public debt, whose financing costs account for a substantial share of government expenditure.

Although international reserves and public debt are distinct components of the public sector's balance sheet, their coordinated management may enable more efficient decisions regarding the use of available resources.

Currently, international reserve management primarily aims to preserve external liquidity and reduce the country's vulnerability to international shocks. Public debt management, in turn, seeks to ensure government financing at the lowest possible cost, taking the associated risks into account.

The proposal seeks to integrate these two perspectives by establishing criteria for assessing when maintaining additional reserves provides a greater economic benefit than using legally available resources to reduce public indebtedness.

Another relevant aspect is the relationship between fiscal confidence and foreign exchange stability.

In certain situations, depreciation of the national currency may be associated with the perception of deteriorating fiscal conditions and increased government financing risk. In such scenarios, a coordinated response combining foreign exchange instruments and debt management measures may help improve perceptions of solvency and economic predictability.

The proposal also seeks to prevent reserve reductions from compromising the country's ability to respond to external crises. To this end, it establishes the mandatory maintenance of a prudential minimum level, preventing resources required for international financial security from being used for fiscal purposes.

The mechanism will be countercyclical in nature: during favorable periods, the country may replenish its reserves and assess the use of any excess resources; during adverse periods, it may use its foreign exchange and fiscal instruments in a coordinated manner.

The purpose is not to replace fiscal balance, but to improve the management of public assets and liabilities, reduce financial costs when economically advantageous, and strengthen the State's ability to respond to economic shocks.

## 3. Expected benefits

- Integrated management of public assets: coordination between international reserve management and public debt management.
- Reduced debt costs: possibility of using excess and legally available resources to amortize public obligations when there is an economic benefit.
- Foreign exchange protection: preservation of a prudential minimum level of reserves to address external crises.
- Financial stability: creation of coordinated response instruments for situations of foreign exchange and financial stress.
- Investor confidence: greater predictability in the management of public resources and in responses to periods of instability.
- Countercyclical management: use of favorable economic periods to strengthen the country's financial position.
- Better use of public assets: ongoing assessment of the relationship between reserve returns and debt costs.
- Reduced external vulnerability: maintenance of sufficient resources to address international financing difficulties.
- Institutional transparency: clear allocation of responsibilities and disclosure of the criteria used in transactions.
- Fiscal discipline: integration of the policy into debt management without replacing the need for balanced public accounts.
- Economic predictability: establishment of permanent rules, reducing dependence on improvised decisions during periods of crisis.

## 4. Potential impacts

Positive impacts:

- Potential reduction in the stock and financial cost of federal public debt.
- More efficient economic use of available resources on the public sector's balance sheet.
- Greater coordination between foreign exchange policy and public debt management.
- Reduced exposure to international liquidity crises.
- Greater capacity to respond to abrupt currency depreciation.
- Potential improvement in the perception of fiscal risk in situations where indebtedness is a relevant factor behind foreign exchange instability.
- Greater predictability for domestic and foreign investors.
- Incentive for technical, long-term management of international reserves.
- Reduced financial costs when debt amortization offers a return greater than the opportunity cost of the reserves used.
- Strengthening of governance over public sector assets and liabilities.

Negative impacts or risks:

- Excessive reduction in international reserves if the prudential level is calculated inadequately.
- Possibility of using resources in transactions that do not provide a net economic benefit.
- Risk of confusing depreciation caused by international factors with a fiscal confidence crisis.
- Possibility that foreign exchange interventions may increase volatility if implemented without appropriate technical criteria.
- Risk that the market may anticipate the policy triggers and attempt to exploit them through speculative movements.
- Need for coordination between institutions with distinct powers and objectives.
- Potential monetary and liquidity impacts resulting from foreign exchange and debt amortization transactions.
- Legal and accounting complexity in the use of results and resources linked to international reserves.
- Possibility that a reduction in gross debt may not represent an equivalent improvement in net debt or in the consolidated financial position of the public sector.
- Risk that the policy may be interpreted as a substitute for fiscal adjustment, reducing incentives for budgetary responsibility.

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

The proposal may be implemented through federal legislation supplementing existing rules on international reserve management, the financial relationship between the Central Bank and the National Treasury, and federal public debt management.

It will be necessary to assess amendments to Law No. 13,820/2019 and other relevant regulations, while respecting the Central Bank's operational autonomy and the legal powers of the National Treasury and the National Monetary Council.

The main challenges are:

- Definition of the prudential reserve level: establish a technical methodology for calculating the minimum volume required for external protection, considering international indicators and stress scenarios.
- Identification of excess resources: distinguish the gross stock of reserves from the financial results and resources actually available for use, in compliance with legal and accounting rules.
- Assessment of economic benefits: compare public debt costs with reserve returns, taking into account foreign exchange risks, carrying costs, bond maturities, and the strategic value of international liquidity.
- Compatibility with existing legislation: define how the policy will interact with Law No. 13,820/2019 and the rules governing the use of Central Bank results.
- Institutional coordination: establish formal cooperation procedures between the Central Bank and the National Treasury while preserving each institution's responsibilities.
- Definition of foreign exchange triggers: establish criteria for identifying stress situations, avoiding the use of a specific dollar exchange rate as the sole indicator of a crisis.
- Distinction between external and fiscal shocks: assess whether currency depreciation results from international factors, changes in economic fundamentals, or deterioration in fiscal confidence.
- Monetary impacts: define how the effects of transactions on financial system liquidity and monetary policy implementation will be managed.
- Execution of amortizations: establish procedures for the National Treasury to use legally available resources to repurchase, settle, or amortize public securities.
- Transparency: require periodic reports on reserve levels, foreign exchange operations, resources used, and financial results achieved.
- Oversight: enable monitoring by the National Congress and the competent oversight bodies.
- Preservation of fiscal responsibility: prevent the use of reserves from being accounted for as a substitute for recurring fiscal revenues or used to justify permanent primary deficits.

The legislation shall make clear that the sale of dollars by the Central Bank does not, in itself, generate fiscal resources available for public debt amortization.

Likewise, the existence of reserves above the prudential level does not automatically authorize their transfer to the National Treasury.

Their use shall occur exclusively through legally permitted mechanisms, taking into account the origin of the resources, the financial results actually available, and the effects on the consolidated assets and liabilities of the public sector.

The policy shall also not establish an obligation to intervene in the foreign exchange market whenever the dollar exceeds a certain value. Triggers shall consider a set of indicators, including volatility, liquidity, external conditions, and fiscal fundamentals.

## 6. Open points

- Define the methodology for determining the minimum prudential level of international reserves.
- Establish whether the prudential level will be set by an absolute value, percentage, or external adequacy indicators.
- Define how frequently the minimum reserve level shall be reviewed.
- Determine which resources and financial results may legally be used for public debt amortization.
- Assess which amendments will be necessary to Law No. 13,820/2019.
- Define the methodology for comparing public debt costs with the returns and maintenance costs of international reserves.
- Establish criteria for identifying when the use of excess resources provides a net economic benefit.
- Define the indicators that will characterize a situation of foreign exchange stress.
- Assess whether triggers should consider exchange rate volatility, cumulative currency movements, liquidity conditions, or fiscal risk indicators.
- Define which decisions will fall within the responsibilities of the Central Bank, the National Treasury, and the National Monetary Council.
- Establish procedures for joint operations during periods of foreign exchange instability.
- Define how the monetary and liquidity effects resulting from these operations will be managed.
- Establish criteria for prioritizing securities and obligations in amortization operations.
- Define transparency, accountability, and auditing mechanisms.
- Assess the possibility of establishing indicative debt reduction targets through excess resources.
- Define how the policy will be integrated with existing fiscal rules.
- Assess the possibility of gradual implementation, with an initial testing period and a review of the parameters after the first years of operation.

- Law No. 4,595/1964: governs the National Financial System and establishes the powers of the National Monetary Council and the Central Bank.
