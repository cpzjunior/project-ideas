# National Program for Attracting and Integrating Immigrants

## 1. Idea

Create a National Program for Attracting and Integrating Immigrants, aimed at positioning Brazil as an attractive destination for people who voluntarily wish to establish a new life in the country, while also strengthening its capacity to welcome refugees and people displaced by humanitarian crises.

The program would operate along two complementary fronts:

- Voluntary immigration: attract individuals and families interested in living in Brazil for reasons such as security, quality of life, employment opportunities, entrepreneurship, and the possibility of building a new life. The program could prioritize applicants from Portuguese-speaking countries and people of working age, without excluding other nationalities, profiles, or family arrangements.
- Humanitarian reception: welcome refugees and people displaced by armed conflicts, persecution, and humanitarian crises, ensuring appropriate protection and integration conditions, with financial and technical support from international cooperation where available.

The program would combine measures to facilitate immigration procedures, promote Brazil internationally, provide free Portuguese language education, support professional and social integration, and encourage voluntary naturalization and acquisition of Brazilian citizenship.

It would also involve foreign communities already established in Brazil, recognizing their role in welcoming newcomers, sharing information about the country, and supporting cultural and social integration.

## 2. Motivation

Brazil has a historical tradition of receiving immigrants, particularly during the 19th and early 20th centuries, when successive waves of immigration contributed to the country's demographic, cultural, economic, and social development.

In a context of geopolitical instability, armed conflicts, and uncertainty about the international order, Brazil could strengthen its position as an alternative destination for people seeking a safer and more stable environment in which to live and work. Its geographic distance from major international conflict zones and its relative political and institutional stability may be attractive factors for potential immigrants, although they do not make the country immune to risks.

However, the program should not be understood solely as a response to the possibility of a major international conflict. Even in periods of relative global stability, an active immigration policy could contribute to economic development, demographic renewal, entrepreneurship, cultural diversity, and stronger international relations.

Brazil could once again assume a more active role in attracting immigrants, adapting its historical experience to the needs and opportunities of the 21st century.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Economic development: increase the workforce, consumer base, tax revenue, and entrepreneurial activity.
- Demographic renewal: help address population aging and the long-term reduction in the working-age population.
- Entrepreneurship and job creation: attract professionals, investors, entrepreneurs, and workers who can establish businesses, expand existing activities, and create employment opportunities.
- International competitiveness: attract qualified professionals and people with experience in strategic sectors, contributing to innovation and knowledge transfer.
- Cultural diversity: strengthen Brazil's multicultural identity and expand opportunities for cultural exchange.
- International relations: deepen diplomatic, economic, and cultural ties with countries of origin.
- Humanitarian contribution: expand Brazil's capacity to protect refugees and people displaced by conflicts and persecution.
- Development of public services: create demand for teachers, translators, healthcare professionals, housing, education, and other services, generating economic activity and employment.
- Portuguese language promotion: expand access to Portuguese education and strengthen Brazil's cultural and linguistic presence internationally.
- Social integration and citizenship: encourage immigrants who wish to do so to become Brazilian citizens and participate fully in the country's civic life.

## 4. Potential Impacts

### Positive impacts

- Increased economic activity through the arrival of workers, consumers, entrepreneurs, and investors.
- Greater availability of skilled and unskilled labor in sectors facing shortages.
- Creation of new businesses and jobs, including in housing, education, healthcare, construction, commerce, and services.
- Strengthening of Brazil's international image as a country open to immigration and humanitarian protection.
- Greater cultural diversity and the development of international connections.
- Expansion of Portuguese language education and related professional opportunities.
- Increased population and economic activity in regions that could benefit from immigration.
- Strengthening of local communities through cultural exchange and the participation of immigrant associations.

### Negative impacts and limitations

- Increased demand for housing, healthcare, education, transportation, and other public services, particularly in municipalities with limited infrastructure.
- Risk of social tensions if integration policies and public communication are insufficient.
- Potential competition for jobs in specific sectors or regions, depending on local economic conditions.
- Administrative and fiscal costs associated with immigration processing, reception, language education, and integration.
- Challenges related to the recognition of foreign academic and professional qualifications.
- Risk of exploitation, informality, discrimination, and precarious working conditions among immigrants.
- Dependence on international funding for some humanitarian initiatives, which may be limited or unpredictable.
- Need to balance immigration growth with the capacity of municipalities and public services.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

### 5.1. Eligibility and Prioritization Criteria

The program should establish clear criteria for the different immigration pathways.

For voluntary immigration, it could prioritize applicants from Portuguese-speaking countries and people of working age, considering factors such as professional qualifications, work experience, entrepreneurial potential, family ties, and interest in settling in Brazil.

These priorities should not become absolute restrictions. Other nationalities, professional profiles, and family arrangements should also be eligible.

For humanitarian reception, eligibility and prioritization must follow applicable refugee protection principles and international commitments. Economic or linguistic preferences should not be used to restrict access to humanitarian protection.

### 5.2. International Promotion

Develop an international communication strategy to present Brazil as a destination for people interested in living, working, studying, or starting businesses in the country.

The strategy could include:

- Official information portals in multiple languages.
- Communication campaigns in Portuguese-speaking countries and other potential source countries.
- Partnerships with Brazilian diplomatic missions, universities, companies, and international organizations.
- Clear information about immigration procedures, employment opportunities, cost of living, education, healthcare, and quality of life.
- Promotion of Brazil's cultural diversity, economic potential, and opportunities for personal and professional development.

### 5.3. Immigration Process Facilitation

Review and modernize immigration procedures to make them more accessible, transparent, and predictable.

Potential measures include:

- Digitalization and centralization of application procedures.
- Clear information about requirements and documentation.
- Online tracking of applications and requests.
- Improved coordination among government agencies.
- Increased administrative capacity to process applications.
- Progressive service targets to reduce processing times.

The implementation should consider the current capacity of public agencies. Significant reductions in processing times may require investments in technology, staffing, and administrative restructuring and should not be assumed to be immediately achievable.

### 5.4. Portuguese Language Education

Free Portuguese language education should be one of the program's central pillars.

The initiative could offer in-person and online courses through public educational institutions and partnerships with universities, municipalities, and civil society organizations.

Courses could include:

- Basic Portuguese for everyday communication.
- Portuguese for professional and workplace contexts.
- Language preparation for people who have not yet arrived in Brazil.
- Post-arrival language support.
- Courses adapted to different levels of proficiency and educational backgrounds.
- Language proficiency assessments and certification.

The program should take advantage of existing public initiatives and educational structures, expanding their reach and capacity where necessary.

### 5.5. Professional and Economic Integration

Promote the integration of immigrants into the Brazilian labor market and entrepreneurial environment.

Potential measures include:

- Information about employment opportunities and labor rights.
- Support for professional qualification and retraining.
- Guidance on the recognition of foreign academic and professional credentials.
- Partnerships with companies, industry associations, and employment agencies.
- Support for entrepreneurship and the creation of new businesses.
- Access to information about business registration, taxation, and regulatory requirements.

Immigrants should be recognized not only as potential workers, but also as consumers, taxpayers, entrepreneurs, investors, and employers.

### 5.6. Humanitarian Reception and International Cooperation

Strengthen Brazil's capacity to receive refugees and people displaced by conflicts, persecution, and humanitarian crises.

The program could coordinate with international organizations, foreign governments, municipalities, and civil society organizations to provide reception, housing, healthcare, education, professional training, and social integration.

International cooperation could contribute financial and technical resources through existing programs and specific projects involving organizations such as the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), the World Bank, the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB), and other international partners.

Such resources would depend on the availability of funding, the approval of specific projects, and the conditions established by each cooperation mechanism. The program should not rely exclusively on international funding.

### 5.7. Participation of Foreign Communities

Foreign communities already established in Brazil could play an important role in welcoming and integrating newcomers.

The program could establish a National Network of Migrant Communities, bringing together immigrant associations, cultural organizations, religious institutions, and community groups.

These organizations could contribute through:

- Reception and initial guidance for newcomers.
- Mentoring and support during the integration process.
- Information about housing, employment, education, and public services.
- Cultural and language exchange activities.
- International promotion of Brazil through their networks and personal experiences.
- Cooperation with public authorities and local communities.

These communities could act as informal ambassadors for Brazil, helping potential immigrants understand the country and supporting newcomers during their transition.

### 5.8. Peaceful Coexistence

The program should promote Brazil as a country where people from different nationalities, cultures, religions, and political backgrounds can live together peacefully.

Immigrants should be free to preserve their identities, languages, cultures, religious beliefs, and political opinions. However, hostilities, persecution, intimidation, and violence associated with conflicts in their countries of origin should not be reproduced within Brazilian communities.

This principle should be based on respect for Brazilian laws and institutions, freedom of expression, the rejection of xenophobia and discrimination, and the peaceful resolution of disagreements.

It should not be used to restrict legitimate political expression or peaceful demonstrations concerning international conflicts.

### 5.9. Naturalization and Citizenship

The program should encourage immigrants who wish to do so to pursue naturalization and become Brazilian citizens.

This could include:

- Clear information about naturalization requirements and procedures.
- Guidance and support throughout the application process.
- Portuguese language courses and proficiency preparation.
- Educational activities about Brazilian history, culture, institutions, and civic life.
- Simplification and digitalization of administrative procedures where legally possible.

Naturalization must remain voluntary. Once naturalized, immigrants become Brazilian citizens and may exercise the political rights established by the Constitution and applicable legislation, including the right to vote subject to the relevant requirements.

### 5.10. Monitoring and Evaluation

Establish mechanisms to monitor the program's implementation and evaluate its results over time.

Potential indicators include:

- Number of immigrants admitted through each pathway.
- Countries of origin and demographic profiles.
- Processing times for immigration applications.
- Participation and completion rates in Portuguese language courses.
- Employment, income, and entrepreneurship indicators.
- Regional distribution of immigrants.
- Use of public services and local infrastructure.
- Naturalization rates.
- Results of humanitarian reception and integration initiatives.
- Economic and fiscal impacts.

Periodic evaluations should help identify implementation problems, improve resource allocation, and adjust program priorities.

## 6. Open Questions

- Which immigration pathways should be created or expanded?
- What eligibility criteria and prioritization mechanisms should apply to voluntary immigration?
- How should humanitarian reception be coordinated with existing refugee protection policies?
- Which countries and regions should be prioritized in international promotion campaigns?
- Should the program offer specific incentives for certain professional profiles, sectors, or regions?
- What targets should be established for immigration application processing times?
- How should free Portuguese language education be structured and funded?
- How should foreign academic and professional qualifications be recognized?
- Which municipalities should participate, and how should local infrastructure and public services be prepared?
- What forms of international cooperation and funding could be used?
- How should the National Network of Migrant Communities be organized?
- What mechanisms could encourage voluntary naturalization and civic integration?
- What would be the estimated implementation and operating costs?
- How should the economic, demographic, and social impacts be measured?
- Which changes to existing legislation or regulations, if any, would be necessary?
