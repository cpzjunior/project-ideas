# Geopolitical Neutrality and International Peace Act

## 1. Idea

Create legislation establishing a state policy to position Brazil as a nation committed to peace, geopolitical neutrality, and South American economic integration, transforming the region into an environment of stability, cooperation, and security for people, businesses, and international investments.

The proposal seeks to reduce the risks of military conflicts in the region, increase economic interdependence among South American countries, and preserve Brazil's autonomy amid disputes between the world's major powers.

The legislation will be based on three main principles:

### 1. Military Non-Aggression

Brazil will formally commit not to initiate offensive wars against any country, regardless of its location, economic power, or geopolitical alignment.

The use of the Armed Forces in military operations must comply with the following principles:

- Preservation of the right to legitimate self-defense in the event of aggression against national territory, population, or sovereignty.
- Prohibition of initiating wars for the purposes of territorial conquest, political imposition, or expansion of influence through military means.
- Priority given to diplomatic and peaceful resolution of international disputes.
- Defense of the territorial integrity and sovereignty of other countries.
- Non-participation in offensive wars initiated by other nations, except for international obligations compatible with the principles established in this legislation and with the Federal Constitution.

This principle must guide Brazilian foreign policy and military actions, regardless of changes in government.

The proposal will not prevent Brazil from participating in international peacekeeping missions, humanitarian operations, or collective defense initiatives, provided that constitutional principles and international commitments are respected.

### 2. South American Economic Interdependence

Brazil will promote the creation of permanent mechanisms for reciprocal investments among South American countries, seeking to establish shared economic interests that increase the cost of potential conflicts and encourage regional stability.

The main initiative will be the creation of a cross-investment program based on economic reciprocity.

Example of how it would work:

- Brazil invests US$ 1 billion in infrastructure, energy, or technology projects in Bolivia.
- Bolivia makes equivalent investments of US$ 1 billion in productive projects, infrastructure, or technology in Brazil.
- Both countries begin to have concrete economic interests in each other's stability and prosperity.

The program must observe the following principles:

- Reciprocity: encouraging investments of equivalent value between participating countries.
- Shared development: prioritizing projects that generate economic benefits for both parties.
- Diversification: investments in infrastructure, energy, logistics, industry, technology, agriculture, and scientific research.
- Continuity: institutional protection of investments against changes in government and diplomatic fluctuations.
- Transparency: disclosure of invested amounts, funded projects, economic results, and returns.

Reciprocity will not necessarily have to occur through direct investments of equal value in every case. Multilateral funds, corporate equity investments, financing, and economic compensation mechanisms may also be used.

The initiative may also include the creation of a regional development fund, with the voluntary participation of South American countries.

The objective will be to make the economic prosperity of participating countries increasingly interconnected, making military conflicts economically disadvantageous for everyone involved.

### 3. Strategic Neutrality Among Major Powers

Brazil will adopt a policy of strategic neutrality in geopolitical disputes between the world's major powers, preserving its decision-making autonomy and avoiding automatic alignment with any international bloc.

The policy must observe the following principles:

- No automatic military alignment with the interests of any major power.
- Maintenance of independent diplomatic and commercial relations with different geopolitical blocs.
- Pursuit of equivalent conditions and non-discriminatory treatment in financial, commercial, technological, and international cooperation agreements among comparable partners.
- Diversification of sources of financing, investments, technology, energy, and foreign trade.
- Preservation of Brazilian autonomy in defining its foreign policy.
- Non-participation in military conflicts between major powers, except in cases of legitimate self-defense and other international obligations compatible with the Constitution.
- Promotion of balanced diplomatic relations with countries such as the United States, China, Russia, members of the European Union, and other relevant powers.

Neutrality must not mean international isolation, withdrawal from existing agreements, or an obligation to accept identical commercial terms from different countries.

The principle will be to ensure that Brazil can establish relations with different powers without becoming politically, economically, or militarily dependent on any of them.

The policy must also encourage national autonomy in strategic sectors, reducing vulnerabilities arising from external dependencies.

## 2. Motivation

The international landscape presents growing risks of conflicts between major powers, trade disputes, military tensions, and fragmentation of international economic relations.

A potential Third World War could have economic, humanitarian, and political consequences of enormous proportions, especially for countries directly involved in the conflicts or dependent on international supply chains.

In this context, South America has geographical and strategic characteristics that may favor its positioning as a region of relative stability, food security, energy availability, and economic development potential.

However, the absence of significant military conflicts among South American countries is not, by itself, sufficient to guarantee the continuation of this condition.

The proposal seeks to transform regional peace into a permanent institutional commitment, accompanied by economic mechanisms that increase incentives for its preservation.

The first principle establishes that Brazil will not use its military capabilities to initiate offensive wars. This seeks to reduce the possibility of Brazilian involvement in international conflicts and strengthen the country's credibility as an actor committed to the peaceful resolution of disputes.

The second principle seeks to create a structure of economic interdependence among South American countries. When different nations have shared investments, companies, infrastructure, and productive projects, potential conflicts begin to represent direct economic losses for everyone involved.

The existence of reciprocal economic interests can function as an additional deterrence mechanism, complementing diplomatic and institutional instruments for conflict prevention.

The third principle seeks to prevent Brazil from being drawn into strategic disputes between major powers. Maintaining balanced relations with different countries makes it possible to preserve commercial, financial, and technological opportunities while reducing the risks of dependency and retaliation resulting from geopolitical alignments.

Furthermore, the combination of these three principles may create an international positioning opportunity for South America.

In a scenario of global instability, countries that offer institutional predictability, legal certainty, political stability, and lower exposure to military conflicts may become more attractive destinations for:

- People who wish to live in regions with lower exposure to international conflicts.
- Companies seeking to diversify their operations and supply chains.
- Investors interested in stable economic environments.
- Skilled professionals seeking opportunities outside geopolitically unstable regions.
- Scientific and technological institutions interested in establishing research centers in safe environments.

The proposal therefore seeks to transform peace and neutrality into strategic advantages for Brazilian and South American economic development.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- **Reduced risk of military conflicts:** institutional commitment not to initiate offensive wars.
- **Strengthened diplomacy:** prioritization of peaceful mechanisms for resolving international disputes.
- **Economic deterrence:** creation of shared financial and productive interests among South American countries.
- **Regional integration:** increased economic, technological, energy, and logistical cooperation.
- **Attraction of international investments:** strengthening South America's image as a region of stability.
- **Attraction of skilled professionals:** creation of conditions to welcome people seeking environments less exposed to international conflicts.
- **Geopolitical autonomy:** reduced Brazilian dependence on major powers.
- **Trade diversification:** expansion of economic relations with different international blocs.
- **Energy and food security:** encouragement of regional cooperation in strategic sectors.
- **Technological development:** promotion of joint research, innovation, and industrial production projects.
- **Institutional predictability:** reduced risks of abrupt changes in the country's international orientation.
- **Strengthening of national sovereignty:** preservation of Brazil's ability to make independent decisions.
- **Enhanced international standing of Brazil:** positioning as a defender of peace, cooperation, and international stability.

## 4. Potential Impacts

**Positive impacts:**

- Reduced likelihood of Brazilian involvement in offensive wars.
- Greater stability in diplomatic relations with neighboring countries.
- Increased bilateral investments among South American countries.
- Expansion of regional trade and productive integration.
- Higher economic costs for countries considering initiating conflicts against regional partners.
- Increased attractiveness of South America for international investments.
- Potential relocation of companies, research centers, and international operations to the region.
- Greater diversification of Brazilian trade and financial relations.
- Reduced exposure of the country to disputes between major powers.
- Development of shared regional infrastructure.
- Greater international negotiating capacity through regional cooperation.
- Strengthening of Brazil's position as an independent diplomatic interlocutor.

**Negative impacts or risks:**

- Possibility of reduced diplomatic response capabilities in situations requiring firmer international positions.
- Risk that neutrality may be interpreted as inaction in the face of international aggression.
- Possibility of excessive economic dependence on neighboring countries.
- Risk of cross-investments being used as instruments of political influence.
- Difficulty maintaining long-term commitments amid changes in government in participating countries.
- Possibility of conflicts between participating countries harming shared investments.
- Risk of major powers exerting economic or diplomatic pressure to obtain Brazilian alignment.
- Need for additional investments in territorial defense and technological autonomy.
- Possibility that economic differences between participating countries may hinder investment reciprocity.
- Risk of regional integration progressing at different speeds depending on the economic and political conditions of each country.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

The proposal may be implemented through a combination of national legislation, international treaties, and multilateral agreements, respecting the constitutional powers of the branches of government.

The Brazilian initiative may establish national principles and mechanisms for the policy, while participation by other countries will depend on their own negotiations and international instruments.

The main challenges are:

- **Legal certainty:** establishing institutional commitments sufficiently durable to prevent changes in government from constantly altering Brazil's strategic orientation.

- **Constitutional compatibility:** assessing the need for constitutional amendments or supplementary regulations, particularly regarding powers related to declarations of war, national defense, and the conclusion of treaties.

- **Non-aggression treaty:** negotiating international instruments that establish reciprocal commitments to non-aggression while preserving the right to legitimate self-defense and existing international obligations.

- **Definition of neutrality:** establishing objective criteria to distinguish strategic neutrality, military non-alignment, and participation in international cooperation initiatives.

- **Balance among major powers:** developing criteria to assess the equivalence of conditions in commercial, financial, and technological agreements without compromising Brazil's freedom to negotiate.

- **Reciprocal investments:** establishing mechanisms to identify, finance, and monitor investments made among participating countries.

- **Financial governance:** defining the administrative and financial structure of a potential regional fund, including participation rules, auditing, management of risks, and transparency.

- **Investment protection:** establishing guarantees against arbitrary expropriations, contractual breaches, and unilateral changes that harm shared projects.

- **Economic asymmetry:** developing mechanisms that allow countries with different economic capabilities to participate, preventing the requirement for equivalent investments from hindering smaller nations' participation.

- **Conflict prevention:** establishing permanent consultation, mediation, and arbitration mechanisms to resolve disputes among participating countries.

- **Strategic autonomy:** reducing external dependencies in sectors such as energy, defense, technology, telecommunications, infrastructure, and industrial production.

- **International credibility:** demonstrating that the neutrality policy represents an effective institutional commitment rather than merely a temporary diplomatic orientation.

- **Regional accession:** negotiating the participation of other South American countries, preferably through multilateral mechanisms that allow gradual accession.

- **Compatibility with existing commitments:** assessing the relationship between the proposal and international agreements to which Brazil is already a party, including Mercosur, the UN, and the Inter-American Treaty of Reciprocal Assistance.

Implementation must take place gradually, beginning with the establishment of Brazil's commitments to non-aggression and strategic neutrality, followed by the negotiation of regional cooperation agreements and reciprocal investments.

The policy must preserve Brazil's ability to defend itself and maintain military deterrence. The commitment not to initiate offensive wars must not be interpreted as an obligation to reduce the country's ability to protect its territory, population, and legitimate interests.

It will also be necessary to distinguish strategic neutrality from legal neutrality in specific conflicts. Brazil must continue to respect international law, applicable decisions of international organizations, and its treaty obligations.

## 6. Open Questions

- Determine whether the commitment to non-aggression will be established through ordinary legislation, a constitutional amendment, or a combination of national legislation and an international treaty.
- Determine whether Brazil's commitment will be limited to South America or apply to all countries worldwide.
- Establish objective criteria for characterizing an offensive war.
- Define the conditions for Brazilian participation in international military operations, peacekeeping missions, and collective defense initiatives.
- Assess the possibility of establishing a regional non-aggression treaty among South American countries.
- Determine whether cross-investments must necessarily have equivalent values or may use economic compensation mechanisms.
- Define the percentage of GDP or minimum amounts that each country may allocate to reciprocal investments.
- Establish priority sectors for investment allocation.
- Determine whether an independent regional fund will be created to manage the resources.
- Establish mechanisms to protect investments against political and diplomatic changes.
- Define the accession criteria for new countries to join the investment program.
- Establish mediation and arbitration mechanisms for disputes among participating countries.
- Define which countries and organizations will be considered major powers for the purposes of applying the principles of strategic neutrality.
- Establish criteria for assessing the equivalence of conditions in commercial, financial, and technological agreements.
- Determine how to handle situations in which major powers impose economic sanctions or demand specific diplomatic positions.
- Assess the need to establish limits on technological, financial, and military dependencies on any power.
- Determine whether the policy should be formally shared with other South American countries.
- Establish indicators to assess the progress of economic integration, investment reciprocity, and Brazil's exposure to international conflicts.
- Define mechanisms for periodic review of the policy while preserving its fundamental principles.

## 7. References

- **Federal Constitution of 1988:** particularly Article 4, which establishes the principles governing Brazilian international relations, including national independence, the defense of peace, peaceful settlement of disputes, non-intervention, and cooperation among peoples.
- **United Nations Charter:** establishes principles related to the prohibition of the use of force in international relations, the peaceful settlement of disputes, and the right to legitimate self-defense.
- **Treaty of Asunción (1991):** instrument establishing Mercosur, setting out objectives for regional economic integration.
- **Treaty of Brasília (2008):** founding instrument of the Union of South American Nations (UNASUR), which established mechanisms for regional integration and cooperation.
- **Inter-American Treaty of Reciprocal Assistance (1947):** instrument for reciprocal assistance and collective defense within the inter-American framework.
- **United Nations (UN):** institutional reference for the principles of peace, international security, and peaceful settlement of disputes.
- **Mercosur:** regional economic integration framework, with mechanisms for trade and institutional cooperation.
- **Development Bank of Latin America and the Caribbean (CAF):** multilateral financial institution focused on financing development and regional integration.
- **New Development Bank (NDB):** multilateral financial institution established by BRICS countries, serving as a reference for financing infrastructure and development.
- **Inter-American Development Bank (IDB):** multilateral institution financing economic and social development projects in Latin America and the Caribbean.

**Core concept of the proposal:** transform South America into a region of peace, geopolitical neutrality, and shared economic prosperity, capable of maintaining balanced relations with all major powers and positioning itself internationally as a safe environment for people, businesses, and investments, even during periods of global instability.
