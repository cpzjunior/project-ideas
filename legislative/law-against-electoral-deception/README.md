# Law Against Electoral Deception

Status: Idea

## 1. Idea

Create a law establishing accountability mechanisms for elected candidates who fail to fulfill the main commitments formally made during their election campaigns.

Every candidate must independently define and officially register the main commitments they intend to fulfill if elected. These commitments must be specific, objective, and auditable, allowing their fulfillment to be assessed at a later date.

The proposal establishes the following mechanisms:

- Official commitments: Each candidate will be responsible for selecting and registering their own main campaign commitments.
- Audit criteria: Commitments must have objective and verifiable criteria that allow their fulfillment to be assessed.
- Oversight: The Superior Electoral Court (TSE) will be responsible for evaluating whether registered commitments have been fulfilled.
- Justifications: Elected officials may provide justifications for unfulfilled commitments, including external circumstances, legal restrictions, budgetary limitations, or unforeseen events.
- Penalties: Unjustified failure to fulfill registered commitments may result in a financial penalty and disqualification from running in one subsequent election.
- Right to a defense: All proceedings must respect the constitutional guarantees of due process, including the right to a full defense and adversarial proceedings.

## 2. Motivation

Election campaigns frequently include promises that influence voters' decisions but may never be fulfilled. Currently, there is no general mechanism specifically designed to hold elected officials accountable for failing to fulfill their main campaign commitments.

The proposal aims to establish a clearer relationship between campaign promises and the responsibilities assumed by elected candidates, encouraging greater transparency, accountability, and seriousness in election campaigns.

Another important objective is to provide greater predictability for voters and the market. The formal registration of main commitments allows citizens, companies, and investors to understand in advance the priorities and policy directions a candidate intends to pursue if elected. This contributes to better-informed decisions and reduces uncertainty about the future direction of public administration.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Electoral accountability: Establish consequences for the unjustified failure to fulfill formally registered commitments.
- Transparency: Make candidates' main commitments publicly accessible and verifiable.
- More responsible campaigns: Encourage realistic, objective, and measurable proposals.
- Predictability: Provide greater clarity about the future government's priorities, benefiting voters, companies, and investors.
- Voter awareness: Provide concrete information that allows citizens to evaluate elected officials' performance.
- Public trust: Strengthen accountability between elected representatives and the public.

## 4. Potential Impacts

- Greater responsibility in the development of electoral platforms and campaign proposals.
- Increased transparency regarding the fulfillment of campaign commitments.
- Greater political and economic predictability, allowing citizens, companies, and investors to take registered commitments into account in their decision-making processes.
- Strengthening of the culture of accountability in public administration.
- Potential reduction in unrealistic or misleading campaign promises.
- Expansion of the oversight responsibilities of the Electoral Justice System.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

- Legal framework: Define the legal basis for imposing penalties on elected candidates who fail to fulfill registered commitments.
- Commitment assessment: Establish objective criteria for evaluating different types of promises, including those related to public policies and long-term initiatives.
- External circumstances: Distinguish unjustified failures from situations beyond the elected official's control.
- Institutional oversight: Define the responsibilities, procedures, and resources required for the TSE to conduct these assessments.
- Constitutional compatibility: Ensure that penalties and ineligibility rules comply with the Constitution and electoral legislation.
- Due process: Ensure the right to a defense and the possibility of challenging decisions.

## 6. Open Questions

- How many commitments should each candidate be required to register?
- Should commitments be evaluated only at the end of the term or periodically?
- How should partially fulfilled commitments be assessed?
- How should shared responsibilities among members of the Executive and Legislative branches be evaluated?
- How should financial penalties be calculated?
- What procedures should govern the assessment of commitments and the imposition of penalties?
