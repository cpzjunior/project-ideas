# Long-Term Party Planning Law

## 1. Idea

Create a law requiring all political parties to develop and maintain strategic government plans with a 15-year horizon, explicitly outlining their main priorities, guidelines, objectives, targets, and measurable indicators for the country.

The plans must present a long-term vision for the main areas of government activity, allowing the public to know in advance the directions each party intends to pursue if it comes to power.

The proposal establishes the following mechanisms:

- Long-term party plans: each party must develop and publish a strategic plan with a 15-year horizon, covering its main political, economic, and social priorities and guidelines.
- Measurable targets and indicators: the plans must establish objective targets and quantitative or qualitative indicators that make it possible to monitor and assess progress toward the commitments made.
- Formal acknowledgment by party members: all party members, without exception, must formally declare that they are aware of and acknowledge the current party plan. This acknowledgment does not imply full agreement with all guidelines or proposals, nor does it prevent internal disagreements.
- Consequences for non-compliance: each party must establish in its bylaws the consequences applicable to members who fail to comply with formally assumed commitments or obligations, while respecting the rules governing participation and internal dissent.
- Transparency: the plans must be public and accessible, allowing voters, companies, and investors to understand each party's long-term proposals.
- Democratic continuity: the plans will allow the public to understand the parties' long-term projects and assess, at each election, whether they wish to continue the political direction adopted by the government or choose representatives aligned with other proposals.
- Periodic reviews: the plans must be reviewed annually, allowing adjustments to party strategies, targets, and priorities.
- Version history: all previous versions of the plans must remain archived and publicly accessible, ensuring that changes can be tracked and preventing previous commitments from simply being deleted or replaced without a record.
- Dialogue between parties: the plans will serve as a reference for negotiations and consensus-building among different parties, enabling transparent programmatic agreements without relying on political negotiations based exclusively on private interests or the allocation of government positions.

## 2. Motivation

The lack of long-term political planning and frequent changes in direction between governments make it difficult to maintain public policies and increase uncertainty for the public and the market.

Currently, voters are primarily familiar with proposals presented for a specific term of office, without necessarily having a clear understanding of the parties' long-term vision or the direction they intend to take the country.

The proposal seeks to establish a culture of strategic planning within political parties, ensuring that each organization has a clear and public vision for the country over the next 15 years. The inclusion of measurable targets and indicators makes it possible to monitor the progress of commitments and assess the results achieved.

Another important objective is to provide greater political, economic, and institutional predictability. With plans established in advance, voters, companies, and investors will be able to understand the potential direction of a future government even before elections take place.

The initiative also seeks to reduce electoral deception, since parties will have formalized and trackable programmatic commitments, while encouraging dialogue among different political forces. Instead of relying on backroom deals and ad hoc negotiations to make governments viable, parties will be able to build agreements based on their public proposals and guidelines.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Predictability: greater clarity regarding the political and economic directions each party intends to pursue in the long term.
- Strategic planning: encouragement of public policy development with a vision that extends beyond a single term of office.
- Verifiable targets: the ability to monitor the progress of party commitments through objective indicators.
- Transparency: allowing the public to understand and compare the national development plans presented by political parties.
- Administrative continuity: facilitating the continuation of public policies and strategic projects across different governments.
- Party accountability: establishing formal commitments and obligations between parties and their members, without requiring full agreement with all proposals.
- Reduction of electoral deception: narrowing the gap between commitments presented by political parties and the actions of their elected representatives.
- Institutional dialogue: encouraging programmatic agreements and consensus among parties based on public proposals.
- Economic stability: providing greater political and economic predictability for companies and investors making medium- and long-term decisions.
- Democratic participation: allowing voters to understand different national development projects and consider their long-term guidelines when choosing their representatives.

## 4. Potential Impacts

- Public: greater predictability regarding the country's direction and more information to support electoral decisions.
- Political parties: the need to develop consistent strategic plans, define measurable targets, and maintain clear programmatic guidelines.
- Government: greater potential for continuity in public policies and structural projects, as well as tools for assessing results.
- Market: greater political and economic predictability, supporting long-term investment decisions.
- Political system: encouragement of programmatic agreements between parties, reducing dependence on exclusively ad hoc political negotiations.
- Democracy: strengthening electoral choices based on national development projects and long-term guidelines, while preserving room for internal disagreements.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

- Plan definition: establish minimum requirements for developing party plans, ensuring that they are objective, comprehensive, and verifiable.
- Targets and indicators: define criteria to ensure that commitments can be monitored, measured, and assessed over time.
- Acknowledgment by party members: define procedures to formally record awareness and acknowledgment of the plan by all members, without requiring full agreement.
- Accountability: establish rules requiring party bylaws to provide for consequences for failure to comply with assumed obligations.
- Party autonomy: ensure that the mandatory plans are compatible with the autonomy of political parties guaranteed by the Federal Constitution.
- Periodic updates: ensure that annual reviews allow proposals to evolve without compromising continuity and predictability.
- Historical preservation: keep all previous versions of the plans archived and available for public consultation.
- Oversight: define how the development, publication, review, measurement of results, and maintenance of the plans' version history will be monitored.
- Democratic freedom: preserve internal debate, differences of opinion, and the possibility of evolving party proposals.
- Planning horizon: structure 15-year plans that can span different terms of office and changes in government.

## 6. Open Questions

- How should the minimum required content of the plans be structured?
- What criteria should guide the definition and assessment of targets and indicators?
- Which body will be responsible for overseeing the development, publication, and updating of the plans?
- How can party obligations be reconciled with the autonomy of elected representatives in the exercise of their mandates?
- What consequences may be provided for in party bylaws for failure to meet commitments while preserving the right to internal dissent?
