# Anti-Overpricing Act

## 1. Concept

Establish legislation that sets objective criteria to prevent overpricing in purchases and contracts entered into by public administration, defining price and quantity limits and requiring technical and economic justifications for acquisitions that cannot follow the general parameters.

The proposal complements existing public procurement and contract rules, particularly Law No. 14,133/2021, turning price research and procurement planning into effective mechanisms for controlling public spending.

The legislation will be based on three main rules:

1. Price limits

Products available on the domestic or international market may not be purchased at prices exceeding 10% of their respective reference market prices.

The limit must apply to the final contract price, taking into account all associated costs, such as freight, taxes, installation, warranty, and other relevant commercial terms.

To determine the reference price, effectively equivalent products must be used, considering comparable technical specifications, quality, and supply conditions.

Specific or specialized items that have no functional equivalents among ordinary products available on the market may be purchased without applying the percentage cap. However, their procurement will require a technical and economic justification demonstrating:

- Why the ordinary products available do not meet the needs of the public administration.
    
- Why the specific requirements are necessary.
    
- How the economic reasonableness of the contracted price was determined.
    

A product may not be classified as specific based on dispensable characteristics or technical requirements artificially created to circumvent the price limit.

2. Justification of quantities

Every purchase must include a formal justification for the requested quantity, demonstrating its necessity and compatibility with the corresponding project, activity, or administrative purpose.

The justification must include the calculation methodology used to determine the quantity, taking into account factors such as:

- Expected demand.
    
- Number of beneficiaries or users.
    
- Utilization capacity.
    
- Available inventory.
    
- Project operational requirements.
    

This requirement will apply to all purchases, regardless of the unit price or product classification.

3. Fines for overpricing and allocation of proceeds

Public officials, suppliers, and other parties proven to be involved in overpricing, fraud, or the deliberate manipulation of prices and quantities must be held liable for the applicable legal and financial consequences.

Punitive fines imposed as a result of such conduct must follow the following principle:

- The fine will be equivalent to five times the amount of the illicit economic benefit obtained, when proven and quantifiable.
    
- The penalty will apply independently of the obligation to fully compensate for the damage caused to public funds.
    
- Liability may extend to the individuals and legal entities involved, subject to the individual assessment of each party's conduct and proven participation.
    

Mandatory allocation of proceeds:

- 100% of the amounts collected from these fines will be allocated to the Federal Government's Treasury.
    
- The funds will be exclusively allocated to paying down federal public debt, including the amortization and settlement of obligations.
    
- The amounts must be accounted for separately, ensuring transparency regarding the funds collected and effectively applied toward reducing public debt.
    
- The proceeds may not be used to finance administrative or ordinary government expenses.
    

The fine will be punitive in nature and will not replace the reimbursement of amounts diverted or improperly paid by the public administration.

## 2. Rationale

Brazilian legislation already requires public contracts to be preceded by price research and estimated values to be consistent with those charged in the market.

However, this proposal seeks to go beyond that requirement by establishing an objective percentage limit for purchases, reducing the scope for contracts involving excessive prices.

The absence of a general percentage limit allows different public agencies to adopt different criteria for assessing price acceptability, making standardization and oversight more difficult.

Furthermore, ordinary products can show significant price differences across public contracts, even when their specifications and commercial terms are equivalent.

Another problem is the possibility of purchasing quantities that exceed the public administration's actual needs. Even when the unit price is appropriate, excessive purchases can lead to wasted resources, unnecessary inventory, and additional storage and maintenance costs.

The proposal also seeks to prevent unnecessary technical specifications from being used to justify high prices or unjustified quantities from being included in projects and contracts.

For specialized items, it recognizes that directly comparable commercial references are not always available. In such cases, the requirement for a technical and economic justification preserves the public administration's ability to procure these items without creating an unrestricted exception.

In addition to preventing and recovering misappropriated public funds, punitive fines will help increase Federal Government revenue. Allocating these funds exclusively to public debt payments will turn the punishment of unlawful practices into an additional source of national debt reduction.

## 3. Expected benefits

- Reduced overpricing: objective limits on the amounts paid by the public administration.
    
- Quantity control: requirement for justifications demonstrating the need for each purchase.
    
- Standardization: application of uniform criteria for assessing prices and quantities.
    
- Public resource savings: reduction of excessive spending and waste of public funds.
    
- Transparency: greater clarity regarding the criteria used to determine contracted prices and quantities.
    
- Easier oversight: simpler identification of contracts involving prices or quantities inconsistent with stated needs.
    
- Prevention of specification manipulation: fewer opportunities to artificially justify high prices.
    
- Encouragement of competition: incentives to use ordinary products and suppliers offering competitive prices.
    
- Administrative efficiency: better use of resources collected through taxes.
    
- Increased Federal Government revenue: generation of additional revenue through punitive fines imposed on those responsible.
    
- Public debt reduction: full allocation of collected fines to the payment of federal public debt, contributing to its amortization.
    

## 4. Potential impacts

Positive impacts:

- Savings in public funds when purchasing ordinary products.
    
- Reduction in purchases involving quantities greater than actual needs.
    
- Greater predictability for suppliers interested in participating in public procurement processes.
    
- Stronger competition among suppliers.
    
- Easier identification of contracts involving prices or quantities inconsistent with the market and the projects submitted.
    
- Greater comparability among purchases made by different public agencies and entities.
    
- Fewer opportunities for corruption involving the procurement of goods and products.
    
- Increased federal revenue through the fines imposed.
    
- Direct contribution to the payment and amortization of federal public debt.
    

Negative impacts or risks:

- Potential difficulties in procuring products with significant regional price variations.
    
- Risk of using inappropriate or outdated market references.
    
- Possibility of suppliers artificially manipulating reference prices.
    
- Risk of technical specifications being manipulated to classify ordinary products as specialized.
    
- Possibility of suppliers choosing not to participate in public procurement processes when the limit fails to adequately account for specific supply conditions.
    
- Need to adapt public procurement systems to automate price comparisons and record quantity justifications.
    

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

The proposal may be implemented as complementary legislation to existing rules governing public procurement and administrative contracts, while respecting the powers of different levels of government and the legal framework applicable to state-owned enterprises.

The main challenges are:

- Determining market prices: establishing a standardized methodology for calculating reference prices, preferably using the median of at least three independent and verifiable sources, when available.
    
- Product comparability: considering technical specifications, quality, quantity, location, freight, taxes, warranty, installation, support, payment terms, and delivery deadlines.
    
- International references: allowing the use of international prices when adequate domestic references are unavailable, taking into account exchange rates, taxes, logistics costs, and import conditions.
    
- Classification of specific items: establishing objective criteria to demonstrate that a given product has no functional equivalent among ordinary products available on the market.
    
- Preventing artificial exceptions: requiring technical and economic justifications for classifying items as specific, preventing dispensable characteristics from being used to circumvent the percentage limit.
    
- Justification of quantities: requiring documentation demonstrating how the requested quantity was calculated and why it is necessary, enabling oversight bodies to identify excessive purchases or quantities inconsistent with the stated purpose.
    
- Oversight: enabling oversight bodies to verify reference prices, submitted justifications, and the compatibility of purchased quantities.
    
- Accountability: establishing consequences for public officials and suppliers involved in fraud, manipulation of references, or fraudulent justifications, distinguishing technical irregularities from deliberate misconduct.
    
- Public procurement systems: adapting procurement platforms to record reference prices, automatically calculate limits, and store technical and quantitative justifications.
    
- Allocation of fines: establishing accounting and financial mechanisms to ensure that all amounts collected are transferred toward payment of federal public debt.
    

The 10% rule must operate as a maximum limit on the final contracted price, rather than merely as a criterion for disqualifying bids.

Any contracts exceeding this limit may only be entered into under exceptions expressly provided for by law, subject to documented technical and economic justification.

The law must establish its application to the Federal Government, states, the Federal District, and municipalities, while respecting their legislative powers and the particularities of their respective public procurement frameworks.

The fines provided for must follow the federal allocation established in this proposal, with proceeds transferred to the Federal Government and used exclusively for payment of federal public debt.

## 6. Open issues

- Determine whether the 10% limit will be applied to the median of market prices or to another statistical methodology.
    
- Determine the minimum number of independent sources required to calculate the reference price.
    
- Establish the maximum validity period for price research.
    
- Define how products subject to significant price fluctuations will be treated.
    
- Establish criteria for using international references.
    
- Define objective criteria for identifying specific products and their functional equivalents.
    
- Establish which authorities may approve the procurement of specific items.
    
- Define procedures for demonstrating the economic reasonableness of exceptional contracts.
    
- Establish criteria for assessing whether requested quantities are appropriate for projects and administrative needs.
    
- Establish administrative consequences for public officials and suppliers involved in fraud or manipulation.
    
- Assess mechanisms for revising reference prices when it is demonstrated that available values do not reflect actual procurement conditions.
    
- Define mechanisms for calculating, collecting, accounting for, and fully transferring fines toward payment of federal public debt.
