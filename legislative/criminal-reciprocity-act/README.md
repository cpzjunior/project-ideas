# Criminal Reciprocity Act

## 1. Idea

Create legislation establishing objective criteria for the recognition and enforcement, in Brazil, of criminal convictions imposed by foreign courts, allowing Brazilians convicted abroad to serve their sentences in Brazilian territory and regulating the treatment of foreign nationals residing in Brazil who have been convicted by other countries.

The proposal seeks to establish a system of international reciprocity in which criminal convictions do not cease to have effect because of a change of country, but their enforcement must fully comply with the Federal Constitution and the Brazilian legal system.

The legislation shall complement existing international legal cooperation rules, especially Law No. 13,445/2017 (Migration Law), establishing more objective, predictable, and standardized procedures.

The legislation shall be based on six main rules:

### 1. Constitutional compatibility of convictions

The recognition and enforcement of any foreign criminal conviction in Brazilian territory shall be conditional upon the substantive compatibility of the conviction and the sentence imposed with the Federal Constitution.

No conviction may produce enforceable effects in Brazil when its application would violate fundamental rights and guarantees or constitutional principles governing criminal law.

The analysis shall specifically consider:

- Human dignity.
- Equality before the law.
- Criminal legality.
- Due process of law.
- Adversarial proceedings and full defense rights.
- Presumption of innocence.
- Individualization of punishment.
- Prohibition of the death penalty, except for the constitutional exception of declared war.
- Prohibition of life imprisonment.
- Prohibition of forced labor, banishment, and cruel punishments.
- Freedom of conscience, belief, and religion.
- Protection against discrimination.

Compatibility shall be assessed regardless of the religious, political, ideological, or cultural nature of the foreign legal system.

Accordingly, convictions based on religious rules or local customs shall also be subject to the same constitutional criteria applicable to any other legal system.

The analysis shall consider the actual effects of the conviction and not merely the foreign legislation used as its legal basis.

### 2. Equivalence and adjustment of sentences

When a foreign conviction is deemed compatible with the Federal Constitution and with the other legal requirements for recognition, its enforcement in Brazil shall comply with the types of penalties permitted under Brazilian law.

The adjustment shall consider:

- Correspondence between the convicted conduct and Brazilian criminal offenses.
- Nature of the offense.
- Actual severity of the crime.
- Type of sentence originally imposed.
- Time already served abroad.
- Maximum and minimum limits established by Brazilian law.
- Rights and benefits provided for under Brazilian criminal enforcement legislation.

The adjustment may not result in an aggravation of the convicted person's penal situation in relation to the limits applicable to recognition of the foreign judgment.

When there is no sufficient correspondence between the criminal offenses or when the conviction depends exclusively on conduct that does not constitute a criminal offense in Brazil, there shall be no automatic conversion into a Brazilian crime.

### 3. Criteria for serving sentences in Brazil

The determination of the country responsible for enforcing the sentence shall primarily consider the convicted person's national and family ties.

Transfer to Brazil shall follow the criteria below:

Brazilian nationals by birth:

- They shall have priority to serve their sentences in Brazil.
- Transfer shall be considered the general rule, subject to applicable constitutional, legal, and international requirements.

Naturalized Brazilian nationals:

- They shall receive the same legal treatment as Brazilian nationals by birth regarding transfer and criminal sentence enforcement.
- Naturalization alone may not constitute grounds for differential penal treatment.

Foreign nationals with family ties in Brazil:

- They may serve their sentences in Brazil when they have a spouse, partner, children, dependents, or other relevant family ties established in Brazilian territory.
- Transfer shall consider the family's place of residence, the existence of economic or emotional dependency, and opportunities for social reintegration.
- The existence of family ties shall be considered a priority criterion, provided that the legal and international requirements are met.

Transfer shall be subject to the applicable international cooperation mechanisms and necessary safeguards, including the convicted person's consent when required.

### 4. Treatment of foreign nationals without relevant ties to Brazil

Foreign nationals convicted abroad who do not hold Brazilian nationality or have relevant family ties in Brazilian territory shall, as a general rule, serve their sentences in the country responsible for the conviction.

After serving their sentences, procedures for return to their country of origin or residence may be applied, in accordance with international agreements and applicable migration legislation.

Foreign nationals residing in Brazil but without relevant family ties may have their situations assessed individually, taking into consideration:

- Length of residence in Brazil.
- Existence of social and professional ties.
- Immigration compliance history.
- Possibility of social reintegration.
- Nature of the offense.
- Requirements of applicable international agreements.

Expulsion shall comply with legal and constitutional restrictions and may not be used to circumvent fundamental guarantees or improperly replace criminal sentence enforcement.

### 5. Simplification of recognition and criminal sentence enforcement

The legislation shall establish a standardized procedure for recognizing foreign convictions, reducing the need for repetitive judicial assessments.

The procedure shall include:

- Verification that the conviction is final.
- Confirmation of the convicted person's identity.
- Verification of document authenticity.
- Analysis of constitutional compatibility.
- Verification of criminal offense correspondence, when required.
- Determination of the sentence enforcement method.
- Calculation of the sentence period already served.
- Verification of requirements for international transfer.

The procedure shall allow the use of objective criteria and computerized systems for document verification and sentence calculations.

Judicial review shall remain available, particularly in cases involving disputes over constitutional compatibility, the legality of the conviction, the convicted person's identity, or violations of fundamental rights.

Simplification may not eliminate the constitutional jurisdiction of the Superior Court of Justice (STJ) to recognize foreign judgments in cases where such recognition is required.

### 6. International reciprocity

Brazil shall encourage bilateral and multilateral agreements to facilitate the transfer of convicted persons and the reciprocal recognition of criminal judgments.

The agreements shall establish:

- Objective criteria for the transfer of convicted persons.
- Rules for the recognition of foreign judgments.
- Procedures for sentence adjustment.
- Sharing of procedural information.
- Recognition of sentence periods served abroad.
- Guarantees of respect for fundamental rights.
- Procedures to prevent double enforcement of sentences.

The principle of reciprocity may not justify the recognition of convictions that are incompatible with the Federal Constitution.

## 2. Motivation

Brazilian legislation already provides mechanisms for international legal cooperation involving the transfer of convicted persons and the enforcement of foreign judgments.

However, the proposal seeks to establish broader and more standardized criteria for determining when a foreign conviction may produce enforceable effects in Brazil and which individuals should have priority to serve their sentences in Brazilian territory.

One of the main problems the proposal seeks to address is the difference between countries' legal systems.

Some countries have criminal laws based on religious principles, local customs, or legal provisions that are incompatible with fundamental rights and guarantees recognized by the Brazilian Constitution.

Brazil should not enforce sentences that it could not impose within its own territory, regardless of the foreign legislation on which the conviction was based.

At the same time, the incompatibility of a particular type of punishment should not necessarily result in impunity when the conduct also constitutes a crime in Brazil and there is a legal possibility of adjusting the sentence.

Another issue is the lack of sufficiently standardized criteria for determining which country is responsible for enforcing the sentence.

Brazilians convicted abroad may have family and social ties in Brazil that are much more significant than those in the country responsible for the conviction. Similarly, foreign nationals convicted abroad may have established family ties in Brazilian territory.

Transferring criminal sentence enforcement to the country with which the convicted person has relevant ties may facilitate social reintegration, the maintenance of family relationships, and supervision during sentence enforcement.

The proposal also seeks to reduce the burden on the Judiciary by standardizing procedures, using objective criteria, and automating document verification and criminal sentence calculations.

Judicial review shall remain focused on issues that genuinely require legal interpretation, particularly constitutional compatibility and the protection of fundamental rights.

Finally, international reciprocity will make it possible to establish a more predictable criminal cooperation policy, preventing differences between national legal systems from resulting in either impunity or the enforcement of sentences incompatible with the Brazilian legal system.

## 3. Expected benefits

- Constitutional protection: prevention of the enforcement in Brazil of sentences incompatible with the Federal Constitution.
- Legal certainty: establishment of objective criteria for the recognition of foreign convictions.
- Combating impunity: possibility of enforcing foreign convictions in Brazil when legally recognized.
- Criminal sentence equivalence: adjustment of foreign sentences to the types of penalties permitted in Brazil.
- Protection of Brazilian nationals: possibility of serving sentences in their country of nationality.
- Family preservation: consideration of family ties when determining where a sentence will be served.
- Social reintegration: facilitation of a return to family and social life during and after sentence enforcement.
- International reciprocity: strengthening of criminal cooperation mechanisms between countries.
- Reduced judicial workload: fewer repetitive assessments through standardized procedures.
- Predictability: more consistent treatment of equivalent foreign convictions.
- Administrative efficiency: use of computerized systems for document verification and sentence calculations.
- Legal sovereignty: preservation of Brazil's autonomy in determining which foreign convictions may be enforced within its territory.

## 4. Potential impacts

Positive impacts:

- Greater protection of the fundamental rights of individuals convicted abroad.
- Reduced possibility of enforcing cruel, discriminatory, or constitutionally incompatible sentences.
- Fewer cases of impunity resulting from the absence of adequate criminal transfer mechanisms.
- Greater ease for Brazilians to serve sentences in Brazilian territory.
- Preservation of family ties for individuals convicted abroad.
- Greater predictability in international legal cooperation.
- Reduced need for repetitive judicial assessments.
- Better utilization of national administrative and prison infrastructure.
- Greater standardization of decisions concerning the recognition of foreign judgments.
- Strengthening of Brazil's position in international criminal cooperation negotiations.

Negative impacts or risks:

- Possible increase in Brazil's prison population due to the transfer of convicted persons from abroad.
- Need to adapt administrative and prison infrastructure.
- Potential conflicts between Brazil and countries that do not accept the adjustment of their sentences.
- Difficulty comparing criminal offenses and types of punishment across different legal systems.
- Risk of disagreements in the interpretation of constitutional compatibility.
- Need to obtain and translate foreign procedural documents.
- Possible initial increase in administrative and judicial workloads during implementation.
- Dependence on international agreements and cooperation from the countries involved.

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

The proposal may be implemented through amendments to Law No. 13,445/2017, the Code of Criminal Procedure, and other legislation related to international legal cooperation and criminal sentence enforcement.

The main challenges are:

- Constitutional compatibility: establishing objective criteria to identify convictions and sentences incompatible with the Federal Constitution, without discrimination based on the religious, political, or cultural origin of the foreign legislation.
- Criminal offense correspondence: determining when conduct for which an individual was convicted abroad sufficiently corresponds to a criminal offense under Brazilian law.
- Sentence adjustment: establishing a methodology for converting foreign sentences into forms permitted under Brazilian law while respecting constitutional and legal limits.
- Judicial recognition: preserving the jurisdiction of the STJ to recognize foreign judgments, pursuant to Article 105, item I, subitem "i", of the Federal Constitution.
- International transfer: ensuring compatibility between the proposal and applicable treaties, agreements, and reciprocity mechanisms.
- Consent: defining the circumstances in which transfer will require the convicted person's consent, in accordance with applicable international instruments.
- Family criteria: establishing which family ties shall be considered relevant and which documents may be used to prove them.
- Equality among Brazilian nationals: ensuring equivalent treatment for Brazilian nationals by birth and naturalized Brazilian nationals, respecting constitutional exceptions.
- Immigration protection: ensuring that expulsion rules are compatible with the restrictions established by the Migration Law and the Constitution.
- Prison capacity: assessing the impact of transfers on the prison population and criminal sentence enforcement costs.
- Computerized systems: developing mechanisms for consultation, document validation, sentence calculations, and transfer monitoring.
- International cooperation: negotiating instruments that allow the transfer of convicted persons even when no specific bilateral agreement exists, subject to applicable legal reciprocity requirements.

The legislation shall establish that constitutional compatibility is an indispensable requirement for the recognition and enforcement of foreign convictions in Brazil.

The analysis shall not be limited to the existence of an international treaty or the formal acceptance of foreign legislation by the country of origin.

The possible incompatibility of a type of punishment shall not automatically prevent recognition of the entire conviction when there is a legal possibility of separating or adjusting the sentence.

Transfer may also not result in double enforcement, undue aggravation of the sentence, or the removal of rights guaranteed under Brazilian law.

The law shall apply in compliance with the powers of the Federal Government, especially in matters of criminal law, criminal procedure, immigration, and international relations.

## 6. Open points

- Determine whether recognition of foreign convictions shall be mandatory whenever the legal requirements are met or whether additional grounds for judicial assessment will be permitted.
- Establish objective criteria for assessing the constitutional compatibility of foreign convictions.
- Determine whether constitutional analysis shall cover all foreign legislation applied or only the legal grounds and actual effects of the conviction.
- Establish a methodology for adjusting foreign sentences to the types of penalties provided for under Brazilian law.
- Determine how offenses with different classifications or constituent elements across countries shall be handled.
- Define the priority criteria among Brazilian nationals by birth, naturalized Brazilian nationals, and foreign nationals with family ties in Brazil, while respecting constitutional equality.
- Establish which family ties shall be considered sufficient to justify transfer.
- Determine how foreign nationals with habitual residence in Brazil but without family members in the country shall be treated.
- Establish procedures for transfer when the country responsible for the conviction has no specific treaty with Brazil.
- Define the circumstances in which the convicted person's consent may be waived, if legally permitted.
- Establish criteria for preventing transfer when it may undermine sentence enforcement or the convicted person's rights.
- Determine the treatment of convictions involving the death penalty, corporal punishment, mutilation, or other constitutionally prohibited sanctions.
- Establish how the sentence period served abroad shall be credited.
- Define procedures for reviewing and challenging recognition decisions.
- Establish criteria for the expulsion of foreign nationals after serving their sentences, in compliance with legal restrictions.
- Assess the financial and prison-related impacts of international transfers.
- Determine which procedures may be automated and which must remain subject to mandatory judicial review.
