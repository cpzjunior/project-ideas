# Commissioned Positions Rationalization Act

## 1. Idea

Create national legislation establishing objective limits on the number of commissioned positions within the Brazilian public administration, using the population served as the primary criterion for determining their size.

The proposal aims to reduce the size of the State's political and administrative structure, decrease permanent personnel expenses, and prevent the excessive use of freely appointed positions across the three branches of government.

The central principle is that the number of commissioned positions must be proportional to the population served by the public entity, rather than the size of its permanent civil service workforce, available budget, or revenue-generating capacity.

The legislation will be based on seven main rules:

### 1. Population-based limits on commissioned positions

Establish a maximum number of commissioned positions for each municipality, calculated exclusively based on its population.

The proposed formula will be:

C = maximum of 10 and the result of P/1,000 rounded up to the next whole number.

Where:

- C = maximum number of commissioned positions.
- P = official population of the municipality.

Examples:

| Population | Position limit |
|---|---:|
| Up to 10,000 inhabitants | 10 |
| 25,000 inhabitants | 25 |
| 50,000 inhabitants | 50 |
| 100,000 inhabitants | 100 |
| 250,000 inhabitants | 250 |
| 500,000 inhabitants | 500 |
| 1 million inhabitants | 1,000 |
| 5 million inhabitants | 5,000 |
| 12 million inhabitants | 12,000 |

The maximum number must account for the entire municipal administration, including:

- City Hall.
- Municipal departments.
- Municipal Chamber.
- Public authorities.
- Public foundations.
- Other entities belonging to the municipal public administration.

The limit will be global for each municipality, preventing each government body or branch from using an independent quota and artificially multiplying the permitted number.

The internal distribution of positions will remain under the responsibility of each institution, subject to constitutional and legal limits.

The population used in the calculation will be the latest official estimate published by the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE).

No limit proportional to the number of permanent civil servants, municipal revenue, or available budget will be established.

### 2. Application to the three branches of government and all levels of government

The proposal intends to cover public administration at all levels of government:

- Federal Government.
- States.
- Federal District.
- Municipalities.

The limits must cover the Executive, Legislative, and Judicial branches, as well as constitutionally autonomous bodies, subject to their respective structures and powers.

The application must account for:

- Commissioned positions in direct public administration.
- Commissioned positions in indirect public administration.
- Advisory positions.
- Management and supervisory positions.
- Commissioned positions within courts and legislative bodies.
- Commissioned positions in public authorities and foundations.

For states, the Federal District, and the Federal Government, separate population-based limits must be established, taking into account the population covered by each entity and the size of its institutional structures.

The municipal formula of one position per 1,000 inhabitants will serve as the initial reference for municipalities and must not be automatically applied to other levels of government without specific assessment.

The proposal must prevent the transfer of positions between government bodies, entities, or administrative structures from being used to circumvent the limits.

### 3. Elimination of excess positions within 90 days

Compliance with the new limits must occur within a maximum period of 90 days after the legislation enters into force.

The deadline will be mandatory, with no possibility of administrative extension.

The following stages must be observed:

- Within 30 days: identify and disclose the number of existing commissioned positions and determine which exceed the limits.
- Within 60 days: carry out the dismissals and publish the acts eliminating excess positions.
- Within 90 days: complete full compliance with the legal limits across all administrative structures.

Positions exceeding the established limits must be abolished, and their exclusively commissioned occupants must be dismissed.

Excess positions may not be retained through generic justifications of administrative necessity.

The elimination of positions must produce an effective reduction in public expenditure, including salaries, position-related bonuses, and associated employment charges.

### 4. Protection of permanent civil servants and positions of trust

The proposal does not provide for the dismissal of permanent civil servants as a consequence of reducing commissioned positions.

Civil servants admitted through public examinations who occupy commissioned positions that are abolished must return to their original permanent positions, where applicable.

Positions of trust, intended exclusively for permanent civil servants, will be treated separately from commissioned positions.

The legislation must preserve the distinction between:

- Commissioned positions: positions subject to free appointment and dismissal.
- Positions of trust: management, supervisory, and advisory duties performed exclusively by permanent civil servants.

However, the artificial conversion of abolished commissioned positions into positions of trust, additional compensation, or other payment arrangements for the purpose of circumventing the established limits will be prohibited.

### 5. Prohibition on artificially recreating or replacing positions

After compliance with the limits, no government body may recreate excess commissioned positions through changes in nomenclature, organizational structure, or administrative affiliation.

The prohibition must cover:

- Creation of equivalent positions under different names.
- Artificial transfer of positions to public authorities and foundations.
- Creation of parallel advisory structures.
- Fragmentation of positions across different administrative units.
- Use of additional compensation to reproduce benefits associated with abolished positions.
- Indirect hiring used exclusively to maintain freely appointed structures.

The creation of new commissioned positions will only be permitted when the population-based limits and all other constitutional and legal requirements are respected.

The legislation must also require each commissioned position to have actual management, supervisory, or advisory duties, in accordance with Article 37, item V, of the Federal Constitution.

### 6. Transparency and oversight

All public bodies must maintain up-to-date information on their commissioned positions in an open format accessible to society.

Disclosure must include:

- Total number of authorized positions.
- Number of occupied positions.
- Number of vacant positions.
- Name and employee identification of each occupant.
- Individual compensation.
- Assigned department.
- Authority responsible for the appointment.
- Description of duties.
- Employment relationship of the occupant.
- Monthly and annual costs of the commissioned structure.

Oversight bodies must monitor compliance with the limits and identify any attempts to circumvent the legislation.

Oversight must involve, according to their respective powers:

- Federal Court of Accounts (TCU).
- State and municipal Courts of Accounts.
- Internal audit offices.
- Office of the Comptroller General (CGU).
- Public Prosecutor's Office.
- Other internal and external oversight bodies.

Failure to comply with the limits must trigger an investigation and the application of appropriate administrative and legal consequences.

### 7. Accountability and nationwide implementation

The legislation must establish accountability mechanisms for authorities who maintain, recreate, or authorize positions in violation of the established limits.

Accountability must take into consideration:

- Deliberate maintenance of excess positions.
- Creation of structures intended to circumvent the limits.
- Failure to implement mandatory compliance.
- Submission of false information to oversight bodies.
- Expenditure on irregular commissioned structures.

Penalties must comply with due process, the right to a full defense, and the specific requirements of each applicable accountability framework.

Nationwide implementation must respect the administrative and financial autonomy of government entities and branches.

To ensure mandatory application across all levels of government and the three branches, the proposal must assess the need for a constitutional amendment establishing general limits, supplemented by implementing legislation.

## 2. Rationale

Brazilian public administration maintains a significant number of commissioned positions, used for management, supervisory, and advisory functions.

Although these positions serve a legitimate purpose, their expansion can create disproportionate administrative structures, permanent expenses, and excessive dependence on political appointments.

Municipalities deserve particular attention.

Data from the 2025 Public Sector Human Resources Yearbook, referring to 2023, indicated approximately 699,800 employment relationships involving individuals exclusively occupying commissioned positions in municipal Executive branches.

This figure demonstrates the scale of the issue and the need to establish objective criteria for determining the size of these structures.

Currently, the organization of commissioned positions depends on different laws and administrative decisions, without a uniform national formula based on the population served.

The proposal seeks to change this approach.

The size of the freely appointed structure must not be determined by the number of permanent civil servants, available budget, or revenue-generating capacity of the public entity.

There must be an objective, calculable, and enforceable limit.

Using population as a parameter establishes a direct relationship between the size of the administrative structure and the number of citizens served.

Another problem is the possibility of indirectly expanding these structures through the creation of positions in public authorities, foundations, and other government bodies.

For this reason, the proposal considers the entire municipal administration, preventing administrative fragmentation from being used to circumvent the limits.

The legislation also aims to accelerate expenditure reduction.

Rather than relying on retirements, natural departures, or gradual adjustments over several years, the proposal requires excess positions to be eliminated within 90 days.

The objective is to generate short-term savings while preserving permanent civil servants and essential administrative activities.

Finally, the proposal seeks to strengthen professional public management by reducing dependence on external appointments and encouraging the use of permanent civil servants for leadership and advisory positions.

## 3. Expected benefits

- Reduction in public expenditure: lower spending on salaries and employment charges associated with excess commissioned positions.
- Reduction in the political structure: objective limits on the number of freely appointed positions.
- Population-based criteria: sizing administrative structures according to the population served.
- Fewer political appointments: reduction in the number of positions available for external appointments.
- Professionalization of management: encouragement of permanent civil servants taking on leadership positions.
- Nationwide application: establishment of parameters for different levels of government and branches.
- Fiscal efficiency: mandatory compliance within 90 days.
- Transparency: individualized disclosure of positions and their respective costs.
- Reduction of redundant structures: elimination of unnecessary administrative positions.
- Fraud prevention: prohibition on recreating positions through artificial changes in nomenclature.
- Administrative efficiency: encouragement of organizational restructuring.
- Budget predictability: greater control over permanent expenditure on freely appointed positions.

## 4. Potential impacts

Positive impacts:

- Reduction in permanent personnel expenses.
- Savings resulting from the elimination of excess positions.
- Reduction in the freely appointed administrative structure.
- Fewer opportunities to use positions to accommodate political interests.
- Incentives to use permanent professionals in management positions.
- Greater standardization of administrative structures.
- Simplification of public expenditure oversight.
- Reduction of redundant administrative structures.
- Greater transparency regarding appointment costs.
- Incentives to rationalize the structures of all three branches of government.

Negative impacts or risks:

- Potential increased workload for permanent civil servants who assume duties previously distributed among commissioned positions.
- Need for administrative restructuring in government bodies with a large number of positions.
- Potential legal challenges involving the administrative and financial autonomy of government branches.
- Risk of temporary loss of management capacity in certain government bodies.
- Need to review administrative structures and local legislation.
- Potential attempts to replace abolished positions with indirect hiring arrangements.
- Need to establish specific criteria for the Federal Government and states.
- Potential insufficiency of the limit in municipalities with particularly complex administrative structures.

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

The proposal has a measurable fiscal objective, but its nationwide implementation requires careful consideration of the constitutional allocation of powers.

The Federal Constitution establishes the autonomy of the Federal Government, states, Federal District, and municipalities, while also recognizing the administrative and financial autonomy of different institutions and branches of government.

Therefore, an ordinary federal law cannot simply order the elimination of positions within the administrative structures of all these entities and institutions.

The proposal must assess the adoption of a constitutional amendment establishing general limits on commissioned positions, followed by implementing legislation.

The main challenges are:

- Legislative instrument: determine the combination of a constitutional amendment and implementing legislation required to cover all three branches and every level of government.
- Population-based formula: establish the limit of one commissioned position per 1,000 inhabitants, with a minimum of ten positions, for municipalities.
- States and Federal Government: establish specific population-based criteria for these levels without automatically applying the municipal formula.
- Accounting: precisely define which positions and entities are included in each government's limit.
- Branches of government: establish how the overall cap will be distributed among the Executive, Legislative, and other covered institutions.
- Positions of trust: preserve their legal nature while preventing their artificial use as substitutes for abolished commissioned positions.
- Compliance deadline: ensure that excess positions are effectively eliminated within 90 days.
- Budgetary impact: estimate savings from salaries, bonuses, and employment charges, subtracting any restructuring costs.
- Administrative continuity: identify essential duties that will need to be redistributed among permanent civil servants.
- Oversight: establish monitoring mechanisms involving Courts of Accounts and oversight bodies.
- Anti-circumvention: prevent the use of other hiring arrangements to rebuild freely appointed structures.
- Federal autonomy: reconcile national limits with the administrative organization of government entities and constitutionally autonomous branches.

The proposal must consider that eliminating commissioned positions does not automatically eliminate the corresponding administrative activities.

Necessary duties must be redistributed, simplified, or incorporated into existing permanent structures.

## 6. Open issues

- Confirm the limit of one commissioned position per 1,000 inhabitants, with a minimum of ten positions per municipality.
- Define the treatment of municipalities with populations below 10,000 inhabitants.
- Establish the methodology for determining commissioned position limits in states, the Federal District, and the Federal Government.
- Determine whether the limit will be global for each government entity or whether there will be a specific allocation among the branches.
- Define how commissioned positions in state-owned enterprises and mixed-capital companies will be counted.
- Establish the treatment of commissioned positions occupied by permanent civil servants.
- Confirm the exclusion of positions of trust from the numerical limit, preserving their exclusive designation for permanent civil servants.
- Define mechanisms to prevent the artificial conversion of commissioned positions into positions of trust or additional compensation.
- Establish how excess positions will be eliminated within the 90-day deadline.
- Define the methodology for calculating the financial savings resulting from the reform.
- Assess the need to amend Article 37, item V, of the Federal Constitution.
- Assess the need for specific constitutional provisions to ensure application of the limits across all three branches.
- Define oversight and accountability mechanisms for public officials.
- Establish transitional rules for redistributing administrative duties.
