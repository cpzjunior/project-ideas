# Modernizing the FGTS as a Financial Protection and Savings Instrument

## 1. Idea

Modernize the FGTS while preserving its role in financial protection and housing finance, but increasing its flexibility, attractiveness, and reach.

The funds would be divided into two categories:

- Emergency Reserve: A mandatory portion equivalent to six months of the worker's base salary or reference income. It would retain the FGTS's current withdrawal and protection rules.
- Flexible Balance: A portion available for withdrawal or continued investment in the fund, with potentially more attractive returns inspired by investment instruments such as LCI.

The return on the flexible balance could include a greater share for workers of the financial returns generated by the allocation of funds to housing loans, reducing the portion of the financial spread currently retained by intermediaries.

The proposal also includes voluntary participation by independent contractors (PJ) and self-employed professionals, with their own flexible contributions, following the same accumulation and return model. The goal is to attract new participants without imposing contribution obligations or unnecessary restrictions on access to their funds.

## 2. Motivation

The FGTS plays an important role in protecting workers and financing housing, but its returns and withdrawal rules offer limited incentives for voluntary savings accumulation.

Additionally, PJ professionals and self-employed workers do not have access to an equivalent savings and financial protection mechanism.

The idea aims to transform the FGTS into a more attractive wealth-building instrument without compromising its social and housing-related functions.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Encouragement of financial education and emergency savings.
- Greater financial autonomy and security for workers.
- Potentially more competitive returns on funds kept in the fund.
- Greater worker participation in the returns generated by housing finance.
- Voluntary inclusion of PJ professionals and self-employed workers.
- Expansion of the participant base and the availability of funds for housing finance.
- Encouragement of domestic savings and wealth accumulation.

## 4. Potential Impacts

- Workers: Better returns, greater autonomy, and improved financial planning capabilities.
- PJ professionals and self-employed workers: Voluntary access to a housing investment and savings instrument.
- Financial System: Potential redistribution of part of the financial spread in favor of participants.
- Housing: Potential expansion of available resources for housing finance.
- Economy: Encouragement of long-term savings and wealth accumulation.
- FGTS: Need for adjustments to financial management to balance returns, liquidity, and sustainability.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

- Define a return model that allows a greater share of financial returns to be passed on to participants.
- Assess the use of a structure similar to LCI, considering the legal and operational characteristics of the FGTS.
- Ensure that higher returns do not compromise the fund's financial sustainability.
- Establish voluntary participation mechanisms for PJ professionals and self-employed workers.
- Fully preserve current housing finance rules and existing commitments.

## 6. Open Questions

- What portion of the financial spread could be passed on to participants?
- How can the flexible balance's return structure balance attractiveness and sustainability?
- How can flexible contributions and withdrawals be enabled for PJ professionals and self-employed workers?
- How can the liquidity needs of new participants be reconciled with the terms of housing loans?
