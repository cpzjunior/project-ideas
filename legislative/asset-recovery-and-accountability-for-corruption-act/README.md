# Asset Recovery and Accountability for Corruption Act

## 1. Concept

Establish legislation to significantly increase the financial consequences of corruption and ensure the full recovery of misappropriated public funds, including amounts transferred to family members, third parties, companies, and nonprofit organizations.

The central principle is that **assets derived from illicit activity retain their illicit origin regardless of how many times they are transferred, donated, or placed in the name of other individuals or entities**.

The proposal seeks to make corruption economically unprofitable by targeting both those responsible for the misappropriation and those who knowingly receive, manage, or conceal the funds.

## 2. Rationale

Brazilian legislation already provides mechanisms for accountability for corruption, money laundering, administrative misconduct, and asset recovery. However, this proposal aims to strengthen the effectiveness of these measures in recovering assets, particularly in cases involving structures used to conceal or disperse illicit funds.

The mechanisms to be addressed include:

- Transfers of money and assets to spouses, children, and other family members.
    
- Gifts and donations acquired with misappropriated funds.
    
- Use of friends, intermediaries, and companies to conceal the true owners.
    
- Successive transfers between individuals and legal entities.
    
- Use of associations, foundations, institutes, and other nonprofit organizations to receive or move illicit funds.
    

## 3. Expected benefits

- More effective recovery of misappropriated public funds.
    
- Reduction of the economic benefits derived from corruption.
    
- Deterrence of the use of family members and third parties as instruments for concealing assets.
    
- Accountability for those who knowingly receive or manage assets of illicit origin.
    
- Greater capacity to trace funds transferred between individuals, companies, and nonprofit entities.
    

## 4. Potential impacts

### 4.1. Restitution and financial penalty

Require full restitution of the misappropriated amount, adjusted for inflation and applicable interest, in addition to a financial penalty equivalent to five times the amount misappropriated.

Example: for a misappropriation of R$ 10 million:

- R$ 10 million in restitution.
    
- R$ 50 million in penalties.
    
- Total of R$ 60 million, before statutory additions.
    

The penalty must apply to individuals and legal entities proven to be responsible, regardless of the company's revenue or economic size.

### 4.2. Accountability of individuals and legal entities

Hold all parties involved accountable according to their participation, benefit, and responsibility, including public officials, business owners, intermediaries, beneficiaries, and other participants in the scheme.

### 4.3. Assets transferred to family members and third parties

Establish that the transfer of illicit assets does not change their origin or prevent their recovery.

The rule must cover:

- Gifts and donations.
    
- Assets registered in the names of family members.
    
- Transfers to friends and close associates.
    
- Companies used to conceal assets.
    
- Intermediaries and beneficiaries who are aware of the illicit origin.
    

Anyone who knowingly receives, manages, uses, or conceals assets of illicit origin must be held liable under applicable legislation, including for money laundering when its legal requirements are met.

The recovery of assets of illicit origin must be treated separately from the personal punishment of those who received them, while respecting the rights of bona fide third parties.

### 4.4. Application to nonprofit organizations

The rules must also apply to associations, foundations, institutes, social organizations, OSCIPs (Civil Society Organizations of Public Interest), and other nonprofit entities.

The nonprofit nature or social purpose of an organization may not serve as protection for receiving, moving, or concealing illicit funds.

The organization may be required to return the illicit funds received, while directors, administrators, and other parties involved will be held individually liable when their participation or responsibility is proven.

The organization's legitimate assets must be preserved when they are neither of illicit origin nor related to the misappropriated funds.

### 4.5. Asset tracing and access to information

Provide for asset investigation mechanisms, subject to reasoned judicial authorization, including access to:

- Banking and financial data.
    
- Tax returns.
    
- Corporate ownership interests and ultimate beneficial owners.
    
- Real estate, vehicles, and investments.
    
- Asset transfers and financial transactions.
    

Investigations may extend to family members, partners, friends, associates, and related companies when objective evidence indicates that these individuals or entities were used to conceal assets.

Family relationships or friendships alone must not justify liability or the lifting of confidentiality protections.

### 4.6. No statute of limitations for asset recovery

The proposal must expressly ensure that claims for reimbursement of losses to the public treasury arising from intentional acts of administrative misconduct are not subject to a statute of limitations, in accordance with the Brazilian Supreme Federal Court's ruling in General Repercussion Theme 897.

The extension of this protection to other cases involving the intentional misappropriation of public funds must also be studied, subject to constitutional and judicial limits.

The absence of a statute of limitations for asset recovery must not be confused with the absence of a statute of limitations for criminal or administrative penalties.

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

- Ensure that the penalty of five times the misappropriated amount is compatible with the constitutional principles of proportionality and individualization of liability.
    
- Prevent duplicate penalties and claims for the same damage.
    
- Define liability criteria for family members, third parties, companies, and nonprofit organizations.
    
- Preserve the rights of bona fide third parties.
    
- Establish judicial procedures for tracing, freezing, and recovering assets.
    
- Coordinate the proposal with the Anti-Corruption Law, the Administrative Misconduct Law, the Money Laundering Law, and criminal legislation.
    
- Assess which provisions would require constitutional amendments rather than an ordinary statute.
    

## 6. Open issues

- Define the exact scope of the penalty of five times the misappropriated amount.
    
- Establish criteria for joint and several liability among participants.
    
- Determine how to treat assets transferred free of charge to third parties who were unaware of their illicit origin.
    
- Establish criteria for recovering assets held by nonprofit organizations.
    
- Define asset investigation procedures and the allocation of the burden of proof.
    
- Establish mechanisms to prevent the dissipation of assets during investigations and legal proceedings.
    
- Assess the possibility of legally expanding the circumstances in which claims for reimbursement are not subject to a statute of limitations.
