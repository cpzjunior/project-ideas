# Regulatory Stability and Transition Act

## 1. Concept

Create a legal framework to ensure predictability and legal certainty in regulatory changes affecting legally established economic activities.

The proposal establishes that the State may change rules, restrict, or even prohibit economic activities, but must respect transition periods, technically justify its decisions, and consider the rights, commitments, and investments made in good faith under the previous rules.

The objective is to prevent abrupt regulatory changes from transferring the full costs of government decisions to economic agents, especially when the State itself authorized, regulated, or encouraged a particular activity.

The law must apply generally, regardless of the economic sector, the government in power, or the political orientation behind the decision.

## 2. Rationale

Regulatory predictability is an important factor in maintaining the confidence of economic agents and supporting investment decisions.

When the State suddenly changes the rules governing a legal economic activity, companies and investors are exposed to risks they cannot adequately estimate. Investments made in good faith may lose their value, contracts may become unfeasible, and financial obligations may no longer be fulfilled.

This regulatory risk tends to increase the cost of capital, as investors and lenders begin demanding additional returns to compensate for legal uncertainty.

One example is the possibility of abruptly terminating or rendering unfeasible an economic activity that had previously been regulated and authorized by the State itself, without providing an adequate period for adaptation or the closure of operations.

Although Brazilian legislation already contains legal certainty and transition mechanisms, such as those provided for in the Law of Introduction to the Rules of Brazilian Law (LINDB), there is no general rule establishing a minimum adaptation period for every regulatory change capable of restricting or rendering unfeasible legally established economic activities.

The proposal seeks to fill this gap.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Greater legal certainty: reduced unpredictability in relations between the State and economic agents.
- Reduced regulatory risk: lower exposure of companies and investors to abrupt changes in rules.
- Reduced cost of capital: potential reduction in the risk premium demanded by investors and lenders.
- Encouragement of long-term investments: greater confidence in decisions that depend on extended planning horizons.
- Protection of legitimate expectations: consideration of investments, contracts, and commitments made in good faith under previously applicable rules.
- Improved regulatory quality: encouragement of prior assessments of the economic and legal impacts of public decisions.
- Greater institutional predictability: reduced perception that changes in government may arbitrarily alter the operating conditions of economic activities.

## 4. Potential Impacts

### Positive impacts

- Greater stability in the business environment.
- Reduced uncertainty in sectors subject to government licensing, authorization, or concessions.
- Greater confidence among domestic and foreign investors.
- Encouragement of public policies developed with long-term planning.
- Lower risk of disorderly interruptions of economic activities.

### Negative impacts or limitations

- Possible reduction in the State's agility in implementing regulatory changes.
- Need to balance investment protection with the public interest.
- Possibility of temporarily maintaining activities that need to be restricted for legitimate reasons.
- Greater complexity in drafting regulations and assessing their impacts.

These effects should be mitigated through objective exceptions for duly justified emergency situations.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

The proposal should include the following mechanisms:

### 5.1. Minimum Transition Period

Regulatory changes that prohibit, substantially restrict, or render unfeasible legally conducted economic activities must, as a general rule, provide a minimum adaptation period of 12 months.

For changes with a high economic, operational, or contractual impact, the minimum period must be 24 months.

When significant investments have not yet been amortized, long-term contracts are in place, or complex decommissioning is required, the transition period must be determined according to the time objectively necessary for an orderly adaptation or closure, and may exceed 24 months.

The period must take into account, among other factors:

- The scale of investments made in good faith.
- The time required for technological and operational adaptation.
- Contracts and commitments undertaken under the previous rules.
- The impact of the change on employees, suppliers, customers, and third parties.
- The reasonable capacity of affected economic agents to adapt.

The period may not be shortened for mere administrative or political convenience. Any reduction must be technically justified.

### 5.2. Protection of Authorizations and Established Legal Situations

The termination or modification of authorizations, licenses, and permits granted by the State must take into account:

- The validity and conditions originally established for the authorization.
- Investments made in good faith.
- Contracts and commitments undertaken while the previous rules were in force.
- The time required for an orderly closure of operations.
- The existence of third parties who may be harmed by the change.

This protection must not represent an acquired right to the indefinite maintenance of a particular activity or regulation. Its purpose is to ensure a proportionate and predictable transition.

### 5.3. Technical Justification and Impact Assessment

Regulatory changes with significant economic impact must include a technical justification containing:

- The intended objectives.
- The legal and technical grounds for the decision.
- The expected economic and social impacts.
- The effects on existing investments and contracts.
- The alternatives considered.
- The reasons for choosing the transition period.

### 5.4. Exceptions for Emergency Situations

Transition periods may be shortened or waived when there is a serious and imminent risk to public health, safety, the environment, or the stability of the financial system, among other exceptional situations provided for by law.

The decision must concretely demonstrate the urgency and explain why adopting a transition period would be incompatible with protecting the public interest.

Whenever possible, measures must be adopted to reduce unnecessary losses for affected economic agents and allow the orderly settlement of existing obligations.

This exception may not be used as a generic justification to circumvent the periods established by this law.

### 5.5. Institutional Scope

The rule must apply to agencies and entities of the federal, state, Federal District, and municipal public administrations, including regulatory agencies and other authorities with regulatory or administrative powers over economic activities.

Its application must respect the constitutional powers of each level of government.

### 5.6. Constitutional Limits

An important challenge is preventing the protection established by ordinary law from being circumvented through normative instruments of higher legal standing.

An ordinary law cannot prevent a subsequent law or a provisional measure from establishing provisions incompatible with it.

Therefore, the need for a Proposed Constitutional Amendment (PEC) should be assessed to establish constitutional limits on the abrupt termination or restriction of legally authorized economic activities, including through provisional measures.

The constitutional amendment must preserve the State's ability to act in exceptional situations, without allowing urgency to be used as a generic justification for eliminating regulatory predictability.

## 6. Open Questions

- Establish objective criteria for classifying a regulatory change as having a high impact.
- Define how investments made after the public announcement of a possible regulatory change will be treated.
- Establish criteria for distinguishing legitimate public policy changes from changes that violate legitimate expectations.
- Define the treatment of temporary, precarious, or conditional authorizations.
- Assess the need for compensation in situations where a transition is impossible and legitimate investments are directly harmed.
- Define the limits of exceptions for emergency situations.
- Assess the development of a PEC to complement the law, particularly to regulate provisional measures and other normative changes with the force of law.
- Assess oversight mechanisms and consequences for authorities that unjustifiably fail to comply with transition rules.
