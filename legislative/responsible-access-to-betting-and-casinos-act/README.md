# Responsible Access to Betting and Casinos Act

## 1. Idea

Create legislation establishing financial eligibility criteria for access to betting platforms and casinos in Brazil, making user participation conditional on proof of minimum financial capacity and requiring mandatory responsible gambling mechanisms.

The law will apply to all legally authorized operators, regardless of the type of betting or gaming activity or the operating channel, covering digital, in-person, or any other authorized means.

The objective is to prevent individuals experiencing financial vulnerability from accessing these activities, without imposing an indiscriminate restriction on adult participation.

To obtain and maintain access authorization, users must simultaneously meet the following requirements:

- Prove a minimum gross individual monthly income equivalent to three minimum wages.
    
- Have no active negative credit records with recognized credit protection agencies, such as Serasa and SPC.
    
- Renew access authorization every 12 months.
    

Operators must verify eligibility during registration, renewal, and periodically throughout the validity of the authorization. If a user ceases to meet the financial criteria, their access must be suspended.

The suspension must be communicated to the user, who will have the right to dispute incorrect or improper information before access is potentially reinstated.

Proof of income must consider different sources of income, including formal employment, self-employment, and other legitimate sources. Accepted documents and procedures may be defined through supplementary regulations.

The legislation will also require responsible gambling mechanisms, including:

- Deposit, spending, and loss limits.
    
- Usage time limits.
    
- Alerts regarding risky behavioral patterns.
    
- Nationally integrated self-exclusion tools.
    

## 2. Rationale

The proposal is based on the understanding that prohibiting or restricting these activities does not necessarily eliminate demand, and may instead drive it toward illegal operators or those not subject to government oversight.

The existence of a regulated market makes it possible to establish uniform access criteria, supervise operators, protect consumers, and combat illegal activities.

The formalization of the sector also increases the government's capacity for oversight and taxation, subjecting operators to legal, tax, and regulatory obligations.

The proposed financial criteria seek to prevent individuals experiencing economic vulnerability from accessing activities that may worsen their debt or compromise their financial stability.

The combination of minimum income and the absence of negative credit records establishes two complementary requirements: minimum financial capacity and financial standing.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Increased Public Revenue: expansion of the tax base through the formalization of operators.
    
- Formalization of Economic Activity: subjecting operators to tax, accounting, and regulatory obligations.
    
- Combating the Illegal Market: strengthening mechanisms for identifying and monitoring illegal activities.
    
- Financial Protection: restricting access for individuals who do not meet the minimum eligibility criteria.
    
- Responsible Gambling: preventing compulsive behavior through limits, alerts, and self-exclusion.
    
- Regulatory Consistency: applying the same requirements to all authorized operators.
    
- Greater Oversight Capacity: monitoring activities and imposing penalties in cases of non-compliance.
    
- Transparency: improved monitoring of financial transactions and operator obligations.
    

## 4. Potential Impacts

The regulation may increase tax revenue, encourage job creation, and strengthen formal economic activity associated with the sector.

The uniform application of eligibility criteria will reduce regulatory loopholes and prevent operators from using differences in activity types or operating channels to circumvent legal requirements.

Implementation will require integration between operators, regulatory agencies, and credit protection systems, as well as standardized procedures for proof of income and credit record verification.

It will also be necessary to ensure mechanisms for disputing incorrect negative records, preventing improper access restrictions.

Finally, access restrictions may encourage some ineligible users to seek out illegal platforms. Efforts to combat these operators must accompany the implementation of the legislation.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

Implementation will require:

- Application of the law to all authorized operators, regardless of the activity type or operating channel.
    
- Compliance with the betting and gaming activities whose operation is authorized under specific legislation.
    
- Amendments to existing legislation to establish financial eligibility requirements.
    
- Definition of taxes applicable to regulated activities, in accordance with applicable tax legislation.
    
- Integration of operators with recognized credit protection agencies.
    
- Definition of the frequency of credit record checks.
    
- Creation of procedures for disputing and correcting financial information.
    
- Establishment of technical and operational requirements for implementing responsible gambling mechanisms.
    
- Continuous oversight and enforcement of penalties in cases of non-compliance.
    

Operators that allow access to ineligible individuals, disregard suspensions, or fail to implement mandatory mechanisms will be subject to fines, suspension, and potential revocation of their operating authorization.

The legislation must comply with the General Data Protection Law (LGPD), limiting the processing and sharing of personal information to what is strictly necessary to verify user eligibility. Data may not be used for targeted advertising or purposes incompatible with the legislation.

## 6. Open Issues

- Define the documents accepted as proof of income, including for self-employed and informal workers.
    
- Define the frequency of checks with credit protection agencies.
    
- Establish deadlines and procedures for access suspension and disputes involving negative credit records.
    
- Define deposit, spending, loss, and usage time limits.
    
- Establish temporary self-exclusion periods and the conditions for potentially reversing indefinite self-exclusion.
    
- Define the authority responsible for managing the national self-exclusion system.
    
- Define the tax model and the allocation of collected revenue.
    
- Establish transition rules for existing users and operators.
