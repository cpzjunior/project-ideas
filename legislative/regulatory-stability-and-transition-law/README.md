# Regulatory Stability and Transition Law

## 1. Idea

Create a legal framework to ensure predictability and legal certainty in regulatory changes affecting legally established economic activities.

The proposal establishes that the State may change rules, restrict, or even prohibit economic activities, but must respect transition periods, provide technical justification for its decisions, and take into account the rights, commitments, and investments made in good faith under the previous rules.

The objective is to prevent abrupt regulatory changes from shifting the entire cost of government decisions onto economic agents, especially when the State itself has authorized, regulated, or encouraged a particular activity.

The law must apply generally, regardless of the economic sector, the government in office, or the political orientation behind the decision.

## 2. Rationale

Regulatory predictability is an important factor in economic agents' confidence and investment decision-making.

When the State suddenly changes the rules governing a legal economic activity, companies and investors are exposed to risks they cannot adequately assess. Investments made in good faith may lose their value, contracts may become unfeasible, and financial obligations may no longer be met.

This regulatory risk tends to increase the cost of capital, as investors and lenders begin demanding additional returns to compensate for legal uncertainty.

One example is the possibility of abruptly terminating or rendering unfeasible an economic activity that had previously been regulated and authorized by the State itself, without providing an adequate period for adaptation or the winding down of operations.

Although Brazilian legislation already contains legal certainty and transition mechanisms, such as those provided for in the Law of Introduction to Brazilian Law (LINDB), there is no general rule establishing a minimum adaptation period for every regulatory change capable of restricting or rendering unfeasible legally established economic activities.

The proposal seeks to fill this gap.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Greater legal certainty: reduced unpredictability in relations between the State and economic agents.
- Reduced regulatory risk: lower exposure of companies and investors to abrupt changes in the rules.
- Lower cost of capital: potential reduction in the risk premium required by investors and lenders.
- Encouragement of long-term investment: greater confidence in decisions that depend on extended planning horizons.
- Protection of legitimate expectations: consideration of investments, contracts, and commitments undertaken in good faith under previously applicable rules.
- Improved regulatory quality: incentives for prior assessment of the economic and legal impacts of public decisions.
- Greater institutional predictability: reduced perception that changes in government may arbitrarily alter the operating conditions of economic activities.

## 4. Potential Impacts

### Positive Impacts

- Greater stability in the business environment.
- Reduced uncertainty in sectors subject to government licensing, authorization, or concessions.
- Greater confidence among domestic and foreign investors.
- Encouragement of public policy development based on long-term planning.
- Lower risk of disorderly interruptions of economic activities.

### Negative Impacts or Limitations

- Potential reduction in the State's agility in implementing regulatory changes.
- Need to balance investment protection with the public interest.
- Possibility of temporarily maintaining activities that need to be restricted for legitimate reasons.
- Greater complexity in drafting regulations and assessing their impacts.

These effects should be mitigated through objective exceptions for duly justified urgent situations.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

The proposal should include the following mechanisms:

### 5.1. Minimum Transition Period

Regulatory changes that prohibit, substantially restrict, or render unfeasible legally conducted economic activities must, as a general rule, provide a minimum 12-month adaptation period.

For changes with a high economic, operational, or contractual impact, the minimum period must be 24 months.

Where there are significant investments that have not yet been amortized, long-term contracts, or a need for complex decommissioning, the transition period must be determined according to the time objectively required for adaptation or an orderly shutdown, and may exceed 24 months.

The period must take into account, among other factors:

- The scale of investments made in good faith.
- The time required for technological and operational adaptation.
- Contracts and commitments undertaken under the previous rules.
- The impact of the change on employees, suppliers, customers, and third parties.
- The reasonable ability of affected parties to adapt.

The period may not be shortened solely for administrative or political convenience. Any reduction must be technically justified.

### 5.2. Protection of Existing Authorizations and Established Situations

The termination or amendment of authorizations, licenses, and permits granted by the State must take into account:

- The validity and conditions originally established for the authorization.
- Investments made in good faith.
- Contracts and commitments undertaken while the previous rules were in force.
- The time required for an orderly shutdown of operations.
- The existence of third parties who may be adversely affected by the change.

This protection must not constitute an acquired right to the indefinite maintenance of a particular activity or regulatory framework. Its purpose is to ensure a proportionate and predictable transition.

### 5.3. Technical Justification and Impact Assessment

Regulatory changes with significant economic impact must include a technical justification containing:

- The intended objectives.
- The legal and technical grounds for the decision.
- The expected economic and social impacts.
- The effects on existing investments and contracts.
- The alternatives considered.
- The reasons for choosing the transition period.

### 5.4. Exceptions for Urgent Situations

Transition periods may be shortened or waived when there is a serious and imminent risk to public health, safety, the environment, or the stability of the financial system, among other exceptional circumstances provided for by law.

The decision must specifically demonstrate the urgency and explain why adopting a transition period would be incompatible with protecting the public interest.

Whenever possible, measures must be adopted to reduce unnecessary losses for affected parties and allow for the orderly winding down of existing obligations.

This exception may not be used as a generic justification for circumventing the deadlines established by this law.

### 5.5. Institutional Scope

The rule must apply to bodies and entities of the federal, state, Federal District, and municipal public administrations, including regulatory agencies and other authorities with regulatory or administrative powers over economic activities.

Its application must respect the constitutional powers of each level of government.

### 5.6. Constitutional Limits

An important challenge is preventing the protection established by ordinary legislation from being circumvented through higher-ranking legal instruments.

An ordinary law cannot prevent a subsequent law or provisional measure from establishing provisions that conflict with it.

Therefore, the need for a Proposed Constitutional Amendment (PEC) must be assessed to establish constitutional limits on the abrupt termination or restriction of legally authorized economic activities, including through provisional measures.

The constitutional amendment must preserve the State's ability to act in exceptional circumstances, without allowing urgency to be used as a generic justification for eliminating regulatory predictability.

## 6. Open Issues

- Establish objective criteria for classifying a regulatory change as having a high impact.
- Define how investments made after the public announcement of a potential regulatory change will be treated.
- Establish criteria for distinguishing legitimate public policy changes from changes that violate legitimate expectations.
- Define the treatment of temporary, precarious, or conditional authorizations.
- Assess the need for compensation in situations where a transition is impossible and legitimate investments are directly harmed.
- Define the limits of exceptions for urgent situations.
- Assess the development of a PEC to complement the law, particularly to regulate provisional measures and other legal changes having the force of law.
- Assess oversight mechanisms and consequences for authorities that unjustifiably fail to comply with transition rules.
