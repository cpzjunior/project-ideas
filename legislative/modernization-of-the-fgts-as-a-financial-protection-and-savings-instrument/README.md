# Modernization of the FGTS as a Financial Protection and Savings Instrument

## 1. Idea

Modernize the FGTS while preserving its role in financial protection and housing finance, but increasing its flexibility, attractiveness, and reach.

The funds would be divided into two components:

- Emergency Reserve: a mandatory portion equivalent to six months of the worker's base salary or reference income. It would retain the current FGTS withdrawal and protection rules.
- Flexible Balance: a portion available for withdrawal or for remaining invested in the fund, with potentially more attractive returns inspired by the model of financial instruments such as LCI (Real Estate Credit Notes).

The return on the flexible balance could include a greater share for workers of the returns generated by the use of these funds in housing loans, reducing the portion of the financial spread currently retained by intermediaries.

The proposal also provides for voluntary participation by independent contractors and self-employed professionals, with their own flexible contributions, following the same accumulation and return model. The goal is to attract new participants without imposing contribution requirements or unnecessary restrictions on access to their funds.

## 2. Motivation

The FGTS plays an important role in protecting workers and financing housing, but its returns and withdrawal rules provide limited incentives for voluntary savings accumulation.

Furthermore, independent contractors and self-employed professionals do not have access to an equivalent financial protection and savings mechanism.

The idea seeks to transform the FGTS into a more attractive wealth-building instrument without compromising its social and housing-related purposes.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Encouragement of financial education and emergency savings.
- Greater financial autonomy and security for workers.
- Potentially more competitive returns on funds held in the FGTS.
- Greater participation by workers in the returns generated through housing finance.
- Voluntary inclusion of independent contractors and self-employed professionals.
- Expansion of the participant base and the resources available for housing finance.
- Encouragement of domestic savings and wealth accumulation.

## 4. Potential Impacts

- Workers: higher returns, greater autonomy, and improved financial planning capabilities.
- Independent contractors and self-employed professionals: voluntary access to a housing-related savings and investment instrument.
- Financial system: potential redistribution of part of the financial spread in favor of participants.
- Housing: potential expansion of the resources available for housing finance.
- Economy: encouragement of long-term savings and wealth accumulation.
- FGTS: need to adapt its financial management to balance returns, liquidity, and long-term sustainability.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

- Define a return model that allows a greater share of financial returns to be passed on to participants.
- Assess the use of a structure similar to LCI, taking into account the FGTS's specific legal and operational characteristics.
- Ensure that higher returns do not compromise the fund's financial sustainability.
- Establish a voluntary participation framework for independent contractors and self-employed professionals.
- Fully preserve the current housing finance rules and existing commitments.

## 6. Open Questions

- What portion of the financial spread could be passed on to participants?
- How should returns on the flexible balance be structured to balance attractiveness and sustainability?
- How can flexible contributions and withdrawals be made available to independent contractors and self-employed professionals?
- How can the liquidity needs of new participants be balanced with the terms of housing loans?
