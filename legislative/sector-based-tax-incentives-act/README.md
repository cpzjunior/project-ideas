# Sector-Based Tax Incentives Act

## 1. Idea

Create a law establishing rules for the granting of tax incentives by the government, requiring these benefits to be directed exclusively to economic sectors rather than specific companies.

The proposal is based on the principle that tax incentives directed at individual companies can undermine free enterprise and competition by creating artificial competitive advantages for certain economic players.

The proposal establishes the following mechanisms:

- Sector-based incentives only: the government may not grant tax incentives directed at specific companies. All benefits must be granted to an economic sector, based on objective criteria applicable to all companies that meet the established requirements.
- Limit on eligible sectors: each government entity may maintain tax incentives for a maximum of five economic sectors simultaneously, preventing excessive use of this instrument.
- Federal scope: the rules will apply to the Federal Government, states, the Federal District, and municipalities. Each government entity will have its own limit of up to five eligible economic sectors, in accordance with its taxing authority.
- Objective criteria: the granting of incentives must be based on clear, public, and verifiable criteria, applied equally to companies within the eligible sector.
- Transparency: all incentives granted must have their justifications, conditions, scope, and expected impacts publicly disclosed.
- Competitive neutrality: incentives may not establish exclusive advantages for specific companies and must preserve fair competitive conditions within the eligible sector.

## 2. Motivation

The granting of tax incentives to specific companies can interfere with free enterprise and competition, favoring certain economic players at the expense of their competitors.

This type of benefit can also create relationships of dependence between companies and governments, as well as open the door to discretionary decisions when selecting which organizations will receive tax advantages.

The proposal seeks to establish rules that make the granting of tax incentives more transparent, impartial, and predictable, ensuring that benefits are directed toward entire economic sectors rather than individually selected companies.

Another objective is to limit the number of sectors benefiting simultaneously under each government entity, preventing tax incentives from becoming a recurring economic policy instrument and excessively compromising public revenue.

Applying the rule across all three levels of government seeks to ensure that the restriction cannot be circumvented by transferring the granting of benefits between different levels of government.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Free enterprise: reduced government interference in competition between companies.
- Equal treatment: equivalent tax treatment for companies operating in the same sector and meeting the established criteria.
- Competition: reduction of competitive advantages granted individually by the government.
- Transparency: greater clarity regarding the criteria and justifications for granting incentives.
- Fiscal responsibility: limitation on the number of sectors benefiting simultaneously within each government entity.
- Predictability: clearer and more stable rules for companies and investors.
- Reduced discretion: decreased possibility of granting tax benefits through decisions targeting specific companies.
- Federal uniformity: application of the same general restrictions to the Federal Government, states, the Federal District, and municipalities.

## 4. Potential Impacts

- Companies: more balanced competitive conditions and greater tax predictability.
- Economic sectors: the possibility of receiving incentives under uniform criteria and with sector-wide coverage.
- Government: the need to technically justify the selection of eligible sectors and manage a maximum limit of five sectors per government entity.
- Public revenue: greater control over the number of sectors covered by tax incentives.
- Market: reduction of competitive distortions caused by benefits directed at specific companies.
- Economy: potential redistribution of tax incentives among sectors, according to the economic priorities defined by each government entity.
- Federalism: the need to align tax incentive policies with common rules while preserving the constitutional distribution of taxing powers.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

- Sector definition: establish criteria for defining the economic sectors that may receive incentives, avoiding classifications that are excessively broad or specific.
- Granting criteria: define objective requirements that justify the selection of each eligible sector.
- Sector limit: establish how the limit of five sectors per government entity will be applied and how one eligible sector may be replaced by another.
- Competitive neutrality: ensure that eligibility criteria are not structured in a way that indirectly favors specific companies.
- Legal compatibility: assess the proposal's compatibility with the Federal Constitution and current tax and fiscal regulations, respecting the autonomy of government entities and their taxing powers.
- Federal application: define mechanisms to ensure that the rules are applied by the Federal Government, states, the Federal District, and municipalities.
- Transparency and oversight: establish mechanisms to monitor the incentives granted and verify compliance with the criteria.
- Transition: define how existing tax incentives granted to specific companies or economic sectors will be handled.

## 6. Open Questions

- How will economic sectors be defined and classified for the purposes of this law?
- What criteria should justify the selection of the five eligible sectors for each government entity?
- What will be the maximum duration of tax incentives?
- How will one eligible sector be replaced by another?
- How will existing tax incentives be handled?
- Will there be exceptions for specific situations, such as public calamities or economic crises?
- How will the rule's compatibility with federal autonomy and the taxing powers of each government entity be ensured?
