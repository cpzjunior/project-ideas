# Pension Reform with Individual Capitalization and Social Protection

## 1. Idea

Create a pension reform that gradually replaces the predominantly pay-as-you-go system with a mandatory individual capitalization system, preserving existing structures and rules wherever they can be retained.

The proposal aims to make the pension system financially sustainable in the long term, increase transparency regarding contributions, reduce privileges among professional categories, and guarantee minimum protection for elderly people who are unable to accumulate sufficient resources for their retirement.

The new model will be based on six main rules:

### 1. Mandatory individual capitalization

All workers will be required to contribute to their retirement, accumulating individual pension assets.

The funds will be managed by private financial institutions classified in Segment S1 by the Central Bank of Brazil, through low-risk pension funds.

Management must comply with the following rules:

- Each worker will have an individual pension account.
- The accumulated funds will belong to the worker.
- Investments must follow prudential criteria, prioritizing security and capital preservation.
- Workers will be able to monitor their balance, contribution history, and retirement projections.
- Portability between authorized financial institutions will be permitted.
- The funds must remain segregated from the managing institutions' own assets.
- Institutions must maintain custody controls, audits, and transparency.

Financial institution liability:

- Misappropriation, fraud, embezzlement, and improper use of funds must result in an obligation to fully reimburse the amounts involved, with adjustments and penalties.
- Institutions will be liable for losses resulting from custody failures, negligence, or non-compliance with their obligations.
- Administrators and individuals involved in fraud will be subject to applicable legal sanctions.
- Normal market fluctuations will not, by themselves, constitute grounds for holding an institution liable.

### 2. Minimum retirement age linked to life expectancy

The minimum retirement age must be automatically adjusted according to population life expectancy.

The rule should aim to guarantee approximately ten years of remaining life expectancy at the time of retirement.

The calculation must use separate mortality tables for men and women.

Consequently:

- Men and women may have different minimum retirement ages.
- Women may have a higher minimum retirement age due to their greater life expectancy.
- The minimum retirement age will be periodically updated according to demographic changes.
- The methodology must be public, objective, and independent of periodic political decisions.

The objective is to equalize the expected period of retirement benefit receipt, preventing demographic differences from creating actuarial imbalances.

### 3. Pension ceiling and elimination of privileges

Establish uniform pension rules for private-sector workers and public servants.

The proposal provides for:

- A ceiling on public pension benefits equivalent to the INSS ceiling.
- Application of the same minimum retirement age rules.
- Application of the same contribution and capitalization rules.
- Elimination of special pension schemes that grant advantages without technical justification.
- The possibility of supplementing retirement benefits through accumulated individual assets.
- Convergence of existing pension schemes, including the treatment of military pension schemes.

No professional category should receive pension advantages funded by the State that are not available to other workers under equivalent conditions.

### 4. Retirement Assistance: supplementing income up to one minimum wage

Create a social benefit called Retirement Assistance, intended to supplement the retirement income of individuals who have been unable to accumulate sufficient resources to reach one minimum wage in pension income.

The benefit will be supplementary in nature and will operate as follows:

- The citizen must reach the minimum retirement age.
- They must apply for retirement through existing pension mechanisms.
- If their pension income is below one minimum wage, they may apply for Retirement Assistance.
- The State will supplement the amount received until it reaches one minimum wage.
- Individuals whose retirement benefit already equals or exceeds one minimum wage will not be eligible for the supplement.

Example, considering a hypothetical minimum wage of BRL 1,621:

| Retirement Benefit | Retirement Assistance | Total Income |
|---:|---:|---:|
| BRL 400 | BRL 1,221 | BRL 1,621 |
| BRL 800 | BRL 821 | BRL 1,621 |
| BRL 1,200 | BRL 421 | BRL 1,621 |
| BRL 1,621 | BRL 0 | BRL 1,621 |
| BRL 2,000 | BRL 0 | BRL 2,000 |

The benefit will be funded by tax revenues, separately from the funds accumulated in individual accounts.

Its purpose will be to protect individuals who, despite being subject to mandatory contributions, have been unable to accumulate sufficient pension assets.

### 5. Gradual transition to the new system

The reform must make maximum use of existing pension structures, avoiding the unnecessary creation of new government agencies and administrative systems.

The transition must follow these principles:

- Current retirees will retain their benefits.
- Workers who are already contributing will have their accrued rights preserved.
- Migration to the new model will occur gradually, taking into account age, contribution history, and previously established pension rights.
- New workers will enter directly into the mandatory individual capitalization model.
- The current system will continue operating for as long as there are outstanding pension obligations to be paid.
- Existing pension liabilities must have their own transparent funding arrangements.

The administrative structure of the INSS and existing mechanisms for registration, collection, benefit granting, and oversight must be retained whenever compatible with the new model.

The reform must avoid unnecessary changes to existing rules that are not directly related to the proposal's objectives.

### 6. Administration and oversight of funds

Pension funds will be managed exclusively by private financial institutions classified in Segment S1 by the Central Bank of Brazil, provided they are duly authorized to perform this activity.

Institutions must comply with specific requirements:

- Low-risk pension funds.
- Segregation of pension assets from the financial institution's own assets.
- Periodic independent audits.
- Transparency regarding investments, fees, and returns.
- Regulatory limits on management fees.
- Portability between authorized institutions.
- Liability for misappropriation, fraud, and management failures.

The Central Bank of Brazil, the Brazilian Securities and Exchange Commission (CVM), and other competent authorities will exercise oversight in accordance with their regulatory responsibilities.

Pension assets may not be freely used by financial institutions to finance their own operations or cover third-party obligations.

## 2. Motivation

Brazil's current pension system faces structural challenges related to population aging, the declining proportion of active workers relative to retirees, and the growth of pension expenditures.

Under the predominantly pay-as-you-go model, contributions from current workers finance the benefits of current retirees.

This makes the system's sustainability continuously dependent on the revenue-generating capacity of future generations and on the country's demographic and economic conditions.

Furthermore, workers have little direct connection between the pension assets actually accumulated and the benefit they will receive in the future.

The proposal seeks to modify this structure by introducing mandatory individual capitalization, under which each worker accumulates their own assets to finance their retirement.

Management by large private financial institutions seeks to leverage the infrastructure and operational capacity of the banking system, establishing competition among administrators and rigorous oversight mechanisms.

Asset segregation and institutional liability are intended to reduce opportunities for misappropriation and improper use of funds.

Another problem facing the current system is the disparity between pension schemes and the advantages granted to certain categories of public servants.

The proposal seeks to establish greater uniformity by limiting public pension benefits to the INSS ceiling and allowing supplementary retirement income through individual assets.

Linking the minimum retirement age to life expectancy also seeks to automatically adapt the system to demographic changes, preventing its sustainability from depending exclusively on periodic legislative reforms.

Finally, the creation of Retirement Assistance will provide a social safety net for individuals who are unable to accumulate sufficient resources, without turning social assistance into the primary mechanism for financing retirement.

The proposal therefore seeks to combine individual responsibility, fiscal sustainability, and social protection.

## 3. Expected benefits

- Pension sustainability: gradual reduction in dependence on intergenerational financing.
- Individual assets: accumulation of pension reserves belonging to workers.
- Transparency: individual monitoring of contributions and accumulated funds.
- Asset security: segregation of pension funds from the assets of financial institutions.
- Accountability: financial and legal consequences for institutions involved in misappropriation and fraud.
- Banking competition: opportunities for competition among institutions authorized to manage the funds.
- Elimination of privileges: greater uniformity among pension schemes.
- Actuarial fairness: adjustment of the retirement age to life expectancy.
- Social protection: guarantee of a minimum pension income of one minimum wage for eligible individuals.
- Predictability: greater clarity regarding accumulated assets and expected retirement income.
- Institutional efficiency: use of existing structures to reduce implementation costs.
- National savings: accumulation of long-term financial reserves.
- Financial market development: increased availability of resources for long-term investments.

## 4. Potential impacts

Positive impacts:

- Gradual reduction in dependence on the pay-as-you-go system.
- Accumulation of individual pension assets.
- Greater transparency regarding the allocation of contributions.
- Development of a pension fund market.
- Expansion of long-term domestic savings.
- Greater competition among financial institutions.
- Reduction of pension privileges among professional categories.
- Automatic adjustment of rules to demographic changes.
- Greater predictability for workers and employers.
- Financial protection for elderly individuals who are unable to accumulate sufficient resources.
- Use of existing banking and pension infrastructure.

Negative impacts or risks:

- High fiscal costs during the transition, due to the need to continue paying pensions financed by the old system while part of the contributions is redirected toward individual assets.
- Possibility of market concentration among a small number of financial institutions.
- Risk of insolvency or operational problems at managing institutions.
- Possibility of investment losses, even in funds classified as low-risk.
- Fund management costs.
- Possibility of insufficient assets for workers with low salaries or extended periods without contributions.
- Need for permanent funding of Retirement Assistance.
- Political and legal difficulties in aligning existing pension schemes.
- Possibility of an increase in the retirement age as life expectancy rises.

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

The proposal may be implemented through constitutional, legislative, and regulatory amendments, making use of existing pension structures whenever possible.

The main challenges are:

- Contribution rate: define the mandatory contribution rate and its division between employee and employer.

- Individual capitalization: establish how funds will be accumulated, invested, and converted into income during retirement.

- Banking administration: define the requirements for S1 institutions to be authorized to manage pension funds.

- Asset segregation: establish legal and operational mechanisms to prevent pension assets from being used to cover financial institutions' obligations.

- Liability: define the circumstances under which institutions will be liable for misappropriation, fraud, custody failures, and non-compliance with management rules.

- Investments: establish exposure limits, diversification criteria, and low-risk requirements for pension funds.

- Minimum retirement age: develop an actuarial methodology to determine the retirement age necessary to guarantee approximately ten years of remaining life expectancy, using separate mortality tables for men and women.

- Pension ceiling: establish the convergence of public pension schemes toward the INSS ceiling, while respecting applicable legal transition rules.

- Retirement Assistance: define eligibility criteria, application procedures, and mechanisms for calculating the supplement up to one minimum wage.

- Assistance funding: establish a tax-based funding source for Retirement Assistance, keeping it separate from contributory funds.

- Transition: define how current benefits will be preserved and how the obligations of the old system will be financed during the transition to capitalization.

- Administrative structure: make use of the INSS and other existing agencies' systems to reduce costs and avoid duplicated structures.

- Oversight: define the responsibilities of regulatory authorities and independent audit mechanisms.

The transition must be gradual and financially planned, recognizing that contributions redirected to individual accounts will no longer finance part of current retirement benefits.

The new system cannot be implemented without an explicit solution for this liability.

## 6. Open issues

- Define the mandatory pension contribution rate.
- Define the division of contributions between employer and employee.
- Define whether part of the contribution will be allocated to collective pension protection or whether the entire contribution will be individually capitalized.
- Define how retirement income will be calculated based on accumulated assets.
- Define the eligibility requirements for S1 banks to manage the funds.
- Define whether custody will be independent of the managing institution.
- Establish limits on management fees.
- Define the risk limits and composition of pension funds.
- Define portability rules.
- Define the treatment of individual assets in the event of the worker's death.
- Define the exact methodology for calculating the minimum retirement age based on remaining life expectancy.
- Define how frequently the minimum retirement age will be updated.
- Define transition rules for workers who are already contributing.
- Define how the pension liabilities of the old system will be financed.
- Define eligibility criteria for Retirement Assistance, including the treatment of other income and assets.
- Define the application of uniform rules to public pension schemes and military personnel.
- Define the pension treatment of self-employed and informal workers.
- Define the funding source for the social benefit.
- Assess the fiscal and actuarial impacts of the reform under different demographic and economic scenarios.
