# Long-Term Party Planning Act

## 1. Concept

Create a law requiring all political parties to develop and maintain strategic government plans with a 15-year horizon, explicitly outlining their main priorities, guidelines, objectives, targets, and measurable indicators for the country.

The plans must present a long-term vision for the main areas of State activity, allowing the public to know in advance the directions each party intends to pursue if it comes to power.

The proposal establishes the following mechanisms:

- Long-term party plans: each party must develop and publish a strategic plan with a 15-year horizon, covering its main political, economic, and social priorities and guidelines.
- Measurable targets and indicators: the plans must establish objective targets and quantitative or qualitative indicators that allow progress toward the commitments made to be monitored and assessed.
- Formal acknowledgment by party members: all party members, without exception, must formally declare that they are aware of and acknowledge the current party plan. This acknowledgment does not imply full agreement with all guidelines or proposals, nor does it prevent internal disagreements.
- Consequences for non-compliance: each party must establish in its bylaws the consequences applicable to members who fail to fulfill formally assumed commitments or obligations, while respecting the rules governing participation and internal dissent.
- Transparency: the plans must be public and accessible, allowing voters, companies, and investors to become familiar with each party's long-term proposals.
- Democratic continuity: the plans will allow the public to understand the parties' long-term projects and assess, at each election, whether they wish to continue the political direction adopted by the government or choose representatives aligned with other proposals.
- Periodic reviews: the plans must be reviewed annually, allowing adjustments to party strategies, targets, and priorities.
- Version history: all previous versions of the plans must remain archived and publicly accessible, ensuring traceability of changes and preventing previous commitments from simply being deleted or replaced without a record.
- Dialogue between parties: the plans will serve as a reference for negotiations and consensus-building among different parties, allowing transparent programmatic agreements to be formed without relying on political negotiations based exclusively on private interests or the allocation of government positions.

## 2. Rationale

The lack of long-term political planning and frequent changes in direction between administrations make it difficult to maintain continuity in public policies and increase uncertainty for the public and the market.

Currently, voters are primarily familiar with proposals presented for a specific term of office, without necessarily having a clear understanding of the parties' long-term vision and the direction they intend to take the country.

The proposal seeks to establish a culture of strategic planning within political parties, ensuring that each organization has a clear and public vision for the country over the next 15 years. The inclusion of measurable targets and indicators makes it possible to monitor progress toward commitments and assess the results achieved.

Another important objective is to provide greater political, economic, and institutional predictability. With plans established in advance, voters, companies, and investors will be able to understand the potential direction of a future government even before elections take place.

The initiative also seeks to reduce electoral fraud, as parties will have formalized and monitorable programmatic commitments, while also encouraging dialogue among different political forces. Instead of relying on backroom deals and ad hoc negotiations to make governments viable, parties will be able to build agreements based on their publicly available proposals and guidelines.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Predictability: greater clarity regarding the political and economic directions each party intends to pursue in the long term.
- Strategic planning: encouraging the development of public policies with a vision of continuity beyond a single term of office.
- Verifiable targets: the ability to monitor progress toward party commitments through objective indicators.
- Transparency: allowing the public to understand and compare the national development plans presented by political parties.
- Administrative continuity: facilitating the continuation of public policies and strategic projects across different administrations.
- Party accountability: establishing formal commitments and obligations between political parties and their members, without requiring full agreement with all proposals.
- Reduction of electoral fraud: narrowing the gap between the commitments presented by political parties and the actions of their elected representatives.
- Institutional dialogue: encouraging programmatic agreements and consensus among parties based on publicly available proposals.
- Economic security: providing greater predictability for companies and investors when making medium- and long-term decisions.
- Democratic participation: allowing voters to understand different development plans for the country and consider their long-term guidelines when choosing their representatives.

## 4. Potential Impacts

- Public: greater predictability regarding the country's direction and more information to support electoral decisions.
- Political parties: the need to develop consistent strategic plans, define measurable targets, and maintain clear programmatic guidelines.
- Government: greater potential for continuity in public policies and structural projects, as well as tools for assessing results.
- Market: greater political and economic predictability, supporting long-term investment decisions.
- Political system: encouraging programmatic agreements between parties, reducing dependence on exclusively ad hoc political negotiations.
- Democracy: strengthening electoral choices based on national development plans and long-term guidelines, while preserving room for internal disagreements.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

- Plan requirements: establish minimum requirements for developing party plans, ensuring that they are objective, comprehensive, and verifiable.
- Targets and indicators: define criteria to ensure that commitments can be monitored, measured, and assessed over time.
- Acknowledgment by party members: define procedures to formally document awareness and acknowledgment of the plan by all members, without requiring full agreement.
- Accountability: establish rules requiring party bylaws to provide for consequences when assumed obligations are not fulfilled.
- Party autonomy: reconcile the mandatory nature of the plans with the autonomy of political parties guaranteed by the Federal Constitution.
- Periodic updates: ensure that annual reviews allow proposals to evolve without compromising continuity and predictability.
- Historical preservation: keep all previous versions of the plans archived and available for public consultation.
- Oversight: define how the development, publication, review, measurement of results, and maintenance of the plans' version history will be verified.
- Democratic freedom: preserve internal debate, differences of opinion, and the possibility of evolving party proposals.
- Planning horizon: structure 15-year plans that can span different terms of office and changes in government.

## 6. Open Questions

- How should the mandatory minimum content of the plans be structured?
- What criteria should guide the definition and assessment of targets and indicators?
- Which body will be responsible for overseeing the development, publication, updating, and monitoring of the plans?
- How can party obligations be reconciled with the autonomy of elected representatives in the exercise of their mandates?
- What consequences may be established in party bylaws for failure to fulfill commitments while preserving the right to internal dissent?
