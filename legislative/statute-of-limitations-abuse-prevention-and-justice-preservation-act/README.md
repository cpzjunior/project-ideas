# Statute of Limitations Abuse Prevention and Justice Preservation Act

## 1. Idea

Create legislation establishing that the existence of lawful, authentic, intact, and sufficient material evidence to demonstrate legally relevant facts shall prevent the mere passage of time from being used as grounds to preclude their judicial examination.

The proposal seeks to revise traditional statutes of limitation criteria, establishing that the loss of the ability to judicially enforce a right shall not occur solely due to the passage of time when valid evidence still exists to allow the facts to be examined and ensure the right to an adversarial proceeding and a full defense.

The legislation will be based on four main principles:

1. Preservation of the right to report and seek accountability

A person's right to report, challenge, or seek judicial accountability for an event shall not be eliminated simply because a certain period has elapsed, provided that valid and sufficient evidence still exists to allow the case to be examined.

The application of statutes of limitation, when valid evidence remains preserved, may restrict the complainant's right to seek judicial recognition of a violation.

The proposal seeks to prevent time, by itself, from being used to make the judicial examination of facts that remain verifiable legally impossible.

2. Prevention of statutes of limitation being used as a procedural strategy

The legislation shall prevent statutes of limitation from being deliberately used as a defense strategy to avoid a judicial ruling on the facts.

It shall not be possible to obtain a legal advantage exclusively by artificially prolonging proceedings, filing successive dilatory motions, or engaging in other conduct intended to allow limitation periods to expire.

Delays caused by the judicial system itself shall also not prejudice those who timely sought judicial relief and submitted evidence to substantiate their rights.

The proposal shall specifically consider:

- Appeals and procedural motions used with a predominantly dilatory purpose.
- Tactics intended to prevent or delay service of process and notifications.
- Procedural delays attributable to the operation of the Judiciary itself.
- Situations in which the interested party has already submitted sufficient evidence but cannot obtain a ruling due to the duration of the proceedings.

3. Guarantee of the right to a full defense

The preservation of the possibility of judicial examination while valid evidence exists shall benefit complainants, accused persons, plaintiffs, and defendants equally.

Statutes of limitation shall not be used to prevent a person from seeking judicial recognition of their innocence, challenging an accusation, disputing an obligation, or obtaining recognition of a right.

The existence of preserved evidence shall allow both parties to present their arguments and evidentiary materials, regardless of the time elapsed.

The proposal seeks to prevent a person from being barred from exercising their right to a defense or seeking judicial redress solely because a limitation period has expired, even when the elements necessary to examine the case remain available.

4. Validity of evidence as a criterion for judicial examination

The possibility of judicial examination shall take into account the existence and quality of the evidence rather than depend exclusively on previously established time limits.

For this purpose, the following shall be considered:

- Authenticity of documents and records.
- Integrity of the information.
- Lawfulness of the evidence acquisition.
- Possibility of independent verification.
- Sufficiency of the elements to demonstrate the alleged facts.
- Existence of subsequent events that may modify or extinguish the right or obligation.
- Possibility of effectively exercising the right to an adversarial proceeding and a full defense.

The existence of isolated evidence shall not automatically mean that a complaint or claim is well-founded. The objective is to ensure that the case can be judicially examined as long as adequate evidentiary conditions remain.

## 2. Rationale

Statutes of limitation are a traditional legal instrument based on legal certainty, the stability of social relations, and the need to prevent disputes from remaining indefinitely subject to judicial examination.

However, their application may create situations in which the passage of time becomes more important than the existence of evidence capable of demonstrating the facts.

This may harm individuals whose rights have been violated and who, despite possessing documents, records, or other valid evidentiary elements, are prevented from seeking a judicial ruling because the limitation period has expired.

Another problem is the possibility of using statutes of limitation as a procedural strategy.

In certain situations, one party may have a direct interest in prolonging a legal dispute, filing successive motions, or hindering the progress of proceedings in the expectation that the passage of time will produce a legal advantage.

This creates an inappropriate incentive: instead of focusing efforts on establishing the facts and resolving the dispute, one party may find an advantage in preventing a judgment from being issued.

Delays within the judicial system itself may also have harmful consequences for those seeking justice. It does not seem appropriate for a person who timely filed a claim to be prejudiced by the State's inability to conclude proceedings within a reasonable time.

Furthermore, statutes of limitation may also restrict the exercise of the right to a full defense.

A person who wishes to demonstrate their innocence, challenge an accusation, or obtain recognition that a particular obligation does not exist may encounter time-related obstacles even when they possess preserved evidence to support their position.

The proposal is based on the understanding that legal certainty should not depend exclusively on the expiration of time limits, but also on the ability to preserve evidence, guarantee adversarial proceedings, and allow effective judicial examination.

With technological advances, it has become possible to preserve documents, contracts, bank records, electronic communications, digital signatures, and other evidentiary materials for very long periods.

Therefore, the traditional justification that the passage of time necessarily makes evidence unavailable does not apply indiscriminately to all situations.

The proposal does not seek to eliminate the need for legal certainty, but rather to establish that the passage of time alone should not be sufficient to prevent the examination of facts that remain provable.

## 3. Expected benefits

- Protection of complainants: preservation of the ability to seek judicial recognition of violations as long as valid evidence exists.
- Guarantee of the right to a full defense: possibility of challenging accusations, demonstrating innocence, and disputing obligations even after traditional limitation periods have elapsed.
- Combating procedural impunity: reduction of opportunities to use statutes of limitation as a means of avoiding accountability.
- Prevention of dilatory tactics: elimination of incentives to use the passage of time as a strategy for obtaining procedural advantages.
- Evidence-based justice: greater emphasis on examining facts and evidentiary materials when resolving disputes.
- Protection against judicial delays: reduction of harm caused to parties by delays attributable to the State itself.
- Evidentiary security: encouragement of the preservation of documents, records, and digital evidence.
- Procedural equality: balanced treatment of the interests of those filing complaints and those who need to defend themselves.
- Greater effectiveness of the Judiciary: possibility of examining legally relevant situations that might currently be dismissed exclusively because of the passage of time.
- Reduction of decisions without consideration of the merits: fewer situations in which matters are not judicially examined for exclusively time-related reasons.

## 4. Potential impacts

Positive impacts:

- Reduction in the possibility of strategically using statutes of limitation.
- Greater protection for complainants and individuals harmed by violations.
- Strengthening of adversarial proceedings and the right to a full defense.
- Greater incentive to resolve disputes based on evidence.
- Reduction of advantages arising from deliberate procedural delays.
- Greater protection against failures and delays within the judicial system.
- Expansion of opportunities to review legally questionable situations.
- Greater use of preserved documentary and digital evidence.
- Reduction of situations in which provable facts are not examined exclusively because of the passage of time.

Negative impacts or risks:

- Possibility of an increase in the number of lawsuits and judicial proceedings.
- Risk of legal disputes remaining open indefinitely.
- Need for strict criteria to assess the authenticity, integrity, and sufficiency of evidence.
- Possibility of difficulties in mounting a defense when supplementary evidence has not been preserved.
- Risk of using old, incomplete, or decontextualized documents.
- Need to establish limits to prevent unsupported allegations from keeping disputes indefinitely open.
- Possible increase in the costs of preserving and storing documents and records.
- Need to ensure compatibility with constitutional guarantees, fundamental rights, and legal institutions such as res judicata.

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

The proposal may be developed through amendments to federal legislation, particularly the Civil Code and relevant procedural rules.

However, its application must take into account the particularities of different areas of law, especially the differences between civil, criminal, administrative, and tax limitation periods.

The main challenges are:

- Criteria for evidence validity: establish objective parameters to verify the authenticity, integrity, lawfulness, and sufficiency of evidentiary materials.
- Burden of proof: preserve each party's responsibility to demonstrate the facts supporting their allegations.
- Adversarial proceedings and the right to a full defense: ensure that the opposing party has an effective opportunity to know, challenge, and produce evidence.
- Preservation of legal certainty: establish criteria to prevent unsupported allegations from keeping disputes indefinitely open.
- Dilatory tactics: establish mechanisms to prevent conduct deliberately intended to prolong proceedings from producing limitation-related advantages.
- Judicial delays: ensure that delays attributable exclusively to the operation of the Judiciary do not prejudice parties who have timely exercised their rights.
- Digital evidence: establish criteria for the use of electronic records, digital documents, electronic signatures, and other materials whose integrity can be verified.
- Subsequent events: allow the submission of evidence of payment, settlement, set-off, extinguishment of obligations, or other events that modify the legal situation originally demonstrated.
- Res judicata: preserve the stability of final judicial decisions, establishing specific treatment for exceptional cases involving judicial review.
- Criminal law: assess the constitutional limits of the proposal, considering that the non-applicability of criminal statutes of limitation is subject to constitutional restrictions and cannot be freely expanded through ordinary legislation.
- Administrative and tax law: assess the specific limitation and forfeiture rules applicable to each legal framework.

The legislation shall distinguish between the loss of a claim due to the interested party's unjustified inaction and the inability to exercise a right caused by external circumstances, especially delays by the State or dilatory conduct by the opposing party.

The proposal shall also assess the application of transitional rules, preventing legislative changes from creating legal uncertainty in situations that have already been settled.

## 6. Open issues

- Determine whether the preservation of claims will apply to all legal relationships or whether specific regimes will be established for different areas of law.
- Establish objective criteria to determine when evidence is sufficient to allow judicial examination.
- Determine whether the existence of authentic documentary evidence will be sufficient or whether a preliminary assessment of evidentiary sufficiency will be required.
- Establish how to handle situations in which some evidence has been preserved but other important evidence has disappeared.
- Define mechanisms to prevent appeals and procedural motions from being used with a predominantly dilatory purpose.
- Assess the possibility of applying the proposal to proceedings that have already commenced.
- Establish specific treatment for situations in which the expiration of limitation periods results from delays attributable exclusively to the Judiciary.
- Define specific criteria for civil, criminal, administrative, and tax proceedings.
- Assess the constitutional limits relating to the non-applicability of criminal statutes of limitation.
- Establish rules for the preservation and validation of digital evidence.
- Determine how the proposal will interact with forfeiture periods, res judicata, and other legal institutions that establish time limits for the exercise of rights.
- Assess mechanisms to prevent the proposal from being used to reopen indefinitely disputes that have already been definitively resolved.
