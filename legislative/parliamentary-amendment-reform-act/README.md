# Parliamentary Amendment Reform Act

## 1. Idea

Create legislation establishing mechanisms to gradually reduce the volume of funds allocated to parliamentary amendments, replace discretionary allocation with public lottery mechanisms, and ensure full transparency regarding the origin, destination, and execution of public funds.

The proposal seeks to preserve the Legislative Branch's participation in budget preparation and the mandatory execution of amendments, while reducing opportunities for political negotiations, concentration of power, and the use of public funds as instruments of parliamentary favoritism.

The objective is not to eliminate parliamentary amendments, but to establish a model in which their allocation is impartial, predictable, and independent of political relationships between the Executive and Legislative Branches.

The legislation will be based on four main rules:

### 1. Gradual reduction of the parliamentary amendment budget

Establish an annual 1% reduction in the overall limit of funds allocated to parliamentary amendments.

The reduction shall follow a simple and predictable rule:

- The overall amendment limit shall be reduced by 1% annually, using the limit from the previous fiscal year as the reference.
- The reduction shall be cumulative, using the already reduced limit from the previous year as the basis.
- The rule shall account for real values, adjusting for inflation, to prevent nominal increases from offsetting the intended reduction.
- The reduction shall cover all parliamentary amendment categories, preventing a reduction in one category from being offset by an increase in another.
- The mandatory execution of amendments shall be preserved within the applicable constitutional and legal limits.

The gradual reduction aims to progressively decrease the weight of parliamentary amendments in the public budget without imposing abrupt cuts or excessively compromising the ability to address regional needs.

### 2. Allocation of funds through public lotteries

Replace discretionary fund allocation mechanisms with a public, random, and auditable lottery system.

The proposal is based on the understanding that the allocation of public funds should not depend on the political influence of parliamentarians, party leaders, or their relationship with the Executive Branch.

The lottery serves an important institutional purpose: preventing the government from using control over budget execution to favor allied parliamentarians or hinder the execution of proposals submitted by opposition members.

Historically, dependence on political decisions regarding fund allocation has created opportunities for non-transparent negotiations between the Executive and Legislative Branches. The secret budget, associated with rapporteur amendments (RP9), demonstrated the risks of a model in which the nomination and allocation of funds lack sufficient transparency and clear criteria.

The proposal seeks to eliminate this type of distortion by replacing discretionary selection with an impartial mechanism.

Its operation shall comply with the following principles:

- Parliamentarians may continue submitting amendment proposals.
- All proposals shall undergo uniform technical and legal assessment criteria.
- Eligible projects shall participate in a public lottery to allocate the available funds.
- The lottery shall use a previously published methodology, an auditable algorithm, and mechanisms to prevent manipulation.
- The results shall be public and binding, subject to budgetary limits and legal and technical restrictions.
- No parliamentarian may receive preferential treatment or be disadvantaged because of their political position, party, or alignment with the government.
- The identities of the authors may be publicly disclosed without affecting the lottery results or the execution of the funds.

The lottery shall replace discretionary political selection, not the Legislative Branch's participation in submitting budgetary requests.

The mandatory execution of amendments shall be preserved, subject to duly justified technical and legal restrictions.

**The purpose of the lottery is not to randomly choose where public money will be spent, but to prevent its allocation from depending on political relationships or discretionary decisions.** Projects will remain subject to technical criteria, while selection among eligible proposals will take place through an impartial and verifiable mechanism.

### 3. Full transparency of parliamentary amendments

Every parliamentary amendment shall have publicly available information identifying its origin, destination, and execution.

The information shall include:

- Name of the parliamentarian responsible for the proposal.
- Political party and state represented.
- Amount originally requested.
- Amount approved and effectively allocated.
- Beneficiary municipality, government agency, or entity.
- Specific purpose of the funds.
- Project or activity being funded.
- Contracts and suppliers involved.
- Execution schedule.
- Amounts actually paid.
- Results and financial reporting.

The following rules shall also be established:

- Prohibition of informal allocations without identifying the responsible parliamentarian.
- Prohibition of transfers that prevent the origin of funds from being traced.
- Disclosure of lottery results and eligibility criteria.
- Public justification for any impediment to the execution of a selected amendment.
- Availability of information through a public platform, with accessible and regularly updated data.

No public funds may be allocated or executed without their origin, purpose, and beneficiary being identifiable.

Transparency shall allow any citizen to identify who proposed the allocation of funds, who benefited from it, and how the money was actually used.

### 4. Objective criteria for execution impediments

The mandatory execution of amendments shall be preserved, preventing the Executive Branch from using budget execution as an instrument of political negotiation.

To achieve this:

- The Executive Branch may only prevent execution for technical, legal, or fiscal reasons established by law.
- All justifications shall be formally documented and made publicly available.
- The criteria shall be uniform, regardless of the parliamentarian responsible.
- Oversight bodies may verify the legitimacy of execution impediments.
- Differences in political positions, party affiliation, or support for the government shall not be used to justify unequal treatment.

The intention is to ensure that amendment execution depends on objective requirements rather than political convenience.

## 2. Motivation

The current parliamentary amendment model allows a significant portion of the public budget to be allocated through individual or collective decisions made by members of the Legislative Branch.

Although parliamentary participation in budget preparation is legitimate and constitutionally established, the significant growth in the volume of amendments and the concentration of power over their allocation create problems related to administrative efficiency, transparency, and the balance between the branches of government.

One of the main problems is the use of budget execution as an instrument for political negotiation between the Executive and Legislative Branches.

When the release of funds depends on discretionary government decisions, parliamentarians may be favored or disadvantaged according to their political alignment. This creates a relationship of dependence that is incompatible with the independence that should exist between the branches of government.

### The precedent of the secret budget

A relevant example of the problems with the current model was the so-called secret budget, associated with rapporteur amendments (RP9).

This mechanism allowed public funds to be allocated without adequate transparency regarding the identities of the parliamentarians responsible for the proposals and the criteria used to distribute the amounts.

The case demonstrated how a lack of transparency and the concentration of power over budget allocation can undermine public oversight and encourage non-transparent political negotiations between the branches of government.

Although the Federal Supreme Court declared the original rapporteur amendment model unconstitutional in 2022, the structural problem of political influence over fund allocation remains relevant.

Simply prohibiting a specific amendment category does not prevent similar practices from being reproduced through other nomination and allocation mechanisms.

For this reason, the proposal does not merely seek to increase transparency or create new identification requirements. It aims to change the very logic of fund allocation by eliminating the possibility of discretionary political selection.

### The lottery as a mechanism for independence between the branches of government

The adoption of public lotteries serves an institutional purpose that goes beyond randomness.

The Executive Branch should not have instruments to reward allied parliamentarians or hinder the execution of proposals submitted by opposition members.

Likewise, the allocation of funds should not depend on agreements between party leaders or individual negotiations to obtain funding.

The lottery seeks to eliminate this possibility by replacing political influence with an impartial, predetermined mechanism.

Parliamentarians will continue to be able to submit requests and projects, but the selection of successful projects will not depend on their proximity to the government or their political negotiating power.

The mechanism also reduces the need for anonymity regarding the authorship of proposals. Since selection will not depend on discretionary political decisions, the identities of parliamentarians may be fully disclosed, allowing society to oversee the origin of the proposals.

In this way, the proposal seeks to reconcile three objectives:

- Preserve the Legislative Branch's autonomy from the Executive Branch.
- Ensure full transparency regarding the use of public funds.
- Prevent the allocation of amendments from being used as an instrument of political negotiation.

### Reduction in the volume of funds

Another problem is the continuous growth of the budget allocated to parliamentary amendments.

The expansion of these funds reduces the resources available for centralized planning of public policies and increases the individual influence of parliamentarians over budget allocation.

The annual 1% reduction establishes a gradual downward trajectory, allowing the weight of amendments to decrease without imposing abrupt cuts or excessively compromising the ability to address local needs.

The proposal also recognizes that mandatory amendment execution serves an institutional purpose: preventing the Executive Branch from using control over budget execution to exert political pressure on the Legislative Branch.

For this reason, mandatory execution will be preserved, provided that applicable technical, legal, and fiscal criteria are respected.

Finally, full transparency will allow society to monitor the origin and use of funds, identifying those responsible for the proposals and the results actually achieved.

## 3. Expected benefits

- **Reduction in public spending:** gradual annual 1% reduction in the budget allocated to parliamentary amendments.
- **Fiscal savings:** progressive reduction in the volume of funds committed to amendments, allowing greater budget availability for other priorities.
- **Impartial allocation:** replacement of discretionary selection with public and auditable lotteries.
- **Independence between the branches of government:** reduced possibility of using amendments as an instrument of political negotiation between the Executive and Legislative Branches.
- **Parliamentary equality:** application of uniform criteria to all parliamentarians.
- **Institutional preservation:** maintenance of the Legislative Branch's participation in budget preparation and the mandatory execution of amendments.
- **Transparency:** full identification of authorship, beneficiaries, and the allocation of funds.
- **Combating the secret budget:** elimination of proposals without public identification of their responsible authors and reduction of opportunities for alternative mechanisms of political allocation.
- **Budget predictability:** establishment of permanent rules for reducing and allocating funds.
- **Easier oversight:** availability of standardized information for oversight bodies and the public.
- **Reduction of political bargaining:** decreased opportunities for individual negotiations over the allocation of public funds.
- **Administrative efficiency:** encouragement of the selection of projects that meet technical criteria and address actual needs.

## 4. Potential impacts

**Positive impacts:**

- Gradual reduction in expenditures allocated to parliamentary amendments.
- Greater availability of funds for other public policies and national priorities.
- Reduced political influence over fund allocation.
- Reduced dependence of parliamentarians on the Executive Branch for the execution of their amendments.
- Greater equality of opportunities among parliamentarians.
- Strengthening of transparency and traceability in public spending.
- Reduced opportunities for political favoritism in fund allocation.
- Greater predictability regarding the amounts available for amendments.
- Preservation of the Legislative Branch's ability to submit regional demands.
- Greater ease in identifying irregularities in the nomination and execution of funds.
- Reduced possibility of reproducing mechanisms similar to the secret budget.
- Strengthening of institutional independence between the Executive and Legislative Branches.

**Negative impacts or risks:**

- Possibility that technically relevant projects will not be selected through the lottery.
- Need for strict criteria for project eligibility.
- Risk of regional or sectoral concentration of funds in certain lotteries, depending on the design of the mechanism.
- Possibility of excessive proposal submissions to increase the chances of selection.
- Need to adapt budgetary and transparency systems.
- Political resistance from parliamentarians who currently have influence over fund allocation.
- Possibility of conflicts between lottery-based allocation and the constitutional rules governing mandatory amendment execution.
- Need to reconcile the annual reduction with existing constitutional minimums.

## 5. Feasibility and challenges

The proposal may be implemented through a set of legislative amendments involving the legislation governing parliamentary amendments and, if necessary, the Federal Constitution.

The main challenges are:

- **Budget reduction:** establish a permanent rule for a 1% annual real reduction, accounting for inflation and preventing nominal increases from neutralizing the intended decrease.
- **Constitutional limits:** assess the need to amend Article 166 of the Federal Constitution to allow the gradual reduction of the currently established mandatory limits.
- **Compatibility with mandatory execution:** ensure that the lottery does not remove constitutionally guaranteed execution rights for individual or party caucus amendments.
- **Lottery model:** define how funds will be allocated among eligible projects, considering amounts, categories, and budgetary limits.
- **Technical criteria:** establish objective eligibility requirements for proposals, preventing favoritism or arbitrary exclusions.
- **Randomness:** develop a public lottery system with an open algorithm, verifiable methodology, and independent auditing.
- **Prevention of manipulation:** ensure that lottery rules are published before proposals are submitted and approved.
- **Regional allocation:** assess mechanisms to prevent excessive concentration of funds in certain regions without compromising the randomness of the process.
- **Execution impediments:** establish objective circumstances and mandatory justifications for preventing the execution of selected projects.
- **Transparency:** integrate information on authorship, lotteries, beneficiaries, contracts, and execution into public platforms.
- **Oversight:** allow the Federal Court of Accounts (TCU), Office of the Comptroller General (CGU), and other competent authorities to audit the lotteries and the use of funds.
- **Accountability:** establish consequences for lottery manipulation, submission of false information, and improper use of funds.

The annual 1% reduction shall apply to the overall amendment limit, preventing different categories from being used to artificially offset the reduction.

The formula shall account for the cumulative reduction based on the previous fiscal year's limit, in real terms.

The lottery shall replace discretionary selection of beneficiaries or projects while respecting the constitutional powers of Congress and the mandatory execution of the amendment categories established in the Constitution.

The legislation shall preserve the ability of all parliamentarians to submit requests, without allowing the Executive Branch to use political criteria to determine which proposals will be executed.

## 6. Open issues

- Determine whether the 1% reduction will apply to the overall amendment limit or separately to each category.
- Define the methodology for calculating the annual real reduction, accounting for inflation.
- Assess the need for a constitutional amendment to change the limits on parliamentary amendments.
- Determine whether the lottery will be conducted by project, financial amount, or value ranges.
- Define how projects with different costs will be handled within the same lottery process.
- Establish minimum technical criteria for proposal eligibility.
- Determine whether there will be limits on the concentration of funds by municipality, state, or region.
- Establish how to prevent the same parliamentarian from artificially increasing their chances through the submission of multiple equivalent projects.
- Define the methodology for generating and auditing random numbers.
- Assess the possibility of using an independent platform to conduct the lotteries.
- Define the participation of the TCU and CGU in overseeing the procedures.
- Establish which technical, legal, and fiscal impediments may justify the non-execution of a selected project.
- Define deadlines for technical assessments, lotteries, and fund execution.
- Establish rules for cases in which a selected project becomes unfeasible after the lottery.
- Assess how to reconcile the lottery with individual amendments, party caucus amendments, and other existing categories.
- Determine whether lottery criteria will also apply to special transfers, known as PIX amendments.
