# National Remote Work Incentive Program

## 1. Proposal

Establish a national program to encourage Brazilian companies to adopt telework through tax incentives granted to legal entities that implement remote work arrangements.

The proposal provides for two levels of incentives:

- Predominantly remote work arrangement: a 1% discount on the amount of the tax designated for the program payable by companies in which at least 50% plus one of their employees work entirely under a remote work arrangement.
- Hybrid work arrangement: a 0.5% discount on the amount payable under the same tax by companies that formally adopt a hybrid work model, provided that the employees counted toward eligibility work remotely at least three days per week.

The percentages represent proportional discounts on the amount of tax payable, rather than reductions in percentage points of the applicable tax rate.

Eligibility for the benefit must be conditional upon evidence of employees' actual work arrangements, so that merely including telework provisions in employment contracts is not sufficient to qualify.

The objective is to use tax policy as an instrument to encourage a reduction in daily commuting, contributing to urban sustainability, improved quality of life for workers, and greater operational efficiency for companies.

## 2. Rationale

The daily commute of millions of workers represents a significant source of pressure on Brazil's urban infrastructure.

Daily travel between homes and workplaces contributes to traffic congestion, air pollution, fuel consumption, and increased demand for public investment in urban mobility.

The expansion of telework has the potential to mitigate these impacts by reducing the number of necessary commutes and enabling more efficient use of existing infrastructure.

In addition to collective benefits, remote work can provide significant individual benefits, particularly by reducing commuting time. This additional time can be devoted to family, personal, and leisure activities, contributing to workers' quality of life.

From a corporate perspective, adopting telework can also reduce expenses related to office maintenance, physical infrastructure, energy, and other operating costs.

Despite these benefits, the transition to remote or hybrid work models depends on individual corporate decisions, which require companies to consider their respective costs and advantages. The tax incentive seeks to make this choice more economically attractive, aligning private interests with social and environmental benefits.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- Reduced traffic congestion: fewer commutes during peak hours.
- Environmental benefits: potential reductions in pollutant emissions and fuel consumption.
- Improved quality of life: less time spent commuting and more time available for personal and family activities.
- Lower corporate costs: incentives to optimize physical office space and operating expenses.
- Greater urban efficiency: reduced pressure on transportation systems and urban infrastructure.
- Economic decentralization: greater opportunities to hire professionals residing outside major urban centers.
- Modernization of work practices: incentives to adopt digital tools and results-oriented management processes.

## 4. Potential Impacts

### Positive Impacts

- Lower concentration of commuting during peak hours.
- Potential reduction in public spending associated with expanding urban mobility infrastructure.
- Lower operating costs for participating companies.
- Greater flexibility in work organization.
- Expanded employment opportunities for professionals residing in different regions.
- Potential reduction in demand for large commercial and corporate office spaces.

### Negative Impacts or Limitations

- Tax revenue losses resulting from the discounts granted.
- Benefits limited to economic activities compatible with telework.
- Potential distortions in work organization if companies prioritize tax eligibility over operational considerations.
- Difficulty in verifying the effective adoption of remote work arrangements.
- Potential impacts on economic activities dependent on the daily circulation of workers, particularly in commercial and business districts.
- Need to prevent companies from improperly replacing on-site employment arrangements with formal telework contracts solely to qualify for the benefit.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

### 5.1. Eligibility Criteria

Eligibility for the benefit must be determined based on the proportion of employees actually working under each arrangement.

To qualify under the predominantly remote work arrangement, a company must have at least 50% plus one of its employees working entirely under a telework arrangement.

To qualify under the hybrid work arrangement, the employees counted toward eligibility must work remotely at least three days per week, while maintaining on-site activities on the remaining days.

The criteria must take into account the number of employees and their actual remote work frequency, rather than merely the number of employment contracts formally classified as telework arrangements.

### 5.2. Verification of Work Arrangements

Participating companies must maintain records demonstrating their employees' actual work arrangements.

Verification may include employment contracts, internal policies, attendance records, and other relevant documentation.

Oversight mechanisms must prevent the benefit from being granted solely on the basis of formal declarations that do not reflect actual working arrangements.

### 5.3. Application of the Tax Benefit

The program will grant the following discounts:

- 1% discount on the amount of tax payable for companies with at least 50% plus one of their employees working entirely under a telework arrangement.
- 0.5% discount on the amount of tax payable for companies whose employees counted toward eligibility work remotely at least three days per week.

The percentages are fixed and represent proportional discounts on the total amount of tax payable, not reductions in percentage points of the applicable tax rate.

The tax and the mechanism for granting the benefit will be defined at a later stage, taking into account legal suitability, economic efficiency, and the feasibility of implementation within the Brazilian tax system.

### 5.4. Oversight and Fraud Prevention

The program must establish oversight mechanisms to prevent:

- False declarations of employees working remotely.
- Artificial amendments to employment contracts without any actual change in work arrangements.
- Improper inclusion of employees in the calculation of the minimum required percentage.
- Granting of the hybrid work benefit to employees who do not meet the minimum requirement of three remote workdays per week.
- Improper retention of the benefit after eligibility criteria are no longer met.

Failure to comply with the requirements must result in the loss of the benefit and, where applicable, repayment of amounts improperly claimed, plus applicable statutory charges.

### 5.5. Results Assessment

The program must be monitored through indicators that allow its results to be assessed, including:

- Number of participating companies.
- Number of workers covered.
- Estimated reduction in commuting.
- Impact on tax revenue.
- Regional distribution of beneficiaries.
- Changes in the adoption of remote and hybrid work arrangements.

Periodic assessments will make it possible to determine whether the environmental, social, and economic benefits justify the tax cost of the initiative.

## 6. Open Issues

- Determine the most appropriate tax for granting the benefit and the corresponding implementation mechanism.
- Define how part-time employees, employees on leave, and employees in special circumstances will be treated.
- Determine how frequently the number of employees under each work arrangement must be calculated.
- Establish criteria for verifying the requirement of three remote workdays per week.
- Define how public holidays, vacations, leaves of absence, and weeks with fewer business days will be treated.
- Assess the possibility of establishing maximum benefit limits per company.
- Define how companies whose activities require physical presence, such as manufacturing, hospitals, retail, and operational services, will be treated.
- Estimate the tax revenue foregone and assess the need for budgetary offsetting measures.
- Define the initial validity period and criteria for reviewing or renewing the program.
- Assess the constitutional and tax law compatibility of the benefit, including restrictions applicable to the creation of differentiated tax regimes.
