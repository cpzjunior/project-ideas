# National Program to Incentivize Small Businesses and Entrepreneurship

## 1. Idea

Create a national tax and economic incentive program primarily aimed at Individual Microentrepreneurs (MEIs), microenterprises, and small businesses.

The objective is to encourage entrepreneurship, job creation, workforce professionalization, technological modernization, and the formalization of economic activities.

The proposal is based on the principle that the government can encourage economically beneficial behavior through incentives rather than creating new obligations and oversight structures.

The program will be divided into six independent categories, allowing each business to voluntarily choose those best suited to its circumstances.

### 1.1. Entrepreneurship Incentive

Allow workers to use part of their available FGTS (Severance Indemnity Fund) balance to start a business.

Proposed rules:

- Possibility of withdrawing up to 50% of the available balance.
    
- Use of funds to establish companies and provide initial business infrastructure.
    
- Application of funds toward equipment, facilities, licenses, systems, initial inventory, and working capital.
    
- Initially, limited to one use per CPF (Individual Taxpayer Registration Number).
    
- Release of funds upon proof of formal business registration.
    
- Possibility of releasing funds in stages, according to the formalization and implementation of the business.
    

The measure seeks to transform part of the resources accumulated by workers into productive capital, expanding entrepreneurship opportunities.

### 1.2. First Employment Incentive

Grant tax reductions to small businesses that hire workers for their first formal job.

The proposed benefit will be a 1% reduction in the tax due.

The hiring must represent an actual employment relationship, duly registered in official systems.

No minimum employment period will be required to maintain the incentive. The company must retain its freedom to manage and evaluate employee performance, in compliance with labor legislation.

Regular termination of employment will not result in automatic repayment of the benefit.

Fictitious hires, sham arrangements, or fraudulent agreements intended to obtain the incentive will be subject to repayment of the amounts received and applicable penalties.

### 1.3. Young Apprentice Incentive

Encourage the voluntary hiring of young apprentices by microenterprises and small businesses.

The proposed benefit will be a 1% reduction in the tax due.

This category should prioritize additional hires made voluntarily by companies that are not legally required to meet apprenticeship quotas.

Participation must comply with professional apprenticeship legislation, including training and supervision requirements.

The incentive must not replace existing legal obligations.

### 1.4. Professional Development Incentive

Grant tax reductions to small businesses that invest in the training and professional development of their workers.

The proposed benefit will be a 1% reduction in the tax due.

Eligible expenses will include:

- Technical and vocational courses.
    
- Professional certifications.
    
- Training in technology and automation.
    
- Financial and business management training.
    
- Productivity training.
    
- Training in artificial intelligence and digital tools.
    
- Professional skills development programs.
    

Courses must have a demonstrable connection to the company's activities or the employee's professional development.

Proof will be provided through tax documents, enrollment records, and certificates or proof of participation.

### 1.5. Technological Modernization Incentive

Encourage small business investments in technology, automation, and tools that increase productivity.

The proposed benefit will be a 1.5% reduction in the tax due.

Eligible investments will include:

- Integrated business management systems.
    
- Automation of administrative and operational processes.
    
- Artificial intelligence applied to business activities.
    
- Robotics and automated equipment.
    
- Financial, inventory, and production control systems.
    
- Digitization of operations.
    
- Equipment and software that reduce costs and increase efficiency.
    

The incentive will be granted upon proof of acquisition and use of the resources in business activities.

### 1.6. Formalization Incentive

Encourage the formalization of self-employed workers and small businesses that currently operate informally.

The proposed benefit will be a 1% reduction in the tax due, following formalization and compliance with the program's requirements.

The following may also be offered:

- Simplified accounting guidance.
    
- Assistance with registration regularization.
    
- Guidance on tax obligations.
    
- Facilitation of access to financial services.
    
- Assistance with issuing invoices and financial organization.
    

This category should prioritize lower-income entrepreneurs and small businesses.

## 2. Rationale

Microenterprises, small businesses, and MEIs represent an important share of Brazilian economic activity but frequently face difficulties investing, hiring, training workers, and modernizing their operations.

Small business owners have less financial and administrative capacity to absorb the costs of hiring, training, technology, and formalization.

At the same time, the government has an economic and social interest in encouraging business creation, formal job generation, and increased productivity.

The proposal seeks to establish an incentive-based relationship between the public sector and small business owners.

Rather than creating new general obligations, the program offers economic advantages to companies that voluntarily contribute to objectives of public interest.

The partial use of FGTS funds for entrepreneurship also seeks to expand the options available for using workers' resources, allowing part of their accumulated assets to be directed toward creating productive business activities.

## 3. Expected Benefits

- **Entrepreneurship:** expanded access to initial capital for starting small businesses.
    
- **Job Creation:** incentives for hiring workers in their first formal jobs.
    
- **Professional Inclusion:** expanded opportunities for young people and individuals without formal work experience.
    
- **Professional Development:** increased workforce qualifications.
    
- **Modernization:** greater adoption of technology and automation by small businesses.
    
- **Formalization:** reduction of economic informality.
    
- **Productivity:** improvement of business processes and reduction of waste.
    
- **Economic Development:** strengthening of small businesses and local economies.
    
- **Future Tax Revenue:** potential expansion of the tax base through business growth and formalization.
    

## 4. Potential Impacts

### Positive Impacts

- Greater number of formalized small businesses.
    
- Expanded first-job opportunities.
    
- Greater participation of small businesses in professional training.
    
- Increased investment in technology.
    
- Improved business productivity.
    
- Expanded income-generating capacity.
    
- Greater circulation of resources within local economies.
    
- Reduced economic barriers for new entrepreneurs.
    

### Fiscal and Financial Impacts

The program will result in tax revenue foregone, the scale of which will depend on the number of participating companies, the taxes covered, and the percentages effectively granted.

The use of FGTS funds for entrepreneurship will also require an assessment of the impact on the fund's available resources and its ability to fulfill its other statutory purposes.

The program must establish financial limits, eligibility criteria, and periodic assessments to ensure fiscal sustainability.

### Tax Reduction Model

The proposed reductions will be applied to the amount of tax due, not to its tax rate.

|Category|Proposed Reduction|
|---|---|
|First Employment|1%|
|Voluntary Young Apprentice|1%|
|Professional Development|1%|
|Technological Modernization|1.5%|
|Formalization|1%|

Companies may combine benefits from different categories, subject to an overall limit of a 5% reduction in the tax due.

The percentages are initial parameters and must be subject to fiscal impact studies.

Implementation must account for the different tax regimes. For companies opting for Simples Nacional, a specific mechanism compatible with its unified tax collection system will need to be established.

## 5. Feasibility and Challenges

### 5.1. Priority Target Audience

The program will primarily target:

- Individual Microentrepreneurs (MEIs).
    
- Microenterprises.
    
- Small businesses.
    

Larger companies will not be the priority audience and may participate only in specific categories if there is an economic and budgetary justification.

The program must distinguish between new and existing businesses:

- New businesses may access incentives related to establishment, formalization, initial hiring, and business setup.
    
- Existing businesses may access incentives related to expansion, hiring, professional development, and modernization.
    

### 5.2. Fraud and Corruption Prevention

Program administration must use objective criteria and automated processes.

The following measures will be adopted:

- Enrollment through a single digital platform.
    
- Automatic verification of registration and tax data.
    
- Cross-referencing of information with the Federal Revenue Service, eSocial, FGTS, and tax documents.
    
- Electronic proof of expenses and hires.
    
- Publication of granted amounts and aggregated results.
    
- Enhanced auditing of atypical transactions.
    
- Prohibition of discretionary approval of beneficiaries by public officials.
    
- Recovery of incentives obtained fraudulently.
    
- Application of legal penalties in cases of fraud, sham arrangements, or document falsification.
    

Expenses involving companies related to the business owners must undergo additional controls to prevent the creation of artificial transactions intended to obtain benefits.

### 5.3. Business Management Freedom

Hiring-related incentives must not require companies to retain a specific employee for a minimum period solely to preserve the benefit.

Entrepreneurs must retain the freedom to evaluate the performance and suitability of hired workers.

Regular termination of employment will not result in automatic repayment of the incentive.

Repayment will be required in cases of fraud, fictitious hiring, sham arrangements, or improper use of the program.

### 5.4. Tax Compatibility

The application of benefits across different tax regimes will need to be assessed, particularly:

- Simples Nacional.
    
- Presumed Profit (Lucro Presumido).
    
- Actual Profit (Lucro Real).
    

The regulations must establish which taxes may receive reductions and how to prevent companies from being disadvantaged by the inability to claim tax offsets under simplified regimes.

### 5.5. Results Assessment

The program must undergo annual evaluation, considering:

- Number of participating companies.
    
- Number of additional jobs created.
    
- Number of workers in their first formal jobs.
    
- Number of young apprentices voluntarily hired.
    
- Number of workers trained.
    
- Investments made in technology.
    
- Number of formalized businesses.
    
- Fiscal cost per result achieved.
    
- Business survival rates after 24 and 36 months.
    

The program should begin with a three-year pilot period, followed by an assessment to determine whether it should be maintained, revised, or expanded.

## 6. Open Issues

- Define the financial limit for using FGTS funds for entrepreneurship.
    
- Assess whether FGTS withdrawals should be released in full or in stages.
    
- Define which taxes may receive incentives.
    
- Establish a specific mechanism for companies under Simples Nacional.
    
- Define financial limits per company and per category.
    
- Assess the possibility of differentiated percentages based on company size and revenue.
    
- Define objective criteria for qualifying as a first formal job.
    
- Establish conditions to distinguish voluntary apprenticeship hires from legally required hires.
    
- Define which institutions and courses will be eligible for the professional development incentive.
    
- Establish criteria for recognizing investments in technological modernization.
    
- Define how the benefit will be calculated for companies with low or no tax due.
    
- Estimate the fiscal impact and the effects on FGTS resources.
    
- Define the government agency responsible for administering and evaluating the program.
