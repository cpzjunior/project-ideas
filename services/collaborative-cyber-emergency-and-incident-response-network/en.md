# Collaborative Cyber Emergency and Incident Response Network

## Description

A collaborative network that connects companies, financial institutions, technology vendors, service providers, business partners and, potentially, public authorities in an environment for communication and coordination during cyber emergencies.

The proposal aims to enable an organization that identifies a significant cyber incident to quickly activate its own response mechanisms and, when necessary, alert other potentially affected organizations, reducing response time and limiting the spread of attacks and the resulting financial losses.

The concept is based on a simple mechanism: each organization has its own **Panic Button**, integrated with its security systems and processes. When activated, it sends a standardized message that can be used internally by the organization itself or forwarded to the network for communication with other participants.

External sharing is not necessarily required. If the incident is limited to the organization that identified it and does not pose a risk to other companies, the alert can be used exclusively to activate its internal emergency systems, teams and procedures.

When there is a potential impact on third parties, the network allows the notification to be distributed to relevant organizations, taking into account their business, operational, financial and technological relationships.

Each participating organization retains autonomy over its systems, data and response procedures. Upon receiving an alert, it can execute its own previously authorized actions, such as blocking transactions, restricting access, suspending integrations, strengthening authentication controls or initiating contingency procedures.

More than a threat intelligence sharing platform, the proposal is to establish a **cyber emergency network**, capable of transforming communication between organizations into a coordinated and virtually immediate response.

The objective is to reduce the time between identifying an attack and implementing defensive measures, narrowing the attackers' window of opportunity to commit fraud, move financial resources or compromise other companies within the relationship network.

The initiative could begin with the financial sector, given the criticality of its operations and the financial impact of incidents, and evolve into a nationwide, cross-sector network involving companies from different industries and public institutions.

## How It Works

Each participating organization implements its own emergency activation mechanism, known as the Panic Button, connected to its monitoring, security, fraud prevention or operational systems.

When an organization identifies a situation that warrants an emergency response, the mechanism sends a standardized message. This message can represent different situations, such as credential compromise, infrastructure intrusion, ongoing fraud, a compromised vendor, an attack on an integration or suspected compromise of a related organization.

The activation can have two scopes:

* **Internal activation:** when the incident is limited to the organization itself or does not pose an identified risk to third parties, the alert is used exclusively to activate internal response systems, teams and processes. In this case, no other organization needs to be notified.
* **Collaborative activation:** when the incident may affect other organizations, the alert is forwarded to the network, which identifies potentially impacted participants and distributes the notification automatically.

The network receives alerts intended for sharing, validates their origin and applies distribution rules to identify which participants should be notified. The objective is to ensure that the information quickly reaches organizations that may suffer consequences from the incident.

Upon receiving an alert, each participant evaluates the information according to its own policies and can automatically activate its local emergency procedures.

The network does not need access to organizations' internal systems or to execute actions directly on their behalf. Its primary function is to enable emergency information to be handled internally or quickly reach the right participants in a format that supports a coordinated response.

The solution can offer features such as:

* **Organizational Panic Button:** each participant's own mechanism for reporting an emergency, activating its internal procedures and, when applicable, sending an alert to the network.
* **Standardized Emergency Protocol:** definition of a common format for incident communication, allowing different organizations to interpret and process alerts automatically.
* **Intelligent Alert Distribution:** routing notifications to potentially affected organizations, taking into account business, operational, financial and technological relationships.
* **Business Relationship Network:** representation of connections between participants, making it possible to identify vendors, customers, partners, financial institutions and service providers that may be impacted by an incident.
* **Real-Time Notifications:** automated communication between participants, with the objective of reducing the time between incident identification and alert delivery.
* **Local Automated Response:** ability for each organization to preconfigure actions that its own systems can execute upon receiving specific types of alerts.
* **Emergency Classification:** categorization of alerts by incident type, severity, criticality, scope and information confidence level.
* **Acknowledgment of Receipt:** recording that an organization has received and processed a particular notification, allowing alert dissemination to be tracked.
* **Response Tracking:** ability to report that an organization has initiated containment, mitigation or investigation measures, while respecting its confidentiality policies.
* **Authentication and Trust Between Participants:** mechanisms to ensure that alerts originate from legitimate organizations and that their distribution complies with the network's participation rules.
* **Controlled Information Sharing:** ability to include technical indicators, evidence and additional information about the incident, respecting confidentiality levels and access criteria.
* **Incident and Notification History:** record of reported emergencies, notified organizations, acknowledgments received and response outcomes, in accordance with retention and confidentiality policies.
* **Network Efficiency Metrics:** monitoring of metrics such as distribution time, acknowledgment time, time to containment and alert coverage.
* **Collaborative Governance:** definition of common rules for participation, responsibilities, trust levels, information handling and network usage.
* **Institutional Integration:** ability to establish relationships with regulatory bodies, industry associations, financial institutions and organizations specializing in cyber incident response.

An example of its use would be a financial institution identifying an intrusion at a vendor that has integrations with several banks.

Upon confirming an emergency situation, the institution activates its Panic Button and sends an alert to the network.

The network identifies the participants connected to the compromised vendor and distributes the notification to potentially affected institutions.

Each bank receives the alert and can implement its own protective measures, such as temporarily suspending the integration, restricting related transactions, requiring additional validations or initiating contingency procedures.

The vendor can also be notified to initiate its own containment measures.

Thus, instead of each organization discovering the problem independently, investigating the situation and initiating individual communications with its partners, the network allows relevant participants to be informed virtually simultaneously.

In another scenario, a company may identify an intrusion in an internal environment without any evidence that vendors, customers or partners have been compromised. In this case, the Panic Button is activated to immediately initiate its internal emergency procedures, without sharing the alert with other organizations.

Another example would be a company identifying the compromise of credentials used in integrations with customers and business partners.

By activating the emergency mechanism, the network distributes the alert to related organizations, allowing each one to block or restrict the use of the compromised credentials before the attacker can exploit all available connections.

## Applications

The solution can serve organizations across different sectors, especially those that rely on digital integrations, business relationships and operations shared with third parties.

The network can include:

| Category | Examples of participants |
|---|---|
| Financial Institutions | Banks, investment banks, credit unions and payment institutions |
| Financial Infrastructure | Payment system operators, clearing houses and financial market infrastructure providers |
| Technology Companies | Cloud providers, data centers, software companies and security solution vendors |
| Telecommunications | Telecom operators, internet service providers and communication infrastructure companies |
| Private Companies | Manufacturers, retailers, service companies, marketplaces and large corporate groups |
| Supply Chains | Vendors, distributors, service providers and business partners |
| Public Sector | Government agencies, public entities and incident response organizations |
| Industry Organizations | Business associations, regulatory bodies and information-sharing organizations |

The solution can be used in different contexts:

* **Internal Incident Response:** rapid activation of internal teams, systems and emergency procedures, even when the incident does not affect other organizations.
* **Financial Institutions:** rapid communication of incidents that may compromise financial operations, integrations, transactions, systems or relationships with third parties.
* **Financial Fraud Prevention:** dissemination of alerts about attacks and compromises that could be used to carry out fraudulent transfers, divert payments or exploit relationships between institutions.
* **Supply Chain Protection:** notification of customers and partners when a vendor or service provider suffers a compromise that may affect other organizations.
* **Corporate Environment Protection:** communication of incidents involving credentials, access, applications, infrastructure or shared services.
* **Technology Companies:** notification of customers about incidents that may affect services, platforms, hosted environments or integrations.
* **Large Corporate Groups:** coordination of responses among subsidiaries, business units, vendors and partners.
* **Critical Operations:** communication of cyber emergencies in sectors such as energy, telecommunications, healthcare, transportation and infrastructure.
* **Cooperation Between Financial Institutions:** creation of an emergency communication mechanism that complements existing incident and fraud information-sharing processes.
* **National Coordination:** ability to connect different economic sectors through a collaborative cyber emergency response network.

From an institutional perspective, there is an opportunity to involve the Central Bank of Brazil, particularly in discussions about cooperation mechanisms and incident response within the national financial system.

The Central Bank could be a relevant institutional interlocutor in assessing the initiative's alignment with the needs of the financial sector, without necessarily becoming an operator or controller of the network.

The long-term vision is to build a collaborative infrastructure of national interest, capable of connecting public and private organizations through a common cyber emergency communication mechanism.

## Business Model

The initiative can explore different monetization models, considering that building the network depends on the participation of multiple organizations and the development of trust among participants.

* **Institutional Subscription:** charging participating organizations a recurring subscription fee based on size, criticality and the volume of relationships.
* **Corporate Plans:** differentiated plans based on the number of connections, business units, integrations and communication resources used.
* **Integration Services:** charging for the implementation and integration of the emergency mechanism with each organization's systems and processes.
* **Governance and Compliance Services:** offering services related to network membership, institutional validation, participation policies and compliance with governance requirements.
* **Aggregated Intelligence and Metrics:** providing reports and statistical indicators on incidents, occurrence patterns and network efficiency, while preserving information confidentiality.
* **Response and Coordination Services:** offering complementary services to support organizations in structuring their emergency response processes.
* **Institutional Licensing:** ability to license the initiative to industry associations, business consortia or entities seeking to establish their own emergency networks.
* **Institutional Partnerships:** opportunities for funding, sponsorship or cooperation with industry entities and organizations interested in strengthening the cyber resilience of a particular sector.

The business model can generate value even for organizations that use the solution exclusively internally. In this case, the subscription provides a standardized emergency activation mechanism and integration with internal response processes.

Participation in the collaborative network adds the ability to communicate and coordinate with other organizations, expanding the benefits as the number of relevant participants grows.

An important aspect of the business model is network neutrality. Since competing organizations will need to share sensitive information during emergency situations, the initiative's credibility and independence will be fundamental factors for adoption.

One possibility is to separate institutional governance from commercial operations: an independent entity could establish the network's rules and principles, while a specialized company would be responsible for providing and operating the required services.

## Proposal Differentiators

The main differentiator is the transformation of emergency activation and threat information sharing into a communication and response mechanism between organizations.

While traditional cybersecurity initiatives focus on sharing indicators, reports and threat intelligence, this proposal aims to enable a participant to report an emergency and trigger, within seconds, a response from its own systems and, when necessary, those of other relevant participants.

The main differentiators are:

* **Independent Use of the Network:** each organization can use the Panic Button to activate its internal procedures, even without sharing the incident with third parties.
* **Response Speed:** reduction of the time between incident identification and communication to potentially affected organizations.
* **Coordination Between Organizations:** structured communication between companies with business, financial, operational or technological relationships.
* **Automated Response:** ability for each participant to implement its own protective measures based on received alerts.
* **Institutional Autonomy:** each organization retains control over its systems, data, policies and response decisions.
* **Cross-Sector Coverage:** ability to connect financial institutions, private companies, technology vendors and public authorities.
* **Network Effect:** the greater the participation of relevant organizations, the greater the ability to disseminate alerts and provide collective protection.
* **Focus on Containment:** priority on reducing attackers' window of opportunity and limiting the extent of losses, rather than merely reporting that an incident has occurred.

The proposal does not eliminate the possibility of a successful attack or guarantee that all financial losses will be avoided. Its purpose is to reduce individual and collective response times, limiting an attacker's ability to exploit the incident within the affected organization or across other companies.

* **FS-ISAC:** international information-sharing and collaboration organization focused on cybersecurity in the financial services sector.
* **Central Bank of Brazil:** institutional and regulatory reference for sharing information about relevant incidents and fraud within the financial system.
* **MISP:** threat intelligence and indicators of compromise sharing platform used by security communities.
* **FIRST:** international organization bringing together security incident response teams and promoting collaboration among them.

These initiatives serve as references and potential complements to the concept. The network's specific value proposition lies in combining internal emergency activation, communication driven by relationships between organizations and the ability to trigger coordinated local responses.
