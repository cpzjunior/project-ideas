# Continuous Strategic Intelligence and Investigative Monitoring Platform

## Description

An AI-powered business intelligence platform designed to investigate strategic issues, identify opportunities, anticipate risks, and continuously monitor developments that may influence business decisions.

The proposal is to transform the way organizations collect, analyze, and use external information, replacing traditional research and monitoring processes with a model of continuous, contextualized investigation driven by business questions.

The platform operates as an investigative intelligence hub, where users can register strategic questions they want to investigate, such as:

- Is there any significant risk involving a particular supplier?
- Is a competitor preparing to launch a new product?
- Is there any regulatory change that could impact our operations?
- Is a company showing signs of financial or operational deterioration?
- Is a business opportunity emerging in a particular industry?
- Could an emerging technology represent a threat or opportunity for our organization?
- Are there any corporate, commercial, or strategic developments involving a particular company?

Based on these questions, the platform conducts investigations using multiple information sources, including news, public databases, documents, corporate records, specialized publications, social media, regulatory data, and other available sources.

Unlike traditional monitoring tools, which identify occurrences and generate alerts, the solution seeks to understand the context of events, correlate seemingly disconnected information, formulate hypotheses, and identify what new evidence needs to be collected.

A central aspect of the proposal is that each investigation has a persistent existence. It maintains its history, hypotheses, evidence, consulted sources, conclusions, uncertainties, and next steps.

Thus, whenever new relevant information emerges, the platform automatically resumes the investigation from its previous state, without having to restart the entire process.

The objective is to transform scattered information into actionable intelligence, enabling organizations to anticipate developments, understand complex scenarios, and make better-informed decisions.

## Operation

When accessing the platform, users create an investigation by providing the question they want to answer, its organizational context, the intended objectives, and the relevance of that issue to the business.

For example, a financial institution could create the following investigation:

Are there any public indicators of financial, operational, regulatory, or reputational deterioration involving a strategic supplier that could compromise the continuity of the services provided?

Based on this question, the platform structures an investigation, identifying related entities, relevant sources, initial hypotheses, and information-gathering strategies.

The investigation can be divided into different stages:

- Investigation Formulation: interpretation of the initial question, identification of the problem, definition of objectives, scope delimitation, and development of the hypotheses to be investigated.
- Intelligent Information Collection: research across multiple sources, including news, documents, public records, corporate information, regulatory data, specialized publications, and other available sources.
- Entity and Relationship Identification: recognition of companies, individuals, economic groups, investors, controlling shareholders, suppliers, competitors, and other entities related to the investigation.
- Event Correlation: identification of relationships between events, information, and evidence that, when analyzed individually, might not appear relevant.
- Hypothesis Investigation: further examination of identified possibilities, seeking evidence that confirms, weakens, or rules out existing hypotheses.
- Evidence Assessment: analysis of the reliability, timeliness, independence, and relevance of sources, distinguishing discovered facts from AI-generated inferences.
- Conclusion Generation: consolidation of findings, presentation of evidence, identification of uncertainties, and development of recommendations.
- Continuous Monitoring: ongoing tracking of investigations after their initial conclusion, identifying new developments that may change previous findings.
- Automatic Reassessment: resumption of investigations when new information emerges, allowing hypotheses, conclusions, and recommendations to be updated.

The platform may offer features such as:

- Investigation Hub: an environment for creating, organizing, and monitoring different investigations, allowing users to view their status, priority, relevance, uncertainty level, and progress.
- AI-Powered Investigative Assistant: a conversational interface for asking questions, deepening analyses, exploring hypotheses, and interacting with existing investigations.
- Autonomous Investigations: AI agents responsible for conducting research, identifying new investigative leads, gathering evidence, and proposing next steps.
- Intelligent Scheduler: a mechanism responsible for managing computational capacity across investigations, prioritizing those with greater urgency, potential impact, or expected value from new information.
- Investigative Budget: control of the resources allocated to each investigation, considering processing costs, queries, APIs, paid sources, and potential value generation.
- Knowledge Graph: visual representation of entities, relationships, events, hypotheses, and evidence identified during investigations.
- Evidence Management: a structured repository of collected information, including source identification, dates, references, reliability, and relationships with the hypotheses under investigation.
- Investigative Memory: preservation of context, history, hypotheses, previous conclusions, outstanding questions, and decisions made, allowing investigations to resume without losing continuity.
- Organizational Context: registration of company-specific information, such as strategic suppliers, competitors, internal policies, risk criteria, business definitions, and preferred sources.
- Human Feedback: ability for users to correct information, challenge inferences, validate relationships, and evaluate conclusions, incorporating these corrections into organizational knowledge.
- Intelligent Alerts: notifications about developments that could actually change the outcome of an investigation, avoiding excessive irrelevant alerts.
- Audit Trail: recording of questions, consulted sources, discovered evidence, considered hypotheses, intermediate decisions, and justifications for recommendations.
- Executive Reports: generation of structured analyses for managers, including conclusions, identified risks, opportunities, evidence, and recommendations.
- Autonomy Management: configuration of permitted autonomy levels, ranging from research and alerts to recommendations requiring human approval.

An important differentiator would be the platform's ability to decide not only what to investigate, but also when to investigate, how much effort to allocate to each issue, and when to suspend or resume an investigation.

For example, an investigation into a supplier may identify a news article involving a related company. Instead of simply generating an alert, the platform could investigate the corporate relationship, search for additional news, consult public records, identify potential legal proceedings or regulatory incidents, and assess whether these developments represent an actual risk to the supplier.

If the evidence is insufficient, the investigation remains open or enters monitoring mode. When new relevant information emerges, it resumes based on all previously accumulated knowledge.

In this way, the platform does not function merely as a search engine or a collection of independent agents. It operates as a system of persistent, coordinated investigations capable of evolving over time.

## Applications

The solution can serve organizations that rely on external information to make strategic decisions, assess risks, identify opportunities, or understand complex scenarios.

Its application potential is particularly relevant in industries where scattered information, frequent changes, and relationships between different entities can produce financial, operational, regulatory, or reputational impacts.

The main target audiences and use cases include:

- Financial Institutions: investigation of companies, economic groups, counterparties, suppliers, and customers; monitoring of reputational, financial, regulatory, and corporate risks; identification of events that may affect exposures and business relationships.
- Compliance and Anti-Money Laundering Departments: in-depth analysis of legal entities, corporate structures, ultimate beneficial owners, business relationships, sanctions, and adverse media, supporting due diligence and continuous monitoring processes.
- Third-Party and Risk Management: monitoring of suppliers, strategic partners, and service providers, identifying signs of financial deterioration, operational problems, critical dependencies, and developments that could compromise business continuity.
- Competitive Intelligence: investigation of competitors, market developments, product launches, acquisitions, strategic changes, significant hiring activity, and trends in specific industries.
- Investment Funds and Asset Managers: monitoring of portfolio companies, potential investments, economic sectors, corporate developments, and events that could change investment theses.
- Strategic Consulting Firms: execution of in-depth research on markets, companies, industries, business opportunities, and competitive developments.
- Insurance Companies: investigation of companies, industries, events, and external factors that could change exposures, claims ratios, and risk assessments.
- Technology and Information Security Companies: investigation of threats, vulnerabilities, attack groups, incidents, technology suppliers, and supply chain-related risks.
- Governments and Public Sector Agencies: monitoring of contracted companies, public tenders, government contracts, economic developments, fraud risks, and relationships between organizations.
- Law Firms and Legal Departments: support for corporate investigations, corporate background research, analysis of ownership relationships, litigation monitoring, and identification of developments relevant to disputes and negotiations.

### Monetization Models

The platform can explore different business models:

- B2B SaaS: monthly or annual subscriptions, with pricing based on the number of users, active investigations, query volume, and processing capacity.
- Enterprise: corporate licensing with dedicated infrastructure, integrations, governance policies, advanced controls, and customizations.
- API as a Service: provision of investigative capabilities through APIs, allowing other platforms to request investigations, retrieve evidence, and receive alerts.
- Pay per Investigation: charges for each investigation conducted, based on its complexity, depth, and resource consumption.
- Data Intelligence: commercialization of structured analyses, indicators, and intelligence generated from public and licensed sources, respecting applicable usage rights.
- White Label: provision of the technology to consulting firms, intelligence companies, financial institutions, and organizations that wish to offer the service under their own brand.
- B2B2C: integration with third-party platforms that would use the investigative capabilities as part of their own products.

An interesting model would be to combine a basic subscription, offering a predefined number of investigations and monitoring activities, with additional charges for more complex investigations or the use of premium sources.

- AlphaSense: a market intelligence platform that uses AI to research, analyze documents, monitor companies, and identify relevant information for strategic decision-making;
- Portend AI: a reference in continuous risk intelligence, connecting public, regulatory, commercial, and corporate information to identify emerging signals and monitor risks associated with organizations;
- Penlink: provides OSINT investigation and investigative intelligence capabilities. Its CoAnalyst360 offers concepts particularly aligned with this proposal, such as persistent context, multi-agent orchestration, and investigation monitoring;
- Celsus Intelligence: a reference in organizing the investigative lifecycle, connecting research, evidence, relationships, assessments, monitoring, and reporting within a governed environment;
- Sentinela: a Brazilian solution focused on due diligence and risk intelligence, using public data, corporate structures, court proceedings, sanctions, and adverse media;
- Intrace: combines OSINT investigations, risk monitoring, relationship analysis, and pattern identification across open sources;
- Gartner: a reference for understanding the Competitive and Market Intelligence Platforms category, which encompasses competitive and market intelligence solutions.
