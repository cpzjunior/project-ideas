# Citizen Newsletter

## Description

Digital platform for personalized monitoring of public information, aimed at citizens who wish to monitor decisions, expenditures, projects, indicators, and activities of federal, state, and municipal governments.

The solution works as a personalized newsletter, in which each citizen selects the topics, public agencies, regions, and indicators they wish to monitor. Based on these preferences, the platform consolidates information from transparency portals, government databases, official publications, and other public sources, sending periodic updates and alerts about relevant events.

The proposal aims to facilitate access to public information, eliminating the need for citizens to manually consult multiple government portals and systems to follow topics of interest.

The objective is to transform passive public transparency, in which citizens must search for information, into a model of active and personalized transparency, in which relevant information is delivered directly to those interested.

The platform must be independent of governments, political parties, and public officials, maintaining transparent criteria for selecting, organizing, and presenting information.

## Operation

- Citizen Registration and Preferences: creation of a personal profile in which users select the topics, agencies, entities, regions, and indicators they wish to monitor. Allows users to configure preferences for frequency, format, and channels for receiving information.
- Public Spending Monitoring: monitoring of government expenditures, budget execution, contracts, payments, personnel expenses, per diems, travel, and other public expenditures. Allows users to monitor specific agencies and entities, identifying relevant changes in expenditure amounts and patterns.
- Procurement and Contract Monitoring: monitoring of bidding processes, public procurement, contract amendments, contracted amounts, suppliers, and contract terminations. Allows users to monitor supplier companies and contracts from selected agencies.
- Public Officials Monitoring: monitoring of public information related to authorities, elected representatives, managers, and public officeholders, including appointments, dismissals, changes in compensation, and institutional activities available from official sources.
- Legislative Monitoring: monitoring of bills, proposed constitutional amendments, votes, legislative changes, and the progress of legislative matters in municipal councils, state legislative assemblies, the Chamber of Deputies, and the Federal Senate. Allows users to monitor specific topics and receive updates on selected proposals.
- Public Policy Monitoring: monitoring of government programs, projects, public works, targets, schedules, investments, and performance indicators. Allows users to monitor specific initiatives and track their progress over time.
- Parliamentary Amendments Monitoring: monitoring of amendments submitted by legislators, allocated amounts, beneficiary agencies, covered locations, and resource execution. Allows users to monitor the allocation and use of public funds.
- Economic and Fiscal Indicators: monitoring of indicators such as revenue collection, public debt, inflation, employment, government expenditures, fiscal results, and budget execution. Allows users to select indicators and track their historical trends.
- Oversight Bodies Monitoring: monitoring of audits, reports, recommendations, decisions, and findings published by institutions such as Courts of Accounts, the Office of the Comptroller General, Public Prosecutors' Offices, and other oversight bodies, respecting legal limits on public disclosure and confidentiality.
- Personalized Newsletter: consolidation of selected information into periodic communications, organized by topic, relevance, agency, and period. Each edition presents objective summaries, identified changes, and links to consult official sources.
- Alerts and Notifications: delivery of notifications about relevant events, such as new contracts, contract amendments, approval of legislative proposals, changes in indicators, publication of audits, and developments in topics monitored by citizens.
- Information History and Comparison: storage of previously published information, allowing users to compare periods, identify changes, and track the evolution of expenditures, contracts, indicators, and public policies.
- Information Search and Exploration: search mechanism that allows users to locate public information by topic, agency, entity, public official, company, region, or period, even when data is distributed across different government portals.
- Data Intelligence and Analysis: use of artificial intelligence to consolidate information, summarize documents, explain technical terms, identify relevant changes, and correlate information from different sources. Results must include references to original sources, allowing citizens to verify the information.
- Integration with Public Sources: automated collection of information made available through transparency portals, government APIs, official gazettes, legislative systems, and public databases. Whenever possible, official integrations must be used, preserving data origin, update dates, and traceability.
- Communication Personalization: ability to receive information by email, application, push notifications, or other available channels. Citizens can define the delivery frequency and the amount of information received, avoiding excessive notifications.
- Information Sharing: ability to share editions, news, indicators, and monitoring results with others, helping expand access to information of public interest.

## Applications

- General Public: individuals who wish to monitor government spending, decisions, projects, and activities without having to manually consult different public portals.
- Taxpayers: citizens interested in monitoring the collection and allocation of resources derived from taxes and other public revenues.
- Voters: individuals who wish to monitor the performance of elected representatives, including proposals, votes, expenditures, commitments, and parliamentary activities.
- Journalists and Communicators: professionals who need to monitor government information, identify relevant events, and track developments in matters of public interest.
- Researchers and Academics: individuals who use public data for studies on public administration, economics, public policies, and government management.
- Civil Society Organizations: entities that monitor public policies, government spending, contracts, and the activities of public agencies.
- Companies and Investors: organizations interested in monitoring regulatory changes, procurement processes, public investments, economic indicators, and government decisions that may affect their activities.
