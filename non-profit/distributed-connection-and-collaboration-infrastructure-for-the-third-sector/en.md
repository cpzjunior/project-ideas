# Distributed Connection and Collaboration Infrastructure for the Third Sector

## Description

A digital platform based on a distributed and interoperable infrastructure, designed to connect third-sector organizations, social initiatives, businesses, governments, volunteers, and citizens through the standardized sharing of information, needs, opportunities, and resources.

The proposal is to create a common infrastructure that enables different organizations to share information and services without having to abandon their existing systems or rely on a centralized platform.

Currently, many social organizations face difficulties in promoting their initiatives, finding people willing to help, identifying available resources, and establishing connections with other institutions.

For example, an NGO that needs to collect food donations may publicize its needs on its own website, social media, and messaging groups. However, another organization with food available may simply never discover that demand.

Similarly, people interested in volunteering need to search across different organizations and platforms to find opportunities that match their location, availability, and interests.

The proposal is to solve this problem through a shared infrastructure in which each organization remains responsible for producing and managing its own information, while being able to make it available to different applications and network participants.

For example, a fundraising campaign published by an organization could be automatically discovered and displayed across different applications, such as volunteering platforms, social campaign portals, community initiative maps, and corporate social responsibility systems.

The organization responsible for the campaign would not need to register independently on each of these applications.

A fundamental aspect is that the proposal does not aim to build just another portal to consolidate social organizations. The goal is to create an infrastructure of protocols, services, and interoperability mechanisms that allows different platforms to continue operating independently while being able to share information and collaborate.

The objective is to expand the third sector's capacity for connection and collaboration, reducing information fragmentation and making it easier to direct resources toward initiatives that genuinely need support.

## How It Works

When joining the infrastructure, an organization registers and configures how it wants to make its information available, using its own interfaces, APIs, or simplified publishing tools.

An institution with its own systems could make information available through APIs. A small NGO could use a form or publishing tool provided by the infrastructure.

Once published, the information could be discovered and consumed by different participants and applications according to their interests and permissions.

The infrastructure could encompass several dimensions:

- Social information publishing: standardized availability of campaigns, needs, volunteering opportunities, projects, events, available resources, and community initiatives.
- Information discovery: mechanisms for locating information by geographic location, category, organization, cause, availability, and other criteria.
- Federated distribution: the ability to share information across different applications and organizations without requiring all participants to use a centralized system.
- System interoperability: provision of APIs, protocols, and data models that enable communication between independent platforms.
- Information synchronization: propagation of updates, status changes, campaign closures, filled positions, and corrections to published information.
- Organizational identity management: identification and validation of participating organizations, making it possible to recognize the origin and accountability for shared information.
- Governance and access control: definition of which information can be public, restricted, or shared only with authorized participants.
- Matching needs with resources: identification of collaboration opportunities between organizations with specific needs and others that have resources to meet them.
- Integration with artificial intelligence agents: provision of structured information for intelligent agents that can discover needs, identify opportunities, and recommend connections.
- Observability and traceability: monitoring of information availability, updates, participants, and connections established through the infrastructure.

The platform could offer features such as:

- Organization directory: a catalog of participating organizations, allowing users to browse their areas of activity, locations, projects, and public information.
- Initiative publisher: a simplified interface for publishing campaigns, projects, needs, opportunities, and available resources.
- Social API Hub: a set of standardized APIs for publishing, querying, updating, and discovering information.
- Social information catalog: an index that allows users to locate information published by different participants without necessarily centralizing the original data.
- Needs and opportunities map: geographic visualization of social demands, campaigns, available resources, and community initiatives.
- Social matching engine: a mechanism for matching needs with resources, connecting organizations that need help with participants who can provide it.
- Distribution hub: a service responsible for making information available to applications, portals, and communities interested in consuming it.
- Event and notification management: propagation of changes and new records through event-driven mechanisms, allowing applications to track changes in near real time.
- Trust management: mechanisms for verifying organizational identities, assessing information provenance, and flagging outdated or inconsistent content.
- Impact dashboard: aggregated metrics on published information, participating organizations, needs addressed, opportunities filled, and connections established.
- Developer SDK: libraries and tools that facilitate the development of applications integrated with the infrastructure.
- AI agent gateway: interfaces that allow intelligent agents to query information, discover organizations, and identify collaboration opportunities.

An important differentiator would be allowing organizations to continue using their own systems without having to migrate their data or replace their existing applications.

The infrastructure would function as an intermediate interoperability layer, connecting different participants and allowing information published in one environment to be discovered and used in others.

For example, during an emergency caused by flooding, a community organization could register a need for drinking water, another institution could make food available, a company could offer transportation, and volunteers could identify opportunities to get involved.

The infrastructure would make it possible to correlate this information, connect participants, and monitor the progress of the response.

If a need were met, the update could be propagated to interested applications, preventing unnecessary mobilization efforts.

## Applications

The solution could serve different segments of the third sector, as well as public and private organizations interested in developing social impact initiatives.

The main audiences and applications include:

- NGOs and social organizations: to promote campaigns, needs, projects, volunteering opportunities, and available resources, expanding their reach without relying exclusively on their own channels.
- Humanitarian organizations: to share information about the needs of vulnerable communities, coordinate resources, and connect organizations during emergency situations.
- Governments and municipalities: to make information about social programs, public initiatives, community services, and needs identified in different regions available.
- Companies: to make resources, services, equipment, volunteers, and corporate social responsibility initiatives available, connecting their actions to existing needs.
- Financial institutions: to connect social investment programs, community development initiatives, beneficiary organizations, and impact projects.
- Universities: to connect extension projects, researchers, student volunteers, social organizations, and communities.
- Corporate foundations and institutes: to identify social initiatives, publicize funding opportunities, monitor projects, and find organizations addressing specific causes.
- Volunteering platforms: to discover opportunities made available by different organizations without relying on individual registrations on each platform.
- Technology companies: to build applications, search engines, maps, matching systems, and intelligent agents using the shared infrastructure.

The infrastructure could also enable different products and applications:

- Social search engine: discovery of campaigns, projects, and initiatives by cause or region.
- Needs map: geographic identification of social demands and available resources.
- Volunteering marketplace: connection of volunteers with opportunities from different organizations.
- Donation platform: identification of campaigns and needs that can receive contributions.
- Emergency hub: information sharing and resource coordination during crises.
- Social impact portal: consolidation of initiatives and projects from different institutions.
- AI-powered social assistant: recommendations on ways to help based on the user's profile and location.
- Social responsibility platform: connection of companies with contribution opportunities and social projects.

From a business perspective, the platform could explore different monetization models:

- Open Core: open availability of protocols, specifications, and essential components, with the commercialization of advanced management, governance, and administration tools.
- B2B: licensing of additional services to companies, foundations, governments, and larger organizations.
- Enterprise: provision of private instances, connectors, specialized support, security controls, and enterprise integrations.
- API as a Service: charging for the consumption of additional services, processing, information enrichment, and integration capacity.
- White Label: provision of the technology to companies, governments, and organizations that wish to offer their own portals and applications.
- Institutional sponsorship: funding of the infrastructure by companies, foundations, and institutions interested in expanding social impact.
- Grants and impact funding: securing resources from national and international organizations that support social innovation initiatives and open digital infrastructure.

## References

- Open Referral: an international initiative that develops open standards and tools to enable organizations to share information about social services, institutions, locations, and available resources. Its HSDS (Human Services Data Specification) standard is an important reference for the infrastructure's data modeling.
- ActivityPub: a protocol standardized by the W3C that enables communication between independent servers, serving as an architectural reference for the federated distribution of information without dependence on a centralized platform.
- RSS: an open content distribution format that allows different applications to discover and consume information published by independent sources.
- Open Finance: an interoperability model between institutions that uses common standards for communication, security, and data sharing, serving as a reference for integration between independent organizations.
- Doare: a Brazilian fundraising platform for social organizations, offering payment, campaign, donor management, and fundraising solutions.
- Open Eligibility: an open standard for classifying social services, relevant to facilitating the discovery and understanding of available opportunities and services.
- Model Context Protocol (MCP): an integration protocol between artificial intelligence models and external tools, enabling the infrastructure's information and services to be made available to intelligent agents.
- Agent2Agent (A2A): a communication protocol between artificial intelligence agents, enabling agents developed by different organizations to discover capabilities and collaborate.
