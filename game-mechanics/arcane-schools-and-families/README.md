# Arcane Schools and Families

## Description

The Arcane Schools and Families system establishes a structure for organizing magical knowledge based on lineages, traditions, and their own philosophies. Unlike conventional universities or academic institutions, schools function like traditional martial arts families, responsible for preserving, developing, and transmitting their methods of manipulating magic across generations.

Each school represents a particular magical identity, with its own techniques, principles, training methods, and interpretations of the nature of magic. Mages establish an official affiliation with only one school, which comes to represent their primary training and identity within the game's universe.

The structure also allows traditions to evolve through exchanges between schools. Experienced mages may temporarily study with other families, acquire complementary knowledge, and establish relationships between different lineages without necessarily severing their original affiliation or gaining unrestricted access to the deepest knowledge of other traditions.

The goal is to create a magical progression structure based not only on the individual acquisition of skills, but also on belonging, reputation, cultural heritage, and the continuity of traditions.

## Functioning

During character creation or initial development, the player chooses an arcane school with which they will be officially affiliated. This choice determines their primary path of magical development, the available training methods, and the institutional relationships they may establish throughout their journey.

Each school is managed by a family or lineage responsible for preserving its knowledge and passing down its techniques. Leadership may be exercised by a master, who represents the highest authority within that tradition and is responsible for guiding its members, defining its training methods, and protecting its secrets.

Schools may have distinct characteristics:

- Traditional schools: value discipline, hierarchy, obedience, and the rigorous preservation of ancestral teachings. Access to the most advanced techniques depends on long periods of training and recognition by the masters.
- Experimental schools: encourage creativity, investigation, and the development of new magical applications. Their members have greater freedom to reinterpret existing techniques and propose new approaches.
- Communal schools: prioritize collective learning, collaboration among members, and the horizontal transmission of knowledge. Individual development is associated with contributing to the strengthening of the family.
- Isolationist schools: restrict contact with other traditions, preserving their knowledge and techniques through highly controlled internal relationships. The admission of new members may involve rigorous selection processes.

Progression within a school occurs through mastery of its techniques, participation in training sessions, completion of challenges, and recognition by the masters. The player can develop their internal reputation and earn positions of greater responsibility, eventually becoming a master or assuming leadership of the lineage, provided the necessary conditions are met.

The system also allows exchanges between schools. After reaching a certain level of development, a mage may request authorization to study temporarily with another family. During this period, they may learn complementary techniques, understand different magical philosophies, and establish relationships with members of other traditions.

However, exchanges have limitations. Visitors do not automatically gain access to the host school's secret knowledge, cannot assume its leadership, and do not replace their original institutional affiliation. In this way, each character maintains a primary magical identity, even when developing knowledge derived from other traditions.

The system may also encompass historical rivalries, alliances, disputes between successors, marriages between lineages, and conflicts related to the interpretation or preservation of ancestral knowledge. These relationships may influence access to training, the availability of masters, and the characters' progression opportunities.

## Applications

Arcane Schools and Families are particularly useful in RPGs and fantasy games that aim to establish a deeper social and cultural structure for character development.

The mechanic allows conventional magical class systems to be replaced with organizations that have their own identities, in which progression is associated with belonging and the transmission of knowledge. Instead of simply choosing a specialization and unlocking abilities, the player becomes part of a tradition with its own history, hierarchy, values, and interests.

The existence of different schools also encourages diversity in playstyles. Two schools may master the same category of magic while having completely different techniques and philosophies. A family specializing in fire magic, for example, may value direct destruction and offensive power, while another interprets fire as an instrument of transformation, purification, and control.

Exchanges between families allow for more complex progression without eliminating the identity of the schools. Players can develop combinations of knowledge, establish diplomatic relationships, and participate in activities that extend beyond the boundaries of their original tradition.

The mechanic also offers opportunities for narratives involving leadership succession, family disputes, ancestral secrets, historical rivalries, and philosophical changes within organizations. In this way, schools cease to function merely as mechanisms for distributing abilities and begin to serve as relevant institutions in worldbuilding.
