# Recurring Seasonal Events

## Description

A system of recurring social events that uses the game's internal calendar to simulate a society with traditions, customs, and collective activities. Weddings, harvest festivals, trade fairs, religious ceremonies, tournaments, funerals, and celebrations take place on specific dates, regardless of the player's presence or participation.

These events are part of the population's culture and daily life, creating occasions when inhabitants gather, establishments change their operating schedules, and different regions take on distinctive characteristics. Events may be associated with seasons, religious traditions, historical events, or local customs.

The player can participate in celebrations, ignore them, or influence certain events. Their presence is not required for the world to keep functioning: events take place even when they are busy with other activities or exploring distant regions.

In addition to temporarily changing NPC routines, events can generate collective memories and lasting social consequences. A wedding may change relationships between families, a tournament may reveal new champions, and a funeral may represent a significant change within the community.

The goal is to make the world feel inhabited by a real community, with a social life that exists independently of the protagonist's actions.

## Functioning

- Internal calendar with dates, seasons, and festive periods.
- Recurring events scheduled for specific dates.
- Variety of social events, such as weddings, festivals, fairs, religious ceremonies, tournaments, funerals, and celebrations.
- Autonomous NPC participation based on their relationships, occupations, and interests.
- Temporary changes to routines, commercial activities, and population movement.
- Environmental decorations and visual changes associated with celebrations.
- Opportunities for the player to participate in activities and festivities.
- Events that take place regardless of the protagonist's presence or participation.
- Recording of significant events in the community's collective memory.
- Potential social and narrative consequences that persist after events have ended.
- Traditions and celebrations specific to different regions and cultures.

## Applications

- Open-world RPGs.
- Life and community simulation games.
- City and settlement management games.
- Historical and medieval simulators.
- Survival games with persistent communities.
- Virtual worlds with time cycles and NPC routines.
