# Mana Compatibility

## Description

A magical system based on the existence of different types of mana, each with its own properties and characteristics, similar to blood types. Every individual is born with a specific compatibility that determines which types of mana their body can efficiently absorb, store, and use.

The use of compatible mana provides greater magical efficiency, while the use of incompatible mana can reduce performance, cause magical rejection, inflict physical damage, or even lead to corruption within the user's body.

This characteristic makes compatibility an important factor in the training and development of mages, influencing their abilities, limitations, and dependence on external sources of mana.

## Functioning

Each character has a type of magical compatibility that determines their affinity with different sources of mana. This compatibility can be identified through examinations, rituals, or magical experiments.

Compatible mana can be absorbed and used normally, providing greater efficiency when casting spells, lower energy consumption, and better utilization of available reserves.

Incompatible mana, on the other hand, produces different levels of rejection depending on the disparity between the types involved. Its effects may include energy waste, spell instability, damage to the body, and progressive magical corruption.

This mechanic also introduces the need to manage mana sources and reserves. Mages may need to seek suppliers, donors, or artifacts containing mana compatible with their nature, creating dependencies and opportunities for negotiation, cooperation, and conflict.

An extremely rare condition is universal compatibility, found in individuals capable of absorbing and using any type of mana without suffering rejection. This characteristic allows greater flexibility in the use of magical sources and significantly increases the ability to sustain prolonged magical activities.

## Applications

Can be used in fantasy RPGs, strategy games, MMORPGs, magic simulators, and experiences featuring complex progression and resource management systems.

It is particularly suitable for games in which magic has well-defined biological or natural rules, allowing the exploration of individual limitations, specializations, dependence on external resources, and differences between characters.

It can also serve as a worldbuilding element, creating mana markets, donor networks, disputes over specific sources, and rare characters with exceptional magical properties.
