# Multiple Coinage Systems

## Description

An economic system based on the coexistence of different currencies and monetary standards, in which each kingdom, civilization, or race has its own means of payment, using metals, precious stones, or other materials of recognized value.

Unlike economies with a universal currency and fixed conversion rates, each monetary system has its own value, influenced by factors such as supply, demand, availability of raw materials, reserves, political stability, commercial activity, and confidence in the authority responsible for minting.

The system allows the player to accumulate, transport, exchange, and produce different currencies, including fractional units of extremely valuable materials, such as small quantities of gold, silver, or ruby.

In this way, the economy becomes a dynamic system in which differences in value between regions create opportunities for trade, speculation, and arbitrage.

## Functioning

Each kingdom or civilization has its own monetary structure, with currencies made from different materials, denominations, and values. These currencies may have variable exchange rates determined by the economic and political conditions of each territory.

The player can acquire currencies through trade, rewards, looting, mining, crafting, or financial services. They can also perform currency exchange transactions at specialized institutions, merchants, exchange houses, or informal markets.

Exchange rates fluctuate according to the economic conditions of the world. A region with a large supply of silver may have a lower valuation of that metal, while a civilization facing a gold shortage may offer more favorable exchange rates to those who possess this resource.

The availability of raw materials also directly influences minting. Wars, trade blockades, mine discoveries, depletion of reserves, and political changes can alter the quantity of currency in circulation and, consequently, its purchasing power.

The system also allows valuable resources to be used in fractional quantities. Instead of relying exclusively on coins with fixed denominations, the player can trade small portions of metals and precious stones, enabling transactions of different magnitudes and facilitating trade between cultures with incompatible monetary standards.

These characteristics create opportunities for more sophisticated economic strategies. The player can buy a particular currency in a region where it has a lower value and transport it to another where its exchange rate is higher, making a profit from the conversion difference. They can also speculate on the appreciation of certain materials, accumulate reserves, or anticipate economic changes caused by world events.

However, these operations involve risks. Political changes, currency devaluation, counterfeiting, trade restrictions, transportation costs, and economic instability can undermine investments and generate losses.

Thus, the choice of which currencies to transport, accumulate, or use constantly depends on the player's location and the economic conditions of each region.

## Applications

Can be used in open-world RPGs, strategy games, economic simulators, MMORPGs, and experiences featuring complex trading and resource management systems.

It is particularly suitable for games with multiple civilizations, economically independent territories, and relevant trade routes, in which movement between regions offers opportunities for negotiation and exploitation of market differences.

It can also be used as a worldbuilding element, establishing distinct economic identities for different cultures, international trade relationships, disputes over natural resources, monetary crises, and the political influence of major financial institutions.
