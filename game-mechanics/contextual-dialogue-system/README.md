# Contextual Dialogue System

## Description

A dynamic dialogue system that allows players to interact with NPCs through intentions, tones, and short personalized phrases, rather than selecting entirely predefined responses.

During a conversation, the game presents a mini-system in which the player defines the intention behind the interaction, chooses the desired tone, and writes a short phrase. The combination of this information is interpreted by the system to generate a response consistent with the NPC's personality, emotional state, and context.

The impact of the interaction is calculated internally, taking into account factors such as player abilities, prior relationship, conversation context, risk level, and the interlocutor's characteristics. Thus, the same phrase can produce different results depending on who receives it and the circumstances.

An attempt at intimidation, for example, may cause an NPC to back down or call the guards, depending on their personality and the player's intimidation ability. Flirting may be reciprocated, ignored, or rejected depending on the level of intimacy and the interlocutor's disposition.

The system combines freedom of expression with narrative and mechanical consequences, allowing for more natural interactions without relying exclusively on traditional dialogue trees.

## Functioning

- Selection of the interaction intention, such as persuading, intimidating, negotiating, provoking, flirting, or asking for help.
- Selection of the message tone, such as friendly, formal, aggressive, sarcastic, or seductive.
- Text field for entering a short, contextualized phrase.
- Message interpretation based on intention, tone, and written content.
- Generation of responses consistent with the NPC's personality, history, and emotional state.
- Internal calculation of outcomes based on player abilities, relationship, context, and risk level.
- Recording of feedback and interaction consequences.
- Dynamic changes in NPC reputation, trust, intimacy, hostility, and disposition.
- Possibility of unexpected reactions, including refusal, confrontation, escape, or intervention by third parties.

## Applications

- RPGs featuring non-player characters and dynamic relationships.
- Social simulation and everyday life games.
- Narrative games with emergent dialogue.
- Adventure games involving negotiation, persuasion, and intimidation.
- RPGs featuring reputation, diplomacy, and social consequence systems.
