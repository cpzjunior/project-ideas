# Natural Disasters

## Description

A system of natural events that uses catastrophes as permanent elements of worldbuilding and narrative, causing phenomena such as earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, floods, droughts, tsunamis, and landslides to produce lasting environmental changes.

Unlike weather events or temporary threats, natural disasters leave geographical, environmental, economic, and social consequences that remain embedded in the game world.

Each catastrophe can reshape the landscape, reveal or conceal regions, alter ecosystems, destroy settlements, and transform the distribution of natural resources. These changes also influence the history and organization of civilizations, making the setting itself function as a record of the events that shaped its existence.

## Functioning

Natural disasters can occur at different periods in the world's history, both before and during the player's journey. Each event has its own characteristics and consequences, determined by its intensity, location, and geographical conditions.

Earthquakes can cause collapses, open fissures, destroy buildings, and reveal previously inaccessible caves or underground structures. Volcanic eruptions can create new geological formations, bury regions, and render certain territories uninhabitable. Floods and tsunamis can alter river courses, destroy coastal cities, and change shipping routes, while prolonged periods of drought can transform fertile areas into desert regions.

These transformations can permanently modify the map, creating new exploration routes, blocking familiar paths, and changing the location of natural resources. Previously inaccessible regions may become explorable, while known areas may disappear or require new means of travel.

The impacts also extend to societies. Civilizations may migrate, abandon cities, rebuild settlements, or develop new cultural practices in response to catastrophes. Certain communities may preserve traditions associated with major disasters, while others may develop social and religious structures shaped by experiences of survival and reconstruction.

The player can encounter traces of these events through ruins, geological formations, historical records, abandoned structures, and accounts from inhabitants. In this way, exploration allows players to reconstruct the environmental and social history of each region without relying exclusively on direct narrative exposition.

When disasters occur during the adventure, their consequences can dynamically transform the world, affecting economic activities, resource availability, route safety, and survival conditions.

Thus, nature acts as a permanent force of transformation rather than merely a temporary threat to the player.

## Applications

Can be used in open-world RPGs, survival games, exploration adventures, civilization-building simulators, and territorial strategy games.

It is particularly suitable for games in which the world's geography and history play a central role, allowing exploration to reveal past events and the environment to display visible consequences of natural transformations.

It can also be used as a worldbuilding element to explain the disappearance of civilizations, the formation of new geographical regions, the emergence of trade routes, and the distribution of resources and settlements.
