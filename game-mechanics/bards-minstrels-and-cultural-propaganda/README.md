# Bards, Minstrels and Cultural Propaganda

## Description

The Bards, Minstrels and Cultural Propaganda system establishes a mechanic for building and disseminating the player's notoriety through artists, musicians, poets, playwrights and traveling performers. Rather than relying exclusively on their own achievements and direct interactions with the population, players can hire artists to transform their accomplishments, stories and significant events from their journey into songs, poems, theatrical plays and public performances.

These artists act as independent agents of cultural dissemination, traveling throughout the kingdom and performing their works in taverns, town squares, markets, festivals, castles and other locations. Their performances influence how the population perceives the player, contributing to the development of their fame, reputation and public image.

However, artists do not function merely as instruments of propaganda. Each professional has their own economic interests, personality, ambitions and expectations. The relationship established with the player depends on factors such as compensation, treatment received, artistic freedom and professional recognition.

The system also accounts for negative consequences resulting from poorly managed business relationships. Artists who do not receive the agreed-upon payment may use their creativity and influence to produce satirical works, mocking songs and theatrical plays that ridicule the player. These works can spread throughout the kingdom, damaging their reputation and turning their own achievements into subjects of ridicule.

The goal is to create a dynamic cultural influence mechanic in which notoriety depends not only on the player's actions, but also on how their stories are interpreted, shared and preserved by others.

## Functionality

Players can hire individual artists or establish contracts with artistic companies to promote their image and achievements throughout the kingdom. Each professional has their own characteristics, which influence the quality of the works they produce, the reach of their performances and their ability to capture the public's attention.

Artists can have different specializations:

- Bards: compose and perform songs about the player's achievements. Their songs can be easily memorized, reproduced and spread by travelers, soldiers, merchants and tavern patrons.
- Minstrels: develop elaborate narratives about historical events, battles, conquests and episodes from the player's life. Their performances offer greater narrative depth and can contribute to building a heroic or legendary image.
- Troubadours: travel across different regions, carrying stories, poems and songs from one location to another. Their main characteristic is their ability to spread narratives geographically.
- Street performers: perform in town squares, markets and other high-traffic locations. Their reach is predominantly local, but they can significantly influence public opinion in the regions where they perform.
- Theater companies: produce plays reenacting events from the player's life. They can turn battles, political achievements, marriages, betrayals and controversial events into public spectacles.
- Poets and chroniclers: produce poems, verses and written accounts about the player. Their works can be reproduced, sold and preserved over time.

Each artist may have their own attributes:

- Artistic talent: determines the quality of their compositions and performances.
- Popularity: represents their ability to attract audiences and gain recognition.
- Reach: determines their ability to travel and promote works across different regions.
- Loyalty: represents their willingness to maintain a professional relationship with the player.
- Ambition: influences their financial and professional expectations.
- Creativity: determines their ability to produce original works and develop their own interpretations of events.

Hiring can take place through individual payments, recurring contracts or permanent sponsorships. Players can establish different levels of compensation depending on the artist's importance, the complexity of the works and the desired reach of their performances.

Honoring financial agreements is essential to maintaining professional relationships. Properly compensated artists tend to continue promoting the player, producing new compositions and dedicating greater attention to their performances. On the other hand, payment delays, insufficient compensation or breach of contract can lead to dissatisfaction and damage the relationship.

In more serious situations, dissatisfied artists may terminate their contracts and produce negative works about the player. A satirical song, for example, may turn an episode of cowardice into a popular narrative about their supposed lack of courage. A theater company may stage a mocking version of a military defeat or a controversial political decision.

These works spread through the artists' travels, the spontaneous reproduction of songs and stories by the population and the actions of other professionals interested in reproducing popular content. As a result, a composition initially performed in a small tavern may reach distant locations and become popular across different regions of the kingdom.

The impact of these performances may vary depending on the region. A song about a military victory may have a strong impact among soldiers and inhabitants of territories near the battlefield, while a theatrical play about a political decision may attract greater interest among merchants, nobles and city dwellers.

The system also distinguishes between notoriety and reputation. Notoriety represents how widely the player is recognized by the population, while reputation represents how positively or negatively people perceive them. Thus, a character may be widely known while also being despised, distrusted or ridiculed.

Another important aspect is artistic freedom. Professionals are not required to faithfully reproduce the player's version of events. They may exaggerate achievements, romanticize events, omit inconvenient details or develop their own interpretations. This allows different artists to produce distinct narratives about the same event, creating disputes over how history is interpreted.

The system may also include independent artists who produce works without any contractual relationship with the player. These professionals may spontaneously publicize events, criticize rulers, satirize important figures or even receive funding from third parties interested in damaging the player's image.

Finally, the works produced may outlive the artists and the events that inspired them. Songs, poems and popular plays may continue to be reproduced for generations, contributing to the construction of the player's historical legacy.

## Applications

Bards, Minstrels and Cultural Propaganda are particularly useful in RPGs and fantasy games that seek to establish a deeper relationship between the player's actions, society and the development of their public identity.

The mechanic allows notoriety to be developed through indirect activities, creating an alternative to conventional reputation systems based on quests, achievements and interactions with NPCs. Players must manage not only their own actions, but also how those actions are interpreted and presented to the population.

The existence of artists with their own characteristics, interests and personalities also encourages the emergence of dynamic situations. An artist may become an important ally in building the player's public image, while another may represent a threat if their interests are not met.

The distinction between notoriety and reputation allows for different narrative possibilities. A character may be known as a great hero, a benevolent ruler, a cruel conqueror or even a figure ridiculed by the population. These perceptions may influence diplomatic relations, soldier recruitment, trade, social interactions and the willingness of different groups to cooperate with the player.

The mechanic also allows for cultural and political rivalries between different characters. Rulers, nobles, factions and organizations may hire artists to promote their own images or damage the reputations of their adversaries. In this way, artistic production becomes a tool for political influence and propaganda.

Another relevant aspect is the construction of a historical legacy. The most important events of the player's journey may be transformed into cultural works that continue to circulate even after the character's death. A ruler may leave behind songs about their conquests, plays about their battles and poems that preserve their memory. Likewise, controversial decisions and failures may remain embedded in popular culture for generations.

The mechanic also offers opportunities for complementary economic systems involving artistic sponsorship, festival funding, the hiring of traveling companies, the commercialization of compositions and the maintenance of professional artists.

In this way, artists cease to function merely as mechanisms for gaining notoriety and become active agents in shaping the culture, collective memory and public opinion of the game's world.

- Kingdom Come: Deliverance: features a medieval setting in which reputation, social recognition and the circulation of information influence relationships between characters and communities.
- Crusader Kings III: features mechanics for building prestige, reputation and dynastic legacy, allowing the memory and public perception of rulers to have political significance.
- Fable: features social consequences associated with the player's fame and reputation, including different reactions from the population depending on their actions and behavior.
