# Multiple Scoreboards

## Description

The Multiple Scoreboards system allows a game to maintain different independent competitive rankings, each associated with a specific set of rules and participation conditions. Instead of grouping all players into a single overall ranking, the system divides competition into categories that reward distinct playstyles.

Each category establishes its own criteria, such as time restrictions, survival rules, progression limitations, or the absence of competitive advantages derived from equipment and items. This allows players with different approaches to compete under more balanced conditions, without a single progression model determining all rankings.

Participation in the rankings is optional and can be terminated by the player. However, leaving a category results in the permanent removal of the character from that scoreboard, preventing them from returning later with their previous competitive history.

## Functioning

During character creation, the player selects a competitive category. This choice determines the rules that will apply to that character and the ranking in which their results will be recorded.

Examples of categories include:

- Casual: establishes a daily gameplay time limit, restricting the amount of progress that can be achieved within a given period. The goal is to reduce the competitive advantage of players with greater time availability.
- Hardcore: allows only one life for the character. Death represents permanent elimination from the ranking, rewarding survival, planning, and risk management.
- Cosmetic: prevents equipment, items, or visual elements from providing competitive advantages. Progression can be recorded normally, but results depend exclusively on the player's skills and decisions.
- General: imposes no additional restrictions, allowing the use of all systems and resources available in the game.

Each scoreboard maintains its own ranking, results history, and scoring criteria. Characters are evaluated exclusively within their selected category, without direct comparison to players subject to different rules.

Participation can be terminated at any time. When abandoning a category, the character is permanently removed from its respective ranking, including their competitive history. This rule prevents players from switching between categories to gain strategic advantages or preserve previous positions after changes in competitive conditions.

The system may also offer separate rankings by season, region, character category, or other criteria, provided that each ranking has clearly defined rules that are consistently enforced.

## Applications

Multiple Scoreboards are particularly useful in games featuring a wide variety of progression styles and different levels of competitive commitment. The system allows casual players, specialists, and enthusiasts of extreme challenges to participate in competitions suited to their preferences.

In survival games, for example, a Hardcore category may reward the ability to stay alive for longer, while a Casual category may limit the influence of daily availability on rankings. In RPGs, Cosmetic rankings can highlight skill and knowledge of game mechanics without allowing more powerful equipment to determine the results.

This separation also reduces conflicts between players subject to different conditions. Instead of attempting to balance every possibility within a single ranking, the game defines specific competitive environments, each with its own merit criteria.

The system can also encourage the creation of specialized characters, increase the variety of competitive experiences, and extend player engagement through alternative challenges.
