# The Open Clinic: Care for the Forgotten

## Concept

A narrative medical simulation game set in an underserved area where access to healthcare is limited and demand for medical services consistently exceeds the available infrastructure's capacity.

The protagonist is a physician who decides to leave his career at a major hospital to open a small community clinic. Instead of working in a sophisticated medical facility, he begins treating people who often cannot find adequate care through other healthcare services.

The experience revolves around direct interaction with patients and their stories. Each person arrives at the clinic bringing not only symptoms but also financial difficulties, family problems, housing conditions, limited access to medication, and other circumstances that affect their health. The physician must listen, investigate, understand each patient's situation, and decide how to provide the most appropriate care within the available resources.

Although the game includes medical elements such as symptom assessment, identification of potential clinical conditions, treatment decisions, and follow-up care, its primary focus is on the human dimension of the profession. Not every problem can be solved in a single consultation, and ongoing care, guidance, and the development of a trusting relationship are often just as important as medical intervention.

The clinic faces scarce resources, growing demand, and situations that require prioritization. The protagonist must balance emergency cases, routine consultations, preventive care, and follow-up appointments for patients who depend on the service. His decisions directly influence the individual health of local residents and the community's relationship with the clinic.

Over time, the small establishment becomes more than just a medical office: it turns into a trusted point of reference for the local population. Individual stories become intertwined, patients return with new challenges, and the results of the physician's work gradually become visible in people's lives.

The narrative explores medicine as a practice of care, attentive listening, and social responsibility, showing that it is not always possible to solve every problem, but that attentive medical care can significantly change the course of someone's life.

## Objective

Build and maintain a trusted community clinic, providing medical care to a population with limited access to healthcare. The player must follow patients' stories, make clinical and personal decisions, and develop a lasting relationship of trust with the community.

## Core Mechanics

- Individualized narrative consultations featuring patients with different symptoms, personal stories, and needs.
- Medical history taking and clinical investigation through conversations, questions, and analysis of information provided by patients.
- Medical reasoning elements, including symptom assessment, diagnostic hypotheses, and treatment decisions.
- Longitudinal patient care, allowing players to observe the progression of medical conditions and treatments over time.
- Decisions regarding patient prioritization, balancing medical emergencies and routine consultations.
- Limited resource management, including medications, equipment, supplies, and patient care capacity.
- Preventive guidance and health education to reduce risks and improve the community's quality of life.
- Interconnected personal stories in which social and family circumstances influence characters' health.
- Narrative consequences of medical decisions, including positive outcomes, complications, and treatment limitations.
- Development of the clinic's reputation and residents' trust throughout the journey.
- Community evolution, reflecting the cumulative effects of medical care and the relationships established.

## References

- This Is the Police: decisions under pressure, limited resources, and narrative consequences.

- Coffee Talk: dialogue, attentive listening, and relationship development through individual interactions.
- Project Hospital: medical practices, diagnosis, and healthcare service organization.
- Papers, Please: decision-making under operational constraints and limited resources.
