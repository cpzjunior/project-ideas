# Neural Dystopia: The Price of Evolution

## Concept

A science fiction RPG set in a dystopian future dominated by megacorporations, where humanity lives in gigantic, overcrowded metropolises marked by extreme inequality, violence, exploitation, and deteriorating living conditions.

In this world, knowledge and skills can be transferred directly into the brain through implants connected to the base of the spinal cord. However, human neural capacity is limited, forcing each individual to carefully choose which knowledge they wish to acquire and preserve. The wealthiest can afford brain expansions that multiply their intellectual capacity, but they often have to sacrifice emotional and sensory functions such as empathy, attachment, and sensitivity. These modifications can also cause grotesque physical deformities, making technological evolution an increasingly alien transformation that distances individuals from their own humanity.

The player starts as an insignificant individual, struggling to survive in a brutal city where violence, exploitation, and crime are part of everyday life. Their initial goal is to earn enough money to buy a ticket to another planet and escape that environment. To achieve this, they must build a career within a megacorporation, accumulating knowledge, money, influence, and power, regardless of the methods required.

Corporate advancement offers increasingly greater opportunities, but also demands sacrifices. The player can develop skills, enhance their body and brain, establish alliances, manipulate colleagues and superiors, engage in industrial espionage, and resort to criminal activities to achieve their goals.

Intellectual development is not merely character progression, but also a choice about identity. Acquiring new knowledge may require replacing previously learned skills, while neural expansions allow individuals to overcome natural limitations at the cost of human functions. An extremely competent professional may eventually lose the ability to understand emotions, form bonds, or experience pleasure from their own achievements.

The cities are gigantic vertical structures, with populations crammed into degraded lower levels, contaminated industrial zones, commercial districts, and protected corporate complexes. The divide between social classes is reflected in the very architecture of the urban environment, while megacorporations control resources, opportunities, and virtually every aspect of everyday life.

In a society where intelligence is power, knowledge is currency, and humanity is a negotiable resource, the player must decide how far they are willing to go to achieve their freedom. However, the closer they get to the top, the harder it may become to recognize what they have left behind.

## Objective

Survive in a dystopian society, build a career within a megacorporation, and accumulate enough resources and influence to secure a ticket to another planet, deciding which intellectual, physical, and emotional sacrifices they are willing to make to achieve their freedom.

## Core Mechanics

- System for transferring knowledge and skills directly into the brain through neural implants.
- Limited neural capacity, requiring choices between knowledge and skills that can be acquired, preserved, or replaced.
- Brain expansions that increase intellectual capacity but may compromise emotional and sensory functions.
- Cybernetic enhancements and bodily modifications, with different benefits and side effects.
- Professional development and hierarchical advancement system within megacorporations.
- Corporate activities involving projects, negotiations, internal disputes, industrial espionage, and competition for opportunities.
- Economic system based on money, knowledge, influence, and access to resources.
- Criminal activities and illegal alternatives for obtaining resources, information, and advantages.
- Reputation and relationship system that influences alliances, opportunities, and conflicts.
- Exploration of vertical megacities with different social levels, industrial zones, commercial areas, and corporate districts.
- Choices and consequences that affect the character's professional trajectory, identity, and personal relationships.
- Progression based on decisions that can gradually compromise human traits in exchange for power and intellectual capacity.

## References

- The Matrix: direct knowledge transfer into the brain, neural interfaces, and questions about identity and humanity.
- Cyberpunk: dystopian aesthetics, megacorporations, social inequality, exploitation, and transhumanism.
- Warhammer 40,000: monumental cityscapes, overpopulation, degraded industrial environments, and a grimdark atmosphere.
