# The Lord of the Glitches: Bug of the Year Edition

## Concept

An action-adventure comedy game with strong metalinguistic elements, in which an ordinary gamer is mysteriously transported into the video game multiverse. During his journey, he encounters MissingNo, the legendary glitch creature from Pokémon Red & Blue, who becomes his guide and adventure companion. Her role is a direct reference to Navi, Link's fairy companion in The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, including the guidance and interaction dynamic between the characters.

MissingNo reveals that something is terribly wrong: video game universes are being struck by a growing wave of increasingly absurd bugs, glitches, and anomalies. However, her concern goes beyond the chaos. Faced with so many new problems, she fears that classic glitches will be forgotten and that her own existence will lose its significance in video game history.

Together, the protagonists travel through parodic versions of major franchises, recreating and exaggerating defining features of their original games.

In Dragon Land, a direct parody of The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim, mammoths fly through the skies, dragons defy the laws of physics, and NPCs exhibit completely bizarre behaviors, referencing the RPG's famous Bethesda bugs and glitches.

In Hidden Blade Anarchy, a parody of Assassin's Creed, ships appear out of nowhere, plunge into the ocean, and assassins climb invisible walls. The setting explores the navigation, movement, and parkour glitches associated with Ubisoft's franchise.

In The Legend of the Girl Who Does Nothing, a parody of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, the hero must rely on classic exploits such as bomb hovering, clipping, and wrong warps to overcome challenges, while Princess Zelda passively waits to be rescued. The adventure's structure itself references Link's journey, the temples, and the challenges of the original game.

As they explore these universes, the protagonists discover that the anomalies are no accidents. They are all part of a plan devised by Bitter Elder Inc., a satirical parody of Sweet Baby Inc. The corporation intends to standardize the multiverse, eliminate experiences it considers inappropriate, and turn every game into a platform for microtransactions, battle passes, and relentless monetization. Under the pretense of teaching players what truly makes a good video game, the organization seeks to control the identity and inner workings of every universe.

Now, it is up to the protagonist and his unlikely companion to stop this conspiracy by using precisely what the corporation wants to eliminate: bugs, glitches, and exploits. Each anomaly becomes a tool for breaking rules, overcoming obstacles, and subverting the systems attempting to control the game worlds.

The adventure combines exploration, combat, puzzles, and humor rooted in gaming culture, with explicit references to franchises, historical bugs, speedruns, memes, and absurd situations that have shaped video game history. The journey also explores the relationship between players and technical imperfections, arguing that not every flaw needs to be fixed and that some of gaming's most memorable moments came from things that were never supposed to happen.

## Objective

Travel across the video game multiverse, investigate the origin of the glitch wave, and stop Bitter Elder Inc.'s plans. The player must use bugs, exploits, and anomalies to overcome challenges, liberate affected universes, and preserve the diversity and identity of different game worlds.

## Core Mechanics

- Exploration of universes inspired by major video game franchises, each with its own visual identity, mechanics, and references.
- MissingNo as the protagonist's companion and guide, in a dynamic directly inspired by Navi from The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time.
- Glitch and exploit system that allows players to manipulate world rules, pass through environments, alter physics, and bypass obstacles.
- Use of classic and historically significant bugs as abilities and progression tools.
- Combat and puzzle-solving that require creativity and experimentation with anomalous behaviors.
- Worlds featuring unpredictable behaviors, including malfunctioning NPCs, physics-defying objects, and unexpected events.
- Parodies of famous franchises, recreating and exaggerating recognizable elements from their original games.
- Discovery of secret glitches and exploits scattered throughout the different universes.
- Metalinguistic interactions and dialogue featuring references to memes, speedruns, historical bugs, and gaming culture.
- Confrontations with agents and systems of Bitter Elder Inc., a parody of Sweet Baby Inc., attempting to standardize the universes and eliminate their anomalies.
- Progression based on discovering and combining glitches, allowing players to find new ways to explore and overcome challenges.
- Narrative following the relationship between the protagonist and MissingNo, including the companion's fear of being forgotten and losing her relevance.

## References

- Pokémon Red & Blue: MissingNo, the franchise's most iconic glitch, used as a central character in the adventure.
- The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time: Navi as the direct inspiration for the protagonist's companion; Link, Zelda, temples, adventure structure, and exploits such as bomb hovering, clipping, and wrong warps.
- The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim: direct inspiration for Dragon Land, including dragons, mammoths, NPCs, and the game's historical bugs.
- Assassin's Creed: direct inspiration for Hidden Blade Anarchy, including assassins, parkour, navigation, and movement glitches.
- Super Mario 64: movement glitches, clipping, and speedrunning techniques.
- The Stanley Parable: metalinguistic elements, humor, and deconstruction of video game conventions.
- Goat Simulator: use of bugs, absurd physics, and unexpected behaviors as central elements of the fun.
- Undertale: metalinguistic humor, references to gaming culture, and interaction with gaming conventions.
- Sweet Baby Inc.: satirical reference used to create Bitter Elder Inc., the story's corporate antagonist.
