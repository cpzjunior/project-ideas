# Endless Fronts: The Price of Peace

## Concept

A strategic and narrative-driven war game set in a fictional world marked by permanent military conflicts, territorial disputes, and geopolitical interests.

Two nations are fighting for regional hegemony, engaging in a conflict that seems to have no end. Behind this war lies a third power, militarily and economically far superior, which supplies both sides with weapons, equipment, and ammunition. Interested in the profits of the arms industry and the preservation of its geopolitical influence, this power deliberately manipulates the military balance, increasing its supply of resources to whichever side begins to lose, preventing either nation from achieving a definitive victory.

In addition to supplying weapons, this power finances rebel groups, separatist movements, and paramilitary organizations, interfering with the political stability of both nations and sabotaging diplomatic initiatives that could lead to the end of the conflict.

The player assumes the role of a military officer serving one of the warring nations, beginning their career in a low-ranking position. Throughout their career, they participate in military campaigns, command troops, earn recognition, and rise through the military hierarchy, progressively gaining greater authority and political influence.

However, their true challenge goes beyond winning battles. The player must find ways to break the cycle of warfare, whether through diplomatic negotiations, political agreements, intelligence operations, or military superiority. Imposing peace through force is also possible, but extremely difficult, as any attempt to definitively shift the balance of power triggers new interventions by the arms-supplying power.

The experience combines strategic battles, military career progression, diplomacy, intelligence, conflict management, and emergent storytelling. The player's decisions influence the regional balance of power, relations between nations, and the political stability of the world.

The universe features dynamic conflicts and events, allowing for extended campaigns and different historical outcomes. Even when a war ends, new challenges emerge in the reconstruction of nations, the maintenance of peace, and the prevention of future conflicts.

## Objective

Rise through the military hierarchy and gain enough authority and influence to break the cycles of warfare ravaging the world. The player must overcome the economic and geopolitical interests fueling these conflicts, establish the conditions for armistices and peace agreements, and build lasting regional stability.

## Main Mechanics

- Strategic battles between armies, featuring different units, equipment, weapons, and military capabilities.
- Military campaigns involving territorial disputes, offensive and defensive operations, and persistent consequences.
- Military career system featuring rank progression, promotions, responsibilities, and increasing player authority.
- Political influence system, allowing the player to establish relationships with rulers, military authorities, and civilian leaders.
- Diplomatic system featuring negotiations, ceasefires, prisoner exchanges, armistices, and peace treaties.
- Multiple paths to achieving peace, including diplomacy, political pressure, intelligence operations, and military superiority.
- Dynamic military balance system, in which the arms-supplying power increases its support for the losing side to prevent definitive victories.
- Foreign intervention system, featuring the supply of weapons, funding, credit, and logistical support to the warring parties.
- Rebel groups, separatists, and paramilitary organizations with their own objectives, interests, and agendas.
- Intelligence and investigation system to uncover clandestine operations, funding networks, and foreign interference.
- Dynamic narrative events, including terrorist attacks, political crises, sabotage, coups, and negotiation attempts.
- Relationships between characters and opposing military leaders, allowing for the development of rivalries, alliances, and diplomatic cooperation.
- Persistent consequences of military and political decisions, affecting national stability and the development of future conflicts.
- Reconstruction and peacekeeping system, involving military demobilization, economic recovery, political reconciliation, and regional stability.
- Potentially endless campaigns, with new conflicts, crises, and negotiation opportunities emerging as the world evolves.

## References

- Warhammer 40,000: for strategic battles, military factions, army building, and a universe of perpetual conflict.
- SPY x FAMILY: for its themes of espionage, geopolitical tensions, and efforts to prevent war between nations.
- Suzerain: for political decisions, diplomatic relations, and the consequences of player choices.
- Foxhole: for its depiction of persistent warfare, involving different military units and collective impact on the conflict.
- Military strategy and geopolitical simulation games featuring dynamic campaigns and diplomacy systems.
