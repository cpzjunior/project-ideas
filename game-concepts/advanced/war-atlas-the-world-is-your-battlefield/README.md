# War Atlas: The World Is Your Battlefield

## Concept

A global military strategy game set on a real, interactive world map, in which players can create wars in virtually any region of the planet by freely choosing the location, historical period, and nations or factions involved.

Unlike traditional strategy games, which use predefined maps and scenarios, War Atlas uses the planet itself as its game board. Players can navigate the world map, zoom in on any region, select a geographical area, and turn it into a war scenario. This region can encompass a continent, a country, an archipelago, an island, or any area defined by the player.

The geographical configuration can be combined with different historical periods, ranging from Antiquity to contemporary or futuristic scenarios. The selected period determines political borders, existing nations, available technologies, military units, equipment, and structures that can be used in the game.

Players can also select which nations, empires, kingdoms, or factions will participate in the conflict, following the historical configuration or creating alternative scenarios. This makes it possible to recreate historical conflicts, alter known events, or create entirely fictional wars in real-world locations.

The experience combines global-scale territorial strategy, military force organization, campaign planning, and battles. The main focus is on military operations and combat, while economic, political, diplomatic, and logistical systems serve as supporting elements without requiring the level of micromanagement found in traditional grand strategy games.

Battles are the central element of the experience. Players organize their armies, define formations, plan operations, and make command decisions, taking into account factors such as terrain, weather, technology, force composition, and supply availability. Combat can be resolved automatically or allow different levels of player intervention.

The map uses real geographical data, including terrain, rivers, mountains, forests, oceans, islands, cities, and infrastructure. These elements influence troop movement, logistics, territorial defense, and the development of battles.

The goal is to offer a geographical and historical war sandbox in which virtually any place on the planet can become a conflict scenario, without relying on predefined campaigns or maps.

## Objective

Create and command wars in any region of the planet by choosing the historical period, participating nations, and conditions of the conflict. Players must organize their military forces, plan campaigns, conquer territories, and win battles, using the geographical environment and available resources to achieve their military objectives.

## Main Mechanics

- **Interactive world map:** use of a real, continuous, and navigable geographical map, allowing players to explore any region of the planet through zoom and free navigation.
- **Custom geographical selection:** ability to select countries, regions, archipelagos, islands, or draw specific areas to define the war scenario.
- **Historical configuration:** selection of historical periods, determining borders, political entities, technologies, military units, and available structures.
- **Nation and faction selection:** choice of countries, empires, kingdoms, alliances, or participating factions, allowing for historical and alternate scenarios.
- **Dynamic scenario generation:** automatic creation of playable areas, territories, borders, and connections based on the selected geographical region.
- **Territorial strategy:** conquest, occupation, and defense of territories, with control over strategic regions and military objectives.
- **Military force organization:** creation of armies, unit composition, formation definitions, commander assignments, and troop distribution.
- **Battle system:** combat between military forces, taking into account army composition, positioning, terrain, weather, technology, and command decisions.
- **Different levels of intervention:** ability to automate combat, command operations, or assume more detailed control over specific battles.
- **Diverse military units:** infantry, cavalry, artillery, armored vehicles, aircraft, naval forces, and other units compatible with the selected period.
- **Terrain and geographical conditions:** influence of mountains, rivers, forests, deserts, oceans, islands, and urban areas on movement, defense, and combat.
- **Simplified logistics system:** management of supplies, ammunition, fuel, reinforcements, and supply lines, with automation to reduce micromanagement.
- **Military infrastructure and structures:** use of ports, airports, railways, roads, fortifications, bases, and industrial facilities according to the historical period.
- **Technology and military evolution:** availability of equipment, weapons, and military capabilities compatible with the historical period or futuristic scenario.
- **Simplified diplomatic system:** ability to establish alliances, agreements, non-aggression pacts, and other relationships between participants.
- **Custom victory conditions:** definition of objectives such as territorial conquest, control of strategic regions, destruction of enemy forces, or survival.
- **Historical and alternate scenarios:** recreation of real wars, alteration of historical events, and creation of fictional conflicts in real-world locations.
- **Artificial intelligence:** automated control of opposing nations, commanders, and military forces, allowing for single-player or multiplayer matches.
- **Different scales of conflict:** ability to create local conflicts involving a few islands or regions, as well as large-scale wars involving entire continents.
- **Geographical and historical sandbox:** freedom to combine different regions, periods, participants, and war conditions, creating virtually unlimited scenarios.

- **Europa Universalis IV:** for territorial representation, national expansion, and conflicts across different historical periods.
- **Crusader Kings II:** for the representation of kingdoms, empires, historical borders, and territorial disputes.
- **Command: Modern Operations:** for military simulation, geographical representation, and operational planning across different environments.
- **Total War:** for military battles, army composition, formations, and tactical decisions during combat.
- **Risk:** for territorial conquest mechanics and military expansion across geographical regions.
