# Time is Money: Life’s Ledger

## Concept

A life simulation game inspired by The Game of Life, in which time is literally the only currency available. Each person starts with a limited amount of time and must decide how to spend it throughout their life. Working, studying, traveling, building relationships, taking care of one's health, and pursuing dreams are all activities that consume time, making every choice a trade-off between different possibilities.

The player progresses through different stages of life, facing opportunities, unexpected events, and decisions that can change their path. Investing time in a career can lead to financial prosperity, while dedicating time to family and friends can provide meaningful experiences and relationships. Some choices offer immediate rewards, others require long-term commitment, and many opportunities disappear as time goes by.

The game puts the relationship between time, money, success, and happiness into perspective. Unlike traditional simulators, accumulating wealth does not necessarily mean having a better life: money can make certain experiences easier to access, but it cannot buy back time already spent or recover missed opportunities.

The experience aims to encourage reflection on the value of time and how each person chooses to spend it. There is no universally correct path or single formula for a successful life. The real challenge is to build an existence that feels meaningful to the player before their time runs out.

## Objective

Manage the time available throughout life, making decisions that balance different aspects of existence and shape a meaningful personal journey. At the end, the player evaluates the legacy they have left behind and the experiences they have lived, realizing that their greatest accumulated asset is not necessarily financial.

## Core Mechanics

- Time as universal currency: every activity, decision, and opportunity has a cost measured in units of time.
- Progression through different stages of life, each with its own opportunities and challenges.
- Choices between career, education, relationships, leisure, travel, health, and personal projects.
- Opportunity system featuring immediate benefits and long-term consequences.
- Management of priorities and opportunity costs, taking into account what is left undone when choosing a particular activity.
- Unexpected events that can disrupt plans and require players to reorganize their available time.
- Development of personal, professional, and social attributes and achievements.
- End-of-life evaluation of the player's journey, considering experiences, relationships, accomplishments, happiness, and legacy.

## References

- The Game of Life: board game structure based on different stages of life and the choices made along the way.
- The Sims: simulation of everyday life, needs, relationships, and personal development.
- Persona 5: time management and choices between competing activities.
- In Time: conceptual inspiration for the idea of time as currency and a resource for survival.
