# Rio Operations: War Against the Narco-State

## Concept

A strategy, management, and political simulation game set in a major Brazilian metropolis facing a collapse of public security. Different criminal organizations exert influence over territories, compete for strategic areas, and undermine the functioning of institutions, while corruption weakens the State's ability to respond.

The player assumes the role of Secretary of Public Security, responsible for coordinating efforts to reclaim territories, weaken criminal organizations, and restore the State's institutional presence. However, taking control of a region does not necessarily mean securing lasting control over it. Stability also depends on public trust, the continuity of public services, and the State's ability to maintain its presence after operations.

The player's responsibilities include tactical operations, intelligence, investigations, strategic planning, and public policy. Every decision has consequences that extend beyond the immediate results of operations. Poorly planned actions can lead to casualties, public outrage, community displacement, and a loss of institutional legitimacy.

Corruption represents a constant threat. Infiltrated officials may divert resources, leak classified information, compromise investigations, and sabotage operations. Combating these networks requires internal oversight mechanisms, investigations, and institutional strengthening, but may also provoke resistance within the administration itself.

The environment evolves continuously: criminal organizations adapt their strategies, territorial influence shifts, alliances are formed, and unexpected crises put the administration under pressure. The player must balance security, legitimacy, and operational capacity, understanding that territorial recovery will only be sustainable when accompanied by the restoration of public trust in institutions.

## Objective

Restore public security and the effective presence of the State throughout the metropolis by weakening criminal organizations, fighting corruption, and rebuilding public trust without compromising institutional stability.

## Core Mechanics

- Strategic public security management, coordinating human, financial, and operational resources.
- Dynamic territorial map, with regions under varying levels of state and criminal influence.
- Planning and coordination of tactical operations to reclaim areas and combat criminal organizations.
- Intelligence and investigation system, allowing the player to identify leaders, financial structures, networks of influence, and infiltrated officials.
- Criminal organizations with adaptive strategies, internal disputes, and the ability to reorganize.
- Institutional corruption, including resource diversion, information leaks, sabotage, and compromised operations.
- Public policies and state presence initiatives to consolidate territorial control after operations.
- Legitimacy and public support system, influenced by operational outcomes, abuses, transparency, and the quality of public services.
- Crisis management and dynamic events, requiring responses to conflicts, allegations, scandals, and shifts in the political landscape.
- Long-term consequences, in which operational and administrative decisions affect security, institutional trust, and the stability of the metropolis.
