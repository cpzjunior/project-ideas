# Arrow in the Knee: Profiting from the Civil War

## Concept

An economic simulation, management, and adventure game set in a medieval fantasy world engulfed in a civil war. Inspired by the famous arrow-to-the-knee joke from *The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim*, the protagonist is a former soldier who was injured in combat and discharged from military service.

With an insufficient pension to make ends meet, he is forced to find a new profession. Taking advantage of the connections he built during his time in the army, he decides to enter the trading business, buying, selling, and bartering goods between different regions and factions involved in the conflict.

As his business grows, he begins offering additional services, such as delivering correspondence, brokering agreements, and transporting information. Eventually, he turns his operation into a sort of primitive wartime bank, keeping records of debts, storing goods on behalf of others, and providing loans based on reputation and trust.

The expansion of his business increases his political and economic influence, but also attracts the attention of bandits, opportunists, and traitors. The protagonist must manage his resources, protect his investments, and navigate relationships between rival factions, preventing the war from destroying everything he has built.

All of this serves a rather peculiar purpose: accumulating enough wealth to fulfill his greatest dream of marrying a wealthy woman and finally retiring.

## Objective

Build a fortune during the civil war by expanding a commercial and financial operation until achieving enough economic independence to permanently leave behind the life of adventure and conflict.

## Main Mechanics

- Trading system based on buying, selling, and bartering goods.
- Dynamic economy influenced by the war, resource scarcity, and territorial control by factions.
- Transportation and delivery of goods between different regions.
- Reputation and relationship system involving characters and factions.
- Messaging and correspondence delivery services between rival groups.
- Brokerage of commercial agreements and negotiations.
- Simplified banking system for recording debts, deposits, and loans.
- Credit management based on customers' reputation and reliability.
- Risks involving defaults, theft, sabotage, and betrayal.
- Business expansion through investments and employee recruitment.
- Protection of goods, properties, and commercial operations.
- Economic progression focused on accumulating wealth and achieving retirement.
