# Shelter Duty: Hope After the Storm

## Concept

A humanitarian simulation, management, and narrative game inspired by major natural disasters, such as the floods in Rio Grande do Sul and Hurricane Katrina. The player assumes the role of a volunteer working at an emergency shelter set up in a school gymnasium after a catastrophe devastates the region.

The shelter continuously receives people who have lost their homes, belongings, and, in some cases, family members. Amid makeshift facilities and scarce resources, the volunteer must organize the distribution of food, water, clothing, medicine, and other essential supplies, as well as handle urgent requests and help resolve everyday problems.

However, the work goes beyond logistics. Every person taken in has a personal story shaped by loss, worries, needs, and expectations. The player must dedicate time to listening to survivors, providing emotional support, and understanding their circumstances, even when the number of requests seems impossible to handle.

Life at the shelter is marked by unpredictable situations. New families arrive, supplies run out, equipment breaks down, and urgent needs compete for the volunteer's attention. It will not always be possible to help everyone immediately, making it necessary to establish priorities and make difficult decisions.

The experience combines resource management with human stories and narrative interactions, highlighting the importance of solidarity and community organization during emergencies. The focus is not on saving the world single-handedly, but on understanding how small acts of care can make a difference in the lives of those who have lost almost everything.

## Objective

Help people affected by the disaster, keeping the shelter operational and managing limited resources while providing care, support, and hope to survivors.

## Core Mechanics

- Emergency shelter management, organizing spaces, infrastructure, and accommodations for survivors.
- Distribution of essential resources, including food, water, clothing, medicine, and hygiene supplies.
- Handling individual requests and urgent needs from shelter residents.
- Priority system, requiring decisions about who needs help first when resources are limited.
- Supply logistics, receiving, organizing, and transporting materials throughout the shelter.
- Characters with personal stories, specific needs, concerns, and different experiences.
- Narrative interactions, allowing the player to listen to personal accounts, provide emotional support, and build relationships with survivors.
- Emergency events and unexpected situations that change the shelter's needs and operations.
- Time and activity management for the volunteer, balancing operational tasks with personal attention to residents.
- Consequences of decisions, reflected in the safety, well-being, and hope of the sheltered community.

## References

- This War of Mine: civilian survival, resource scarcity, and humanitarian decisions during crises.
- Do Not Feed the Monkeys: observation of individual stories and interconnected human situations.
- Spiritfarer: providing care, interpersonal relationships, and emotional storytelling.
- Frostpunk: management of limited resources and difficult decisions during a collective crisis.
