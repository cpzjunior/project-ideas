# Micro World Conquest: Biological Supremacy

## Concept

A strategy and civilization-building game inspired by Europa Universalis and Civilization, in which the player controls a microscopic civilization seeking to expand its territory and ensure its survival within a living, dynamic biological environment.

The world is a complex ecosystem made up of different regions of the host organism, each with its own characteristics, resources, environmental conditions, and threats. The player must explore new territories, compete for resources, develop technologies, and adapt their civilization to constant environmental changes.

Territorial expansion brings the civilization into conflict with rival microscopic species, predators, and other life forms. Beyond these disputes, the host organism itself represents a systemic threat: its immune system can identify and attack the civilization's populations, territories, and structures, requiring adaptation and strategic planning.

Every decision affects not only the civilization's development but also the ecosystem's balance. Excessive resource exploitation, the elimination of species, and changes to environmental conditions can produce unexpected consequences, shifting power dynamics and creating new threats.

Victory does not necessarily depend on military conquest. The player can pursue supremacy through territorial domination, technological and biological evolution, or strategic cooperation with other microscopic species.

## Objective

Develop a microscopic civilization capable of surviving, expanding its influence, and achieving supremacy within the ecosystem by choosing between different paths of evolution, domination, or cooperation.

## Core Mechanics

- Territorial expansion across different regions of the host organism, each with specific conditions and resources.
- Exploration, collection, and management of biological resources required for the civilization's survival and development.
- Technological development and biological evolution, unlocking new capabilities and adaptations.
- Territorial disputes and conflicts with rival microscopic civilizations.
- Diplomacy, alliances, trade, and strategic cooperation between species.
- Dynamic ecosystem in which the actions of civilizations alter environmental balance and resource availability.
- Host immune system as a dynamic threat, with responses that can compromise territories and populations.
- Microscopic predators and unpredictable environmental events that require strategic adaptation.
- Multiple victory conditions, including territorial supremacy, evolutionary superiority, and hegemony through cooperation.

## References

- Europa Universalis: territorial expansion, diplomacy, and geopolitical strategy.
- Civilization: technological development, civilization evolution, and multiple paths to victory.
- Microbiology and ecology: competition, adaptation, symbiosis, and ecosystem balance.
