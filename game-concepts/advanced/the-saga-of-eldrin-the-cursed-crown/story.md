# The Saga of Eldrin

## Prologue: The Endless War

Velmoria was once the greatest empire on the continent, a power whose authority extended far beyond its borders and whose wealth fueled the trade, culture, and prosperity of countless peoples. For generations, the imperial dynasty had maintained control over distinct territories, united by the strength of its institutions and the legitimacy of a bloodline that seemed destined to rule forever. This stability, however, depended on a balance that proved more fragile than its contemporaries had imagined.

The death of Emperor Eldric IV, without leaving any heirs, shattered that balance. The absence of a legitimate successor transformed old rivalries into open disputes, and the great lords, once united in their submission to the crown, began claiming for themselves the right to restore imperial order. None of them, however, was willing to recognize the authority of the others. Before long, Velmoria ceased to exist as a political entity and fragmented into seven kingdoms, each advancing its own claims to the imperial inheritance. What should have been a succession dispute became a prolonged war, in which territories changed hands, old alliances were broken, and entire generations grew up knowing no reality other than violence.

The war destroyed more than just the structures of power. Trade routes were disrupted, cities lost their importance, fields remained abandoned, and the population was left struggling to survive amid arbitrary taxes, looting, and epidemics. The nobility continued fighting over titles and borders, but the cost of their ambitions fell upon those who had no part in choosing their rulers. Among the survivors, an old belief gained strength: imperial blood carried a curse, and all who came near it were eventually consumed by the struggle for power.

While the descendants of the old imperial houses fought over a crown that no one could hold, far from the battlefields and aristocratic halls, a boy was growing up in the streets of Ravencourt. The city, once an important commercial center, had lost much of its prosperity and become a place of abandonment, crime, and inequality. His name was Eldrin. An orphan, with no family or prospects for the future, he seemed destined to disappear among the thousands of individuals forgotten by the war. Even so, there was a restlessness within him that prevented him from accepting his circumstances as something permanent. Eldrin knew nothing of his origins and did not understand the extent of the forces that had shaped the world around him, but, unbeknownst to him, his life was tied to the same bloodline whose struggle had condemned Velmoria to ruin.

## Act I: The Beginning of the Journey

### Chapter 1: The Struggle for Survival

Hunger taught Eldrin to recognize opportunities where others saw only hardship. After the orphanage in Ravencourt closed, while still a child, he was forced to find his own means of staying alive. With no resources and no adult willing to take responsibility for him, he joined other orphans who survived through petty theft, scams, and odd jobs. The group offered some protection, but surviving on the streets required far more than belonging to a makeshift community.

Ravencourt was a city where poverty had become part of the landscape and the law existed primarily to protect the interests of those who could afford it. Eldrin learned to observe the guards' movements, identify distracted merchants, recognize traps, and quickly assess people's behavior. Distrust ceased to be a reaction and became a method. Over time, he developed enough skill to carry out increasingly risky thefts, although he knew that any mistake could cost him his freedom or his life.

Survival, however, did not eliminate his dissatisfaction. Eldrin understood that he had learned how to stay alive, but not how to build a life. He did not want to spend the coming years running from guards, fighting other wretches over scraps, and depending on luck to secure his next meal. The ambition beginning to take shape within him did not yet have a defined objective, but it already marked a break from the fate that seemed to await him.

This determination, however, was not enough to protect him from the consequences of his own inexperience. While attempting to steal a shipment of more valuable goods, Eldrin misjudged the situation and was caught. What initially seemed like another encounter with the city guards turned out to be something different: the men who had surrounded him did not work for Ravencourt's administration, but for one of the region's most influential merchants.

### Chapter 2: An Opportunity Arises

Garrick Thorne had built his influence far beyond conventional trade. He controlled routes, financed commercial operations, and maintained relationships with authorities, nobles, and intermediaries scattered across different territories. His wealth allowed him to compete in markets on favorable terms, but his true advantage lay in his ability to turn information into power. He knew which goods would become scarce before prices rose, which authorities could be persuaded with money, and which political disputes represented commercial opportunities. To protect his interests, he maintained a private militia composed of trained men directly subordinate to his authority.

It was before this man that Eldrin found himself after his failed theft attempt. Taken to a warehouse away from the main markets, he was brought before Garrick, who examined him with the attention of someone seeking to assess not only the immediate value of something, but also what it might become. The merchant realized that the boy possessed agility, observational skills, and, above all, an unusual resistance to intimidation.

Instead of handing him over to the authorities, Garrick offered him a choice. He could continue living as a street thief, exposed to violence and insecurity, or accept an opportunity to learn how to operate in a world where wealth and influence determined who could impose their own rules. Eldrin did not trust the man, but he understood that he was unlikely to find a better alternative. He accepted the offer and began working under Garrick's protection.

The relationship between the two began as a combination of self-interest and convenience. Garrick had no intention of adopting an orphan out of generosity, and Eldrin had no reason to mistake the opportunity he had been given for an altruistic gesture. Even so, the merchant recognized a talent in him that justified the investment. Gradually, Eldrin moved beyond performing simple tasks and began accompanying negotiations, managing small shipments, and dealing with suppliers and intermediaries. He learned that trade depended less on the mere movement of goods than on controlling the conditions under which they were exchanged. A safe road could be worth more than a gold mine; information obtained at the right moment could prevent a war or bring down a competitor.

Garrick also taught him that economic power did not exist separately from politics and force. Contracts needed to be honored, but not all men honored contracts. Merchants depended on authorities, authorities depended on financing, and both had to consider those who had armed men at their disposal. Eldrin began to understand that human relationships were structured around interests, and that the ability to identify those interests made it possible to influence decisions without necessarily resorting to violence.

Under Garrick's guidance, he gradually abandoned the mindset of someone seeking only the next opportunity to survive and began thinking in terms of wealth, influence, and continuity. Wealth ceased to represent merely material security. It became an instrument for gaining autonomy and establishing a position that no one could easily take away. The boy who had once run from the guards was beginning to learn how the men who hired them exercised their power.

### Chapter 3: The Merchant of Fernholm

Years of learning transformed Eldrin into a man capable of conducting his own business. After accumulating experience, capital, and commercial connections, he settled in Fernholm, where he founded a workshop focused on the production and trade of worked metals. The enterprise prospered because Eldrin understood both production and distribution mechanisms. Rather than relying exclusively on occasional buyers, he built stable relationships with suppliers, merchants, and customers from different regions, diversifying his activities and reducing his exposure to the risks of any single market.

His rise attracted the attention of people who, until then, would never have considered a former orphan from Ravencourt relevant. Eldrin began frequenting commercial and political circles that had previously been inaccessible to him, negotiating with representatives of noble houses and participating in agreements involving considerable sums. His wealth gave him a respectable standing, but it did not eliminate the social barriers separating merchants from the aristocracy.

The more his wealth grew, the more evident the need to protect it became. Competitors began to see his expansion as a threat, while members of the nobility considered his economic influence an improper interference in matters they believed belonged exclusively to traditional families. Eldrin discovered that commercial success did not produce opportunities alone. It also created dependencies, stirred rivalries, and attracted individuals interested in controlling what he had built.

Politics, which until then had served primarily to facilitate his business, began to occupy a central place in his decisions. Every tax concession, supply contract, or transportation agreement also had to be evaluated in terms of its political consequences. Wealth could buy protection, but protection often required commitments that restricted the very freedom money was supposed to guarantee.

Eldrin thus understood that he had moved beyond the stage where being a skilled merchant was enough. To preserve his position, he would need to establish relationships with those who held territorial and military authority. His rise depended on transforming wealth into political influence, but this transformation would bring him closer to the very world of disputes and interests from which he had tried to distance himself.

## Act II: Social Ascension

### Chapter 4: The Kidnapping of the Duke's Daughter

The opportunity to enter the aristocracy arose under circumstances Eldrin could never have anticipated. A conspiracy intended to weaken the influence of House Valmont resulted in the kidnapping of Elyana, daughter of Duke Armand. The operation had been planned to strike directly at the duke's political standing, using the young woman as an instrument of pressure and destabilization. When Eldrin learned what had happened, he realized that the incident posed a threat to the region, but also presented an opportunity to demonstrate that his influence extended beyond commerce.

The situation demanded more than money or negotiation. Those responsible for the kidnapping had mercenaries and assassins at their disposal, and any rescue attempt would have to account for the possibility that Elyana might be killed if her captors felt threatened. Eldrin gathered men he trusted and organized an operation to locate the hideout and extract the young woman before her captors could react.

The operation was violent and required Eldrin to draw upon skills he had developed long before becoming a wealthy man. His experience on the streets of Ravencourt, his ability to assess threats, and his willingness to act under pressure proved just as important as the resources he now possessed. After confronting those responsible for the kidnapping, he managed to get Elyana out of the city and lead her to safety along a route that avoided the main roads controlled by the conspirators.

During the escape, the young woman realized that her rescuer did not fit the conventional image of a merchant. Eldrin possessed resources and influence, but he also knew violence firsthand and did not hesitate to take risks when he considered it necessary. The experience created a closeness between them that went beyond Elyana's gratitude and Eldrin's political interests, although neither was in a position to ignore the consequences of a relationship between a merchant of no aristocratic origin and the daughter of one of Althoria's most powerful men.

Upon returning with Elyana, Eldrin was received by Duke Armand, who acknowledged the importance of the rescue. The duke offered him a reward, perhaps expecting him to ask for money, commercial privileges, or some limited benefit. Eldrin, however, had identified a far greater opportunity. Instead of expanding his wealth, he decided to ask for something that could permanently transform his social standing.

### Chapter 5: The Baron of Eldermere

Eldrin requested dominion over Eldermere, a region that had lost almost all its economic and political importance. At first glance, the grant seemed unappealing. The city was run-down, its lands had been abandoned, and its population lived in precarious conditions. Many of its inhabitants had been displaced by the war, while the few nobles who still owned property in the region preferred to keep their distance from a territory that required constant investment and offered uncertain returns.

Armand accepted the request and granted Eldrin the title of Baron. The decision represented a reward for his service, but it also allowed the duke to place the administration of a troubled region in the hands of someone who had demonstrated organizational ability and possessed resources of his own. For Eldrin, the grant meant far more than a title. For the first time, he would have territorial authority, a political base, and the opportunity to build a community under rules not determined exclusively by the interests of other lords.

His status as a newcomer to the aristocracy, however, presented immediate difficulties. Traditional nobles viewed a man whose wealth came not from inheritance and whose position depended on a duke's grant with suspicion. To them, Eldrin had received a distinction that should have belonged to families whose bloodlines had sustained the social order for generations. The title granted him formal authority, but did not guarantee recognition.

Eldrin understood that he could not overcome this resistance simply by claiming the right to rule. He needed to demonstrate that his administration would produce results the old lords had never managed to deliver. Rather than attempting to reproduce the model of exploitation prevalent on neighboring estates, he began reorganizing the region around economic recovery and security. He offered work to the population, encouraged displaced families to return, and brought together people who until then had no prospect of rebuilding their lives.

This policy was not driven by idealism alone. Eldrin understood the economic value of a stable, productive, and protected population. He knew that Eldermere's recovery would depend on its ability to attract workers, merchants, and investors, and that none of them would remain in a region where violence could destroy their resources at any moment. At the same time, he understood that the trust of the inhabitants was a source of legitimacy that his title alone could not provide.

Reconstruction therefore began by creating the minimum conditions necessary for the city to function again. Eldrin needed to transform a domain despised by the aristocracy into an economically viable and politically relevant entity. The success of this undertaking would also demonstrate that his rise did not depend solely on Armand's protection, but on an administrative ability that could guarantee him autonomy.

### Chapter 6: The Southern Commercial Hub

Eldermere's geographical position offered an advantage that its former owners had overlooked. Located at a strategic point along the southern trade routes, the city could connect markets that until then had depended on longer and less secure journeys. Its harbor, although deteriorated, was still capable of receiving vessels, and restoring its facilities would allow the region to become an important center for the movement of goods.

The main obstacle was the lack of security. The roads were infested with bandits, rural properties suffered frequent attacks, and the small groups of soldiers still present in the region were incapable of simultaneously protecting the fields, routes, and city. The neighboring nobles, meanwhile, showed no interest in cooperating with a baron they considered socially inferior. The Count of Fernholm, to whom Eldrin owed fealty, kept his distance and offered no meaningful support.

Faced with this situation, Eldrin decided to organize Eldermere's security using his own resources. He gathered veterans, recruited men from the local population, and began operations against the groups controlling the roads. The crackdown on banditry was accompanied by the restoration of administrative structures and the reorganization of tax collection. His intention was to establish an authority capable of guaranteeing predictability, an indispensable condition for trade to prosper again.

Security, however, was only part of the problem. A city without economic activity could not sustain the expenses of its own defense for long. Eldrin needed to attract outside capital and find partners willing to invest in a region whose reputation was still associated with decline. It was in this context that he established contact with merchants from the Republic of Vaylen, offering favorable tax conditions in exchange for investments, the hiring of local workers, and the use of Eldermere's harbor as a distribution center.

The agreement required considerable tax concessions, reducing the barony's immediate revenue, but Eldrin believed that the expansion of economic activity would generate far greater benefits in the long term. The arrival of the merchants stimulated the harbor's recovery, increased demand for services, and created new job opportunities. Gradually, Eldermere ceased to be merely an abandoned territory and became part of a commercial network connecting different regions of the continent.

Growth, however, brought new disputes. Merchants from neighboring cities realized that Eldermere was beginning to compete directly with their markets and began pressuring their respective lords to limit the baron's expansion. Eldrin responded by avoiding turning economic competition into an open political conflict. Rather than excluding traditional merchants, he offered them agreements that allowed them to participate in the city's growth, gradually integrating them into the commercial network he was building.

This strategy expanded his influence beyond the borders of his own domain. Eldrin now controlled not only a recovering city, but also a network of economic relationships involving foreign investors, regional merchants, and local authorities. His political position began to strengthen because different groups had become, to some extent, dependent on the continuity of his rule.

News of Eldermere's transformation reached Duke Armand. What had initially seemed like a grant made to an ambitious merchant had become an undertaking of strategic importance to the entire southern region. Armand began observing Eldrin more closely, realizing that his ability to organize resources and build alliances could prove useful in disputes far greater than those involving the barony.

For Eldrin, Eldermere's prosperity confirmed that it was possible to build power without relying exclusively on aristocratic inheritance. But success also made him more visible. The more his influence grew, the harder it would become to remain on the sidelines of the conflicts that continued to divide the former imperial territories.

## Act III: War and Military Ascension

### Chapter 7: Rumors of War

Eldermere's stability depended on a regional context that was beginning to deteriorate. The historical rivalry between Althoria and Ostgard had never been resolved, and territorial disputes between the two kingdoms continued to fuel tensions along their borders. Eldrin knew that his city's economic expansion would not be enough to protect it if a large-scale conflict broke out again. Therefore, even while prioritizing trade and reconstruction, he had already begun a program to strengthen its defenses.

The city walls were repaired, the most vulnerable sections were reinforced, and a defensive moat was built to hinder direct attacks. The secondary gates were closed, while the main gate was fitted with structures capable of withstanding prolonged assaults. On nearby rural estates, Eldrin ordered the construction of fences and outer barriers that could slow the advance of raiders and give the population time to move safely into the city.

Watchtowers were also erected along the coast, making it possible to identify suspicious vessels and transmit warnings in advance. These measures came at a high cost. Economic recovery was still underway, and maintaining a permanent military force required expenses that compromised resources intended for development. Eldrin, however, considered security an indispensable investment. There was little point in building a prosperous commercial center if its facilities could be destroyed in the first military campaign.

As rumors of war intensified, it became clear that the available resources would not be sufficient to complete all the works. Eldrin therefore decided to turn to Garrick Thorne, his former mentor, requesting a loan and offering his properties as collateral. Garrick not only agreed to finance the project, but also sent experienced builders to accelerate the restoration of the fortifications. The decision demonstrated that, despite the independence Eldrin had achieved, his journey remained connected to the commercial relationships he had built during the early years of his rise.

When Ostgard finally invaded Althoria's borders, Eldermere was in a particularly vulnerable position. The region's nobles hesitated to mobilize their forces, waiting for instructions and trying to assess the risks of committing to a campaign whose outcome was still uncertain. Eldrin did not have that luxury of time. The threat directly affected his domain, and any delay could jeopardize everything he had built.

He therefore assumed responsibility for the defense. He mobilized the inhabitants fit to fight, reorganized his troops, and accelerated the training of new soldiers. His military experience was still limited, but his ability to plan, manage resources, and read circumstances allowed him to compensate for some of these shortcomings. Eldrin did not possess the command tradition of the great generals, but he knew the limits of his forces and understood that an effective defense would depend on discipline, preparation, and intelligent use of the terrain.

The war represented a definitive turning point in his journey. Until then, his rise had been built primarily through wealth, administration, and negotiation. Now he needed to prove that he could preserve his power when all these instruments proved insufficient. Eldermere's survival would depend on his ability to transform economic resources into military strength and assume responsibilities that extended beyond the boundaries of his domain.

### Chapter 8: The Bloody Battle

The Ostgardian advance on Eldermere was marked by successive attacks against the city's defensive positions. Enemy troops tested the gates, bombarded the walls, and sought to identify the points where the fortifications were most vulnerable. Eldrin's preparations prevented a swift takeover, but did not eliminate the strain caused by the siege. The defenders had to manage supplies, replace combatants, and maintain discipline while the invaders sought to exploit any sign of weakness.

The prolonged resistance forced Ostgard's commanders to revise their plans. Taking Eldermere directly would require more resources and time than they had anticipated. Rather than persist with the siege, they decided to attack Fernholm, whose position could provide a military base for subsequent operations against Eldermere. The city, besides being economically important, was practically undefended and had no commander capable of organizing a consistent resistance.

The threat completely changed the situation. If Fernholm were captured, the Ostgardians could establish their supply lines there and prepare a new siege under far more favorable conditions. Eldrin understood that remaining behind his own walls would mean allowing the enemy to consolidate a strategic position. Despite the risks, he gathered his most experienced soldiers and decided to advance on Fernholm, seeking to strike the invading forces before they had time to organize their defenses.

The attack was planned to exploit the vulnerability created by the occupation itself. Ostgard's troops were concentrated on looting and controlling the city, with some of their contingents dispersed and their lines of communication poorly protected. Eldrin took advantage of this disorganization to launch an unexpected offensive against the enemy rear. The surprise caused confusion among the Ostgardian commanders, who were unable to coordinate an effective response.

The invading formations began to disintegrate under the pressure of the attacks, and the retreat, initially organized, turned into a disorderly rout. Eldrin managed to prevent Fernholm from being used as a base for a new offensive against Eldermere and forced the Ostgardians to abandon the region. The victory consolidated his reputation as a commander, but did not prevent the city from suffering irreparable losses.

Among the dead were Count Renault D'Orsin and his family, murdered before Eldrin could reach their positions. Renault had been an uncooperative lord, frequently indifferent to the difficulties faced by his subordinates, but he was still Eldrin's liege lord. The count's death, which occurred while he was trying to prevent the occupation of his lands, represented a failure that Eldrin could not dismiss as merely an inevitable consequence of war. The responsibility for not arriving in time stayed with him.

Fernholm had been liberated, but it was deeply devastated. The victory could not be mistaken for an immediate restoration of normality. Even so, the military outcome changed Eldrin's position within Althoria. His actions had demonstrated that he could organize a regional defense when traditional nobles failed, and his reputation quickly spread beyond Eldermere. To the population, he had become one of the main figures responsible for preventing the invasion from advancing. To the aristocracy, his rise was beginning to represent a problem that could no longer be ignored.

### Chapter 9: The New Count

Renault D'Orsin's death opened a succession dispute over Fernholm. Aristocratic tradition pointed to the family's heirs, whose rights were supported by bloodline and the conventions governing the transfer of titles. Armand, however, made a different decision. Recognizing the strategic importance of the victory and the ability Eldrin had demonstrated, he granted him the County of Fernholm.

The choice had a political justification. The duke needed someone capable of administering and defending a region that had become essential to Althoria's stability. Renault's heirs possessed familial legitimacy, but had not demonstrated the same organizational ability nor offered Armand guarantees of loyalty. Eldrin, on the other hand, owed his rise to the duke himself and had proven that he could turn limited resources into concrete results.

The grant provoked a strong reaction among members of the traditional nobility. To them, the appointment represented a break with the principles underpinning the aristocratic hierarchy. A man with no noble ancestry had received one of the region's most important titles, bypassing families that considered their position an acquired right. The dissatisfaction was not limited to the succession issue. Eldrin combined wealth, commercial influence, military strength, and now territorial authority over one of Althoria's main centers. His position threatened interests that had remained protected by the existing social structure for generations.

Eldrin did not ignore the political nature of the decision. He knew that Armand had granted him the title not merely as a reward, but because he wanted to consolidate an ally capable of advancing his interests in a strategic region. The appointment increased his autonomy, but also made him dependent on a relationship whose balance could change according to the duke's objectives. The protection he had received came at a price: his rise was now directly associated with Armand's political agenda.

At the same time, the war had changed the duke's own ambitions. Resistance against Ostgard was no longer enough to satisfy him. Armand was beginning to speak openly about reclaiming Althoria's lost territories and restoring the old imperial unity. To him, the fragmentation of Velmoria represented not merely a historical tragedy, but an opportunity to rebuild a central authority under his own dynasty.

Eldrin realized that his journey was entering a new phase. He had achieved a position few men could reach, but its stability would depend on conflicts far greater than those that had marked his rise. His loyalty to Armand was still genuine, but it was beginning to be accompanied by a growing concern: how far could he support the duke's ambition without abandoning the principles that had guided the construction of Eldermere?

## Act IV: Unification and Reconstruction of the Empire

### Chapter 10: The Struggle for the Imperial Crown

The war between Althoria and Ostgard intensified once again, but the conflict had taken on a different meaning. The dispute was no longer limited to defending borders or reclaiming territories. Armand intended to use the military superiority he had achieved to advance against the other kingdoms and rebuild imperial unity under his command. The crown of Velmoria, once an inheritance contested by several houses, had become the central objective of his political agenda.

Eldrin now occupied a decisive position in this project. His administrative experience, economic influence, and prestige earned in Fernholm made him one of the duke's principal allies. His ability to mobilize resources, establish alliances, and organize military campaigns allowed Armand to wage a war on a scale far greater than anything Althoria had previously faced. Eldrin, who had begun his journey seeking autonomy through commerce, had become part of the structure that could restore the empire.

The prospect of reunification, however, did not eliminate the contradictions that had caused Velmoria's fragmentation. The dissident kingdoms were not merely rebellious territories waiting for a sovereign. Over the decades, they had developed their own institutions, established alliances, and created economic and political interests that did not necessarily align with Armand's objectives. Subduing them would require more than military victories. It would be necessary to decide how the conquered territories would be governed, which privileges would be preserved, and how the old elites would be incorporated into the new order.

This question divided the aristocracy. Some nobles saw Armand as an opportunity to recover lost greatness and expand their own domains. Others feared that imperial restoration would concentrate excessive power in the duke's hands and reduce the autonomy they had gained during the fragmentation. Alliances were therefore unstable. Many supported the campaign as long as they believed it would bring them advantages, but showed no commitment to the authority Armand intended to establish after victory.

Eldrin understood that conquering the empire could resolve the territorial problem without necessarily producing political stability. Military force could overthrow rulers, but it could not eliminate the interests sustaining the resistance. Rebuilding a central authority would require concessions, institutions, and some degree of trust among groups that had spent decades fighting one another.

This concern was beginning to distance him from Armand's vision. The duke was increasingly focused on conquering the crown, while Eldrin was thinking about the conditions necessary for the empire to survive afterward. There was not yet a break between them, but their priorities were beginning to diverge. The man who had helped Armand reach the throne was starting to question what would be done once they finally conquered it.

### Chapter 11: The Duke's Right-Hand Man

The unification campaign advanced with the fall of Ostgard and the gradual submission of other territories. The victories expanded Armand's dominion and transformed Eldrin into one of the most influential commanders on the continent. His participation in the campaigns was decisive not only because of his military ability, but also because of his skill in coordinating supplies, negotiating surrenders, and establishing agreements with local authorities. War required a supporting structure that extended beyond the battlefields, and Eldrin had demonstrated that he understood this dimension better than many traditional generals.

Success, however, deepened tensions within the victorious coalition itself. The nobles who had supported Armand to recover their former positions began to realize that Eldrin occupied an increasingly prominent place in the administration of power. His origins continued to be used as an argument to question his legitimacy, but the real concern lay in his influence. A man who controlled important territories, maintained relationships with major commercial centers, and commanded loyal troops represented a potential threat to the privileges of traditional families.

Conspiracies began to multiply. Hostility was rarely expressed directly. Invitations, promises of cooperation, and public displays of respect concealed disputes over positions, contracts, and influence over the duke's decisions. Eldrin realized that the same men who praised him in Armand's presence were working behind the scenes to limit his authority. War had taught him to identify enemies on the battlefield, but the court demanded constant attention to intentions that could rarely be proven.

Armand's transformation also became increasingly evident. The duke who had recognized Eldrin's abilities and trusted him during Althoria's defense was being consumed by his own ambition. As he drew closer to the imperial crown, he began treating any disagreement as a threat to the unity of his project. The trust between them was replaced by a more calculated relationship, in which every decision seemed to contain an implicit struggle for influence.

Elyana occupied a delicate position in this scenario. Her relationship with Eldrin had deepened since the rescue, and the feelings between them could no longer be reduced to a circumstantial consequence of that episode. Even so, her connection to House Valmont made any rapprochement politically significant. A marriage could strengthen Eldrin's position, definitively bringing him closer to the family controlling the imperial campaign. But it could also place him even further under Armand's influence and intensify the resistance of those who already considered him an outsider.

For Eldrin, Elyana represented the possibility of building something not entirely subordinate to the struggle for power. However, the war had turned almost every relationship into a political instrument. Even what he wanted to preserve as a personal choice was subject to family expectations and the consequences of his alliances.

As the conquests continued, Eldrin drew closer to an inevitable decision. He would need to determine whether he would remain the main instrument of Armand's ambition or begin building an independent position. His power had grown enough to make that choice possible, but the price of any break would be high. The empire he was helping to rebuild could become both his greatest achievement and his greatest threat.

### Chapter 12: The Conquest of the Old Capital

The unification campaign reached its decisive moment when Armand's forces advanced on the old imperial capital. The city had survived the fragmentation of Velmoria and witnessed the rise of different houses that, for decades, had claimed the legitimacy of the old dynasty. Its conquest carried a significance that went beyond any territorial advantage. Recovering the capital meant taking control of the empire's foremost political symbol and presenting Armand's campaign as the restoration of a legitimate order.

The resistance was fierce. The walls had been reinforced over the years, and the defenders understood that the city's fall would mean the end of their claims to the crown. Eldrin coordinated a decisive part of the offensive, using the experience accumulated in previous campaigns to overcome the defenses and maintain the troops' advance. The battle consumed men and resources, but ended with the capture of the capital and the replacement of the former rulers' banners with Armand's coat of arms.

With the city under his control, the duke was proclaimed Emperor of Velmoria under the name Armand I. The fragmentation that had begun with the death of Eldric IV seemed finally overcome. The dissident kingdoms had been subdued, and the old capital once again housed an imperial authority recognized through the force of arms and the political victory of a single dynasty.

Eldrin was rewarded with the title of Duke of Althoria, a position that consolidated his rise and placed him among the most powerful men in the new empire. The former orphan from Ravencourt had crossed every social barrier that seemed to determine his fate. He possessed lands, titles, wealth, and military authority. His name was associated with the restoration of Velmoria, and his journey had become an example of extraordinary social ascent.

But the conquest of the capital did not bring the recognition he might have expected. At the imperial court, his origins continued to be treated as an indelible mark. The nobles who had lost influence during the war were unwilling to accept that a man without aristocratic ancestry occupied such a high position. Public deference concealed resentment, and praise was often accompanied by comments intended to remind him that Eldrin would never truly be one of them.

The situation became even more complex because the new empire needed to reorganize the conquered territories. The old rivalries would not simply disappear because a single ruler had assumed the crown. Each kingdom had its own interests, and the nobles who had supported Armand expected rewards proportional to their participation in the campaign. Eldrin understood that stability would depend on limiting internal disputes and preventing the aristocracy from turning the court into an extension of the wars that had just ended.

Looking at the restored empire, he realized that he had helped build a power structure far greater than any he had ever known. Yet Armand's authority continued to rest on the same instruments that had caused the original fragmentation: personal alliances, territorial concessions, and military force. The difference was that now an emperor stood at the center of this structure, and Eldrin occupied a position that made him indispensable to some and dangerous to many.

Victory had ended a war, but it had not resolved the problem that had caused it. The empire was unified on the map; its political unity, however, still had to be built.

## Act V: Intrigue and Betrayal

### Chapter 13: The Shadow of Conspiracy

The consolidation of the new empire proved far more difficult than its conquest. The old aristocracy, which during the war had tolerated Eldrin's rise because of his military usefulness, now had the time and opportunity to organize a reaction. His position as Duke of Althoria and his proximity to the emperor made him an obstacle to the interests of families seeking to regain control of the imperial administration. The conflict ceased to be an open dispute over territories and began unfolding through discreet alliances, the manipulation of information, and attempts to politically isolate those who represented a threat.

Eldrin sought to preserve his position by resorting to the same methods that had guided his rise. He tried to balance the interests of merchants, administrators, and nobles, preventing any group from concentrating enough power to compromise the stability of the government. But the court did not function like a marketplace, where different interests could be reconciled through advantageous agreements. For many aristocrats, the issue was not obtaining better conditions within the new order, but preventing someone like Eldrin from having the authority to participate in shaping it.

Rumors began circulating about his excessive influence, his ambitions, and the supposed existence of a network of allies who answered directly to him. Commercial relationships were presented as evidence of corruption, while administrative decisions were interpreted as attempts to weaken traditional families. Eldrin realized that he was being progressively isolated, but could not precisely identify who was organizing the campaign against him or which members of his own circle had been persuaded to participate.

The problem went deeper than the aristocracy's simple hostility. The trust underpinning his relationship with Armand was also beginning to disappear. The emperor needed to preserve his authority over the nobles who had supported his coronation and could not allow Eldrin's influence to be interpreted as a second power structure within the government. The man who had been indispensable to conquering the empire was beginning to become an inconvenience to its administration.

For the first time, Eldrin realized that the military strength and economic ability that had sustained his rise were not enough to protect him. In a court where decisions were made before they were announced and accusations could acquire legitimacy through repetition, power also depended on controlling narratives and preserving the trust of those at the center of authority. Eldrin had earned the recognition of an emperor, but had failed to turn that recognition into a secure institutional position.

### Chapter 14: The False Accusation

The conspiracy found its opportunity when a formal accusation was brought against Eldrin. According to his accusers, the duke was organizing a rebellion to overthrow Armand I and seize control of the empire. The denunciation was accompanied by forged correspondence, purchased testimonies, and statements from former allies who, under pressure or in exchange for favors, confirmed a carefully constructed story.

The accusation had been prepared to exploit the very characteristics that had made Eldrin powerful. His commercial influence was presented as a network for clandestine financing, his military strength as an organization intended to support a coup, and his relationships with regional authorities as part of a plan to secure political backing. The facts were arranged in such a way as to turn his administrative abilities into evidence of conspiratorial ambition.

Armand ordered his arrest. Eldrin was taken to the dungeons of Black Hollow, where he remained awaiting trial and, subsequently, execution. The emperor's decision represented a definitive break between the two. More than the loss of his position, Eldrin suffered from the realization that Armand had chosen to believe the accusations without offering him a genuine opportunity to defend himself. The man with whom he had shared campaigns, risks, and the reconstruction of the empire had chosen to preserve his authority rather than question the legitimacy of the conspiracy.

In prison, Eldrin realized that he had underestimated the extent of the hostility surrounding him. His trust in Armand and in the agreements underpinning the new government had prevented him from recognizing that, within the imperial structure, his position still depended on the will of a single man. He possessed no institutional guarantees capable of protecting him when the emperor himself decided to treat him as an enemy.

His conviction seemed inevitable, but Eldrin's execution would also pose a problem for Vaylen's economic interests. During the war, he had protected the region's main trade routes, secured contracts, and created conditions that allowed the republic's merchants to expand their influence in Velmoria. His downfall would put these investments at risk and create an opening for an aristocracy that might reorganize the imperial economy according to interests less favorable to trade.

The merchants of Vaylen decided to act. Using the network of contacts and resources they had developed over the years, they organized an operation to extract Eldrin from prison before his execution. Bribes were paid, guards were removed, and informants provided the information needed to open an escape route. The operation was not an act of personal solidarity, but a strategic decision: Eldrin still represented an important instrument for influencing the balance of power within the empire.

The escape succeeded. Eldrin managed to leave Black Hollow and cross the borders into the Republic of Vaylen, where he found protection among those who still considered his survival politically advantageous. But the man arriving in exile was very different from the one who had conquered Fernholm. His titles had been confiscated, his properties were under the control of other lords, and his honor had been publicly tarnished. The empire he had helped build now treated him as a traitor.

His downfall forced him to confront a contradiction he had previously managed to avoid. Eldrin had always believed that his ability to build power would guarantee his independence. Yet his position had been destroyed because it was embedded in a structure in which fundamental decisions remained in the hands of people who could change sides whenever their interests demanded it. Once again, he found himself without territory, without protection, and without any guarantee of a future. The difference was that now he understood the system that had brought him down.

### Chapter 15: Life in Exile

The first months in Vaylen were marked by his withdrawal from everything that had guided Eldrin's life. With no lands to administer, troops to command, or political decisions to influence, he tried to abandon the ambition that had driven him since Ravencourt. The republic offered enough protection for him to remain beyond the reach of imperial agents, but it did not restore the sense of belonging he had lost. His life was suspended between a past he could not recover and a future he could not yet imagine.

It was during this period that Garrick Thorne sought him out again. The former mentor found Eldrin in a remote tavern, living like someone who had accepted his own defeat. Garrick showed no surprise at seeing him in that condition, but neither did he attempt to console him. He knew enough about the man he had helped shape to understand that the mere loss of wealth and position did not explain his surrender.

The conversation was direct. Garrick asked whether Eldrin intended to let his enemies decide how his journey would end and reminded him that defeat was part of any struggle for power. To him, the problem was not that Eldrin had been defeated, but that he had allowed defeat to define what he could still do. Fortune, titles, and territories were instruments, not the essence of his abilities.

Faced with Eldrin's lack of reaction, Garrick placed a knife on the table and challenged him to end his own life if he truly believed everything was over. The provocation was not an expression of contempt, but an extreme attempt to break the resignation into which Eldrin had sunk. If the will to fight still existed within him, he would need to abandon his nostalgia for what he had lost and start working with what he still possessed.

Outside, a company of mercenaries was waiting. They were experienced fighters, men and women accustomed to serving in various campaigns, with no permanent ties to a crown or aristocratic family. Their commitment depended on payment, terms of service, and trust in their commander. They did not represent a national army nor possess any claim to political legitimacy. But they were available, experienced, and could be gathered around someone capable of offering clear objectives and the means to achieve them.

Garrick believed that Eldrin still possessed what most men attempting to command mercenaries lacked: real administrative experience, knowledge of commercial relationships, and the ability to understand the interests of those who followed him. He did not need to immediately rebuild the army he had lost. He needed to establish a new power base, win allies, and find an opportunity to return.

The conversation did not erase the defeat or immediately restore his confidence. But it made Eldrin realize that exile did not have to be a permanent condition. For the first time, his motivation was no longer tied to Armand's approval or the recognition of the aristocracy. He no longer intended to prove that he deserved a place among the nobles, nor to restore his relationship with those who had abandoned him. If he returned, it would be to regain control over his own life and directly confront those responsible for his downfall.

From that moment on, his struggle ceased to be the continuation of an imperial project that belonged to Armand. It became the construction of an independent power, sustained by men who owed no loyalty to a dynasty and by alliances that would have to be won anew.

## Act VI: The Path to the Throne

### Chapter 16: The Exile's Return

Eldrin's return to Velmoria was prepared far from the centers of imperial power. With the support of Garrick, the mercenaries, and the commercial contacts he still maintained in different regions, he began gathering resources, establishing alliances, and identifying the weaknesses in Armand's administration. The loss of his titles had eliminated his formal position, but it had not erased the relationships he had built over the years. Some merchants still depended on the routes he had protected, former soldiers remained loyal to his leadership, and certain regional authorities were dissatisfied with the way the empire was being governed.

Eldrin exploited these grievances to organize his return. Rather than attempting to confront the central power immediately, he focused on recovering territories where he still held influence and where the imperial administration faced resistance. The first campaign served to demonstrate that his strength was not merely a memory of the past. His mercenaries managed to defeat the local garrisons, and news of his return began circulating through the cities that had once experienced his administration.

The speed of the initial victories surprised his adversaries. Many believed that exile had definitively ended his ability to mobilize forces. Others thought that the mercenaries, having no commitment to a lasting political cause, would abandon Eldrin at the first sign of difficulty. But his experience allowed him to organize a campaign in which military objectives were tied to securing resources, protecting routes, and incorporating new allies.

The population of the reconquered regions reacted in different ways. Some saw Eldrin as the former ruler who had brought prosperity and security; others feared that his return would merely prolong the war. Even so, his reputation as an administrator and commander gave him an advantage that other contenders for power did not possess. Eldrin did not need to convince everyone that he would be a legitimate ruler. He needed to demonstrate that his presence offered better conditions than the continuation of an administration considered distant and hostile.

As he recovered his positions, however, he realized that territorial reconquest would not resolve the conflict. The center of the conspiracy remained in the capital, and imperial authority was still exercised by Armand. To confront him, Eldrin would need to understand not only how he had been overthrown, but also why the aristocracy had considered him such a great threat that it had organized his destruction.

It was during the recovery of Fernholm that he began gathering documents and testimonies related to the conspiracy. His initial objective was to find evidence that the accusations leading to his imprisonment were false. But the investigation would eventually reveal something far older and more decisive than the dispute with Armand itself.

### Chapter 17: The Battle of Fernholm

The recovery of Fernholm represented a turning point in Eldrin's campaign. Besides its strategic importance, the region held archives, administrative records, and properties connected to his journey. Upon regaining control of the city, Eldrin began investigating the events preceding his imprisonment, seeking to identify those responsible for fabricating the evidence and the reasons his rise had provoked such a violent reaction.

Among forgotten documents, old correspondence, and testimonies from people who had remained silent out of fear of retaliation, information emerged that could completely change the meaning of his story. Some records mentioned a family connected to the ancient imperial bloodline of Velmoria, supposedly wiped out during a dispute between noble houses. Among the names associated with that family were references that might have some connection to Eldrin's origins, although none established a direct or indisputable link.

According to the accounts discovered, his father might have descended from one of the houses connected to the imperial family and held a position that, although distant from the main line of succession, would become important if disputes over the crown intensified again. His family had supposedly been massacred by a faction of nobles who feared the emergence of a new dynastic claim. The operation had allegedly been organized to eliminate any descendant who might one day claim rights to the throne. However, the documents were fragmented, some contradicted others, and there were no reliable records confirming the involvement of the nobles mentioned.

One version claimed that Eldrin had survived because, as an infant, he had been hidden by a servant who tried to protect him during the massacre. The child had supposedly been removed from the estate and handed over to a woman who found him without knowing his true origins. Later, according to another account, he ended up in the hands of a distant relative who was supposed to protect him but preferred to appropriate the fortune intended for the possible heir and rid himself of the responsibility. Eldrin was allegedly abandoned at an orphanage in Ravencourt, where he grew up without any knowledge of his supposed lineage. The story, however, contained significant gaps. There were no identifiable witnesses who could confirm the child's survival, nor any documents proving that the boy abandoned in Ravencourt was the same one mentioned in the records of the old family.

The discovery could explain events that had previously seemed unrelated. The hostility of certain aristocratic groups, the speed with which the conspiracy against him had been organized, and the interest in destroying him before he could consolidate his political position were no longer merely manifestations of resentment against an ascending commoner. If the accounts were true, his origins represented a concrete threat to the legitimacy of the new dynasty.

There were also indications that the conspirators themselves might have found references to this possible ancestry while seeking to gather evidence capable of incriminating him. Upon identifying the possibility of a connection to the old imperial family, they might have realized that Eldrin could claim a position capable of threatening both the interests of the old noble houses and Armand's authority. The accusation of treason might not have served merely to contain his influence, but also to remove him before he could discover or use that information. There was, however, no proof that the conspirators knew his origins or that the supposed lineage had anything to do with his imprisonment.

Eldrin began to understand that his journey might be connected to a much older struggle than he had imagined. The same aristocracy that, according to the accounts, had destroyed his family might also have tried to prevent him from regaining any political influence. His rise as a merchant and administrator had not merely been a break from the social condition into which he had been born. Perhaps he had unknowingly rebuilt a position of power that his ancestors had lost by force.

The revelation also changed his relationship with the throne. Throughout his life, Eldrin had built his legitimacy through concrete achievements, refusing to depend on an origin he did not know. Now, faced with the possibility of possessing a dynastic right, he realized that this information could be used against Armand. He no longer needed to be merely the man wronged by a false accusation. He could present himself as a descendant of one of the old imperial bloodlines and claim legitimacy predating the very dynasty that ruled Velmoria.

But this possibility brought a contradiction he could not ignore. Eldrin had never planned to rule Velmoria by right of blood. His journey had been built precisely on the idea that the ability to govern should be demonstrated through actions, not determined by family origins. Even so, the possibility of imperial ancestry could offer him the legitimacy necessary to confront an emperor who had conquered the throne through war.

Despite his efforts, Eldrin found no evidence capable of turning those accounts into certainty. The names did not match across all the records, the dates raised doubts, and no witness could confirm that he was the infant supposedly removed from the estate during the massacre. It could be true. It could be a series of coincidences interpreted by men interested in finding a connection to the old dynasty. The possibility that someone had deliberately assembled those fragments to lead him toward a convenient conclusion could not be ruled out either.

Even so, Eldrin chose to believe.

Not because he had discovered the truth, but because, for the first time, that story gave him a reason to do what he had previously hesitated to consider. For years, he had accepted that his authority should be earned through merit and recognized by those he governed. But Armand had demonstrated that achievements, loyalty, and service were not enough to protect him when the interests of power demanded his sacrifice. If there was a possibility that his own blood connected him to the old crown, Eldrin had no intention of ignoring it simply because he could not prove it.

Believing in that origin also meant accepting that his struggle could represent something greater than the recovery of his lands or the restoration of his honor. For the first time, he allowed himself to consider that he might have the right to contest the throne. And even if the story could never be proven, he no longer saw any reason to keep rejecting a possibility that could change Velmoria's destiny.

The conflict was about to assume its definitive form. Eldrin would no longer fight merely to recover lands or restore his honor. He would fight against the dynasty he had helped build, convinced that perhaps he had been born to take his emperor's place.

### Chapter 18: The Crown of Blood

The final confrontation between Eldrin and Armand was preceded by a campaign that brought together the emperor's remaining opponents. The discovery of his imperial origins offered Eldrin a political justification for the war, but did not eliminate the difficulties of his position. Armand still controlled the capital, a significant portion of the army, and the empire's administrative machinery. Moreover, many of the nobles who had participated in the conspiracy remained close to power and were willing to defend the new dynasty to the end.

Eldrin still believed he could avoid total destruction. His objective was to defeat the imperial forces, reach Armand, and capture him alive. He intended to present the evidence of the conspiracy, demonstrate that the accusations against him had been fabricated, and force the emperor to acknowledge the truth. He did not merely wish to replace Armand on the throne. He wanted his innocence to be established and the downfall of his former ally not to be interpreted as a simple dispute between two ambitious men.

But Armand was no longer the ruler Eldrin had known. His long tenure in power and the succession of conspiracies had transformed his perception of events. To him, Eldrin represented a threat to imperial unity and an attempt at usurpation. The revelation of his dynastic origins only reinforced his conviction that his former ally intended to take what Armand had conquered.

The encounter between the two took place amid Eldrin's forces advancing against the imperial positions. The battle became a confrontation between men who had shared the same history but attributed entirely different meanings to what they had built. Each saw the other as the source of the threat that could destroy the empire. Eldrin fought to reach Armand and put an end to the cycle of accusations and betrayals that had brought them both to this point. The emperor fought to preserve an authority he could no longer distinguish from his own survival.

When Eldrin finally reached the place where Armand was, he found him dead. The emperor had been assassinated during the confrontation, but Eldrin had not struck the fatal blow. The identity of the person responsible could not be determined. The chaos of battle, the dispersal of the troops, and the absence of reliable witnesses prevented anyone from establishing what had actually happened.

Armand's death destroyed Eldrin's plan. With the emperor no longer alive, there would be no way to present the evidence and obtain recognition of his innocence from the man who had ordered his arrest. The military victory could be interpreted as a seizure of power, and his rise to the throne would be seen by Armand's former allies as confirmation that the accusations of conspiracy had always been true.

The uncertainty surrounding the emperor's killer became even more troubling. The culprit could have been among the conspirators who had organized Eldrin's downfall, among the men accompanying Eldrin, or among those who simply wanted to eliminate Armand to facilitate a new reorganization of power. Regardless of the assassin's identity, someone had benefited from the emperor's death and prevented the truth about the conspiracy from being established.

Eldrin had won the war, but had failed to control its outcome. The empire was once again facing a succession crisis, and his legitimacy now depended on a combination of military strength, dynastic rights, and political recognition. His rise to the throne seemed inevitable, but victory did not offer him the vindication he had sought.

It was after the battle that he found Elyana. The reunion, which could have represented the recovery of one of the few relationships that had survived the transformations of his life, revealed the distance the war had created between them. Elyana no longer saw in him the man who had risked his life to rescue her or the merchant who had rebuilt Eldermere. Before her stood someone who had led armies across the continent, participated in the fall of a dynasty, and reached the throne through a war whose outcome remained shrouded in suspicion.

She questioned whether Eldrin could still claim that he had never desired the crown. For years, he had insisted that his decisions were motivated by the need to survive, protect his domains, and correct injustices. Now, however, he stood before the supreme power of Velmoria, and everything that had happened seemed to have led him precisely to that place.

Elyana did not wait for an answer. The relationship between them could not withstand the weight of Eldrin's choices, and she decided to leave, distancing herself from a man whose journey had become inseparable from the violence and ambition he had once condemned.

Alone before the throne, Eldrin realized that he had conquered everything that had once seemed impossible. He was the heir to an imperial bloodline, had the support of a victorious army, and now faced the possibility of ruling the continent. But he could no longer clearly distinguish what had resulted from his own decisions and what had been the consequence of forces that had led him there. The man who had always wanted to control his own destiny had reached the point at which he least understood the meaning of his choices.

## Epilogue: The Emperor's Bitter Legacy

### Chapter 19: Glory and Ashes

The unification of Velmoria did not bring the peace Eldrin had hoped for. The war had formally ended, but its consequences remained in the destroyed cities, shattered families, and political structures that needed to be reorganized. The new emperor had inherited a territory unified by force, but deeply divided by decades of rivalry. His task was to transform a military victory into a political order capable of surviving without depending permanently on war.

The difficulty lay in the very nature of the power he had conquered. The nobles who had once opposed Armand now recognized Eldrin's authority, but their submission did not necessarily represent loyalty. Many had lost relatives, properties, and influence during the unification campaigns, while others accepted the new government only because they lacked sufficient strength to challenge it. The court became a space of constant negotiations, in which every appointment, concession, or punishment was interpreted as an opportunity to recover positions or weaken rivals.

The people were also divided. In some regions, Eldrin was remembered as the administrator who had brought prosperity and security. In others, his image was associated with military campaigns, resource requisitions, and the violence required to rebuild the empire. The same journey that had granted him legitimacy among the inhabitants of Eldermere could be interpreted as a threat by those who had suffered the consequences of his conquests.

The merchants of Vaylen, who had been instrumental in his survival during exile, began reconsidering their relationship with the empire. For years, they had benefited from the protection Eldrin provided and the expansion of trade routes. But instability at court and the intensification of political disputes were making investments increasingly risky. Without guarantees that their interests would be preserved, they began transferring resources and vessels to other regions. Eldrin understood the decision. The merchants had never been allies out of personal devotion, but because of a convergence of interests that was now under threat.

The need to consolidate his legitimacy also led him to make a decision that went against his feelings. With Elyana gone, Eldrin needed to establish a dynastic alliance capable of bringing him closer to the aristocratic families that still resisted his authority. He married Demeter, a young woman from one of the empire's leading noble houses, whose lineage could lend greater stability to the succession. The marriage was negotiated as a political agreement, and neither expected the relationship to be sustained by a feeling that went beyond the obligations imposed by their positions.

The birth of Elara brought an unexpected change to the emperor's life. For the first time since Elyana's death, Eldrin found a reason to imagine a future not entirely dictated by power struggles. His daughter represented the continuation of a family he had lost before even knowing his true origins. Perhaps it was possible to build a bloodline that did not need to be defined by violence or condemned by the rivalries that had destroyed the old dynasty.

This hope did not last long. During a ceremony intended to celebrate peace among the empire's different houses, an attack struck the imperial family. Demeter and Elara were killed, and Eldrin arrived too late to prevent the tragedy. Their deaths destroyed the last bond that still connected him to the possibility of a life beyond the struggle for power.

Grief turned into a desperate need to find those responsible. Eldrin ordered investigations and initiated a purge against those who might have been involved in the attack. Nobles suspected of participating in the conspiracy were arrested, properties were confiscated, and entire families suffered the consequences of the accusations. The repression quickly exceeded the limits of a legitimate investigation. The emperor began treating any sign of opposition as evidence of involvement in the murder of his wife and daughter.

But violence produced no answers. For every conspiracy dismantled, new accusations emerged; for every group eliminated, other individuals took the place of its members. The court became even more unstable, and fear began to replace any possibility of trust. Eldrin had tried to eliminate threats to his government through force, but was discovering that repression merely reorganized the interests fueling the conspiracies.

Gradually, he began questioning the very nature of the problem. For years, he had believed that Velmoria's instability was the consequence of incapable rulers, ambitious nobles, and poorly constructed alliances. Now, after eliminating his adversaries and concentrating enough authority to impose his decisions, he realized that conflicts continued to emerge. Perhaps it was not merely men who corrupted the empire. Perhaps the very structure sustaining its existence continuously produced the conditions for new disputes.

### Chapter 20: The Emperor's Twilight

The loss of Demeter and Elara deepened Eldrin's isolation. His ability to govern remained intact, but his relationship with those around him had been progressively replaced by suspicion. Advisors were evaluated according to their immediate usefulness, former allies began to be treated as potential adversaries, and any disagreement was interpreted as an attempt to weaken his authority.

Garrick Thorne still tried to advise him. The man who had recognized his potential on the streets of Ravencourt, taught him to understand the mechanisms of power, and helped him rebuild his life after exile remained one of the few people capable of confronting him without resorting to flattery. Garrick tried to remind him that the empire could not be stabilized through an endless succession of punishments and that permanent distrust would eventually destroy the relationships necessary to govern.

Eldrin, however, could no longer hear these warnings. His experience had transformed prudence into suspicion and autonomy into isolation. He did not believe Garrick was deceiving him, but neither could he accept that anyone might understand better than he did the extent of the dangers surrounding the throne.

The death of his former mentor deepened this contradiction. Garrick was found strangled in his chambers, with no one identified as responsible. Eldrin had not ordered the murder, but he realized that his inability to protect even the man who had accompanied him throughout his entire journey revealed the fragility of his authority. The conspirators no longer necessarily needed to confront him directly. They could operate in the shadows, eliminate his allies, and exploit the distrust the emperor himself had spread throughout the court.

Garrick's death represented the loss of the last connection to the man Eldrin had been before reaching the throne. The mentor had known the boy from Ravencourt, witnessed his transformation into a merchant, and followed his political rise. His presence allowed Eldrin to remember a journey that had not begun with the crown and that, for a long time, had been guided by objectives different from the mere conquest of power.

Without him, the emperor began to see his own story as a succession of losses. His friends had died or drifted away, his former comrades-in-arms were scattered, and his commercial allies were seeking distance from an increasingly unpredictable court. The curse of the Velmorians, which during his childhood had seemed merely a superstition created to explain the tragedies of the old dynasty, now took on a personal meaning. The imperial bloodline seemed to condemn everyone who came near it to loneliness and destruction.

The empire, however, was pacified. Its borders remained under control, the great armies had been demobilized, and no kingdom possessed enough strength to openly challenge the central authority. But Eldrin was beginning to realize that the absence of a declared war did not mean stability. The government continued consuming resources to protect itself from internal threats, and the administration depended increasingly on surveillance and coercion. The unity that had been achieved through force needed to be preserved through that same force, because the political structures had not developed the conditions necessary to sustain lasting coexistence among the former kingdoms.

The conclusion emerged gradually. Velmoria was not merely an empire that had been poorly governed. It was a structure whose survival depended on the concentration of power, the subjugation of territories, and the unequal distribution of privileges. The crown needed to keep the nobles invested in its continuity, while the nobles constantly needed to compete for proximity to the sovereign. One group's stability depended on the restriction of the others, and any attempt to reorganize this balance threatened to trigger a new struggle.

Eldrin had spent his life trying to overcome the consequences of this system. First, he built wealth so he would not have to depend on the powerful. Then, he acquired a domain to demonstrate that he could govern better than the old lords. He became a commander to defend what he had built, helped restore the empire, and finally conquered the throne itself to rectify the injustices he had suffered. At every stage, he believed he was approaching a definitive form of autonomy. But upon attaining supreme power, he discovered that he remained trapped in the same structure that had determined the fate of his ancestors.

The final decision was born from this realization. Eldrin summoned the advisors who still remained at his side and announced that the Empire of Velmoria would be dissolved. The old kingdoms would regain their independence and be free to organize their own institutions, establish alliances, and choose their rulers. The imperial crown would cease to exist, and none of his descendants would be authorized to claim it. There would be no new dynasty nor any attempt to preserve the title in another form.

The proposal provoked disbelief and resistance. To many, the emperor had gone mad or intended to use the dissolution as part of one final political maneuver. Others understood that the decision represented a break with the entire order they had sworn to defend. They tried to persuade him to reconsider, arguing that the empire had finally achieved a stability that could not be squandered. Eldrin, however, no longer believed that the continuation of the imperial structure could produce a result different from the one that had marked its history.

Before the advisors could organize any reaction, Eldrin ordered the palace to be emptied. Documents, symbols, and objects associated with imperial authority were left behind. In the dead of night, the palace's chambers were set ablaze, and the flames began consuming the halls that had housed generations of rulers. Tapestries, archives, and the dynasty's emblems disappeared along with the structure they represented.

Eldrin made his way to the main hall and sat on the throne one last time. Before him stood a goblet of wine laced with poison. He did not intend to leave a successor, claim historical recognition, or turn his death into a new symbol of legitimacy. He simply wanted to end a journey that had begun long before he knew his own name and that, ever since, seemed to have led him inevitably to that moment.

As the fire spread through the palace, he raised the goblet and drank. The last emperor of Velmoria died alongside the institution that had consumed his life. The empire disappeared, but the lands, peoples, and rivalries that had constituted it remained.

Eldrin left behind neither a dynasty, nor a restoration project, nor an order capable of ensuring that history would not repeat itself. He left only the decision to interrupt a cycle whose continuation had required the destruction of everything he had once wished to protect.
